#### Model-Independent Assessments of Tidal Alignment and Density-Weighting Dependence in Galaxy Intrinsic Alignments
 - **Authors:** Paul Rogozenski , Sukhdeep Singh , Jonathan Blazek , Rachel Mandelbaum , Hung-Jin Huang , Ananth Tenneti
 - **Subjects:** Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.42151
 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.42151
 - **Abstract**
 Intrinsic alignments (IA) of galaxies are an important source of systematic uncertainty in weak gravitational lensing measurements and also serve as a test for models of galaxy formation and evolution. These galaxy alignments are usually modeled assuming that the IA are sourced by the tidal fields around the galaxies. However, galaxy properties, like their proximity to other galaxies and their mass, provide alignment contributions beyond the tidal field. In this work, we perform novel model-agnostic tests to determine the impact of density-weighting on the IA of galaxies using data from the IllustrisTNG cosmological hydrodynamical simulation and SDSS-III (BOSS). We show that the primary effect of density-weighting is an increase of the alignment amplitude at large scales, whereas the small-scale impact is sample-dependent. We also assess the tidal alignment paradigm for IllustrisTNG galaxies using two-point correlation functions of galaxy shapes, tidal fields, and the sampled background tidal field of the simulation box. We find, given the precision of our measurements, that central galaxies align linearly to the underlying (non-linear) tidal field well into the 1-halo regime.
#### OASIS III: Bursty star formation drives observed galaxy sizes at $z\geq4$
 - **Authors:** William M. Baker , Tobias J. Looser , Francesco D'Eugenio , Andrew J. Bunker , Stefano Carniani , Stéphane Charlot , Jacopo Chevallard , Mirko Curti , A. Lola Danhaive , Qiao Duan , Kevin Hainline , Jakob M. Helton , Benjamin D. Johnson , Pierluigi Rinaldi , Brant Robertson , Fengwu Sun , Yang Sun , Sandro Tacchella , James A. A. Trussler , Zihao Wu
 - **Subjects:** Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA) ; Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41491
 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.41491
 - **Abstract**
 We employ JWST/NIRSpec MSA PRISM spectroscopy together with NIRCam imaging to investigate the impact of star-formation variability on galaxy sizes for a representative sample of 51 galaxies at $z\geq4$. The OASIS survey enables us to probe low-star-formation-rate (SFR) lulling and mini-quenched galaxies down to $\log_{10}(M_\star/M_\odot)=8.3$ and $\log_{10}(\mathrm{SFR}/M_\odot\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1})<-1.6$, i.e. more than one order of magnitude below the star-forming main sequence (MS). This enables us to probe objects that are often missed by shallower spectroscopic surveys, in addition to the MS and starburst systems, yielding an SFR- and mass-representative sample at high redshift. We derive galaxy half-light sizes using ForcePho, enabling us to resolve the highly compact nature of these sources, and we analyse their star-formation histories and interstellar medium (ISM) properties with the full-spectral-fitting code ppxf. We find a clear anti-correlation between burstiness, as traced by the ratio of the SFR from H$α$ compared to that from non-parametric spectral fitting averaged over 100 Myr, and galaxy UV/optical half-light radii as measured with ForcePho from a combination of well-sampled NIRCam photometry. Using partial correlation coefficients we show that the anti-correlation between burstiness (as traced by the SFR ratio) and size cannot simply be explained by secondary dependencies on stellar mass or redshift. We interpret this relation as the effect of central starbursts in SFR-rising (bursting) systems, where intense central star formation reduces measured galaxy half-light sizes. This burstiness then regulates galaxy sizes at high redshift. Should the same mechanism be present at $z>10$, this could explain the size bimodality seen in the UV luminous galaxies as simply stemming from a more fundamental relationship between burstiness and size.
#### OASIS II: The Ionized Gas Traces Feedback-Driven Declines into the Quiet Side of Bursty Star Formation at z > 4
 - **Authors:** Tobias J. Looser , Francesco D'Eugenio , William M. Baker , Santiago Arribas , Andrew J. Bunker , Stefano Carniani , Stéphane Charlot , Jacopo Chevallard , Mirko Curti , Qiao Duan , Kevin Hainline , Jakob M. Helton , Yuki Isobe , Benjamin D. Johnson , Pierluigi Rinaldi , Brant Robertson , Fengwu Sun , Sandro Tacchella , James A. A. Trussler , Hannah Übler , Joris Witstok , Zihao Wu
 - **Subjects:** Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41490
 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.41490
 - **Abstract**
 Models of bursty star formation in low-mass (10^8-10^9.5 Msun) galaxies at z>4 differ most in the suppressed phases: what drives the decline, how fast star formation falls, and how long it stays low. OASIS Paper I showed that such quiet galaxies are common and old; here we measure how they got there and how their ionized gas responds. We compare Balmer-line star-formation rates (SFRs) with 100-Myr averages from the stellar continuum, in NIRSpec/PRISM spectra of 54 OASIS galaxies at z>4, including three mini-quenched galaxies below the quenching threshold and five lulling galaxies at least 0.6 dex below the star-forming main sequence (SFMS) but still star-forming. Galaxies above the SFMS rise and fade equally often, whereas those below it are mostly fading: the lulling galaxies formed little more than a tenth of their mass in the last 100 Myr, and their decline began tens to ~100 Myr before observation. Suppressed phases thus last of order 100 Myr, as long as or longer than in most simulations, whereas recovery is much faster: few galaxies below the SFMS are rising. The ionized gas tracks these changes in star formation. [OIII]/[OII] falls from galaxies above the SFMS to the lulling ones, even at fixed mass and after dust correction, and follows the recent change in SFR (SFR_10/SFR_100) more closely than the position relative to the SFMS: two rising galaxies below the SFMS are highly ionized. This is expected if fewer and softer ionizing photons than in strongly star-forming galaxies meet gas depleted by earlier star formation. Lulling galaxies show ISM conditions distinct from those of strongly star-forming galaxies. The declines are faster than passive gas exhaustion predicts, favoring feedback that expels the gas or keeps it from forming stars. Quiet phases are thus long, feedback-driven episodes, and the ionized gas traces where a galaxy is in its burst cycle.
#### OASIS I: Spectroscopy of Mini-Quenched and Lulling Galaxies Constrains the Quiet Side of Bursty Star Formation at z > 4
 - **Authors:** Tobias J. Looser , Francesco D'Eugenio , William M. Baker , Santiago Arribas , Andrew J. Bunker , Stefano Carniani , Stéphane Charlot , Jacopo Chevallard , Mirko Curti , Qiao Duan , Kevin Hainline , Jakob M. Helton , Yuki Isobe , Benjamin D. Johnson , Pierluigi Rinaldi , Brant Robertson , Fengwu Sun , Sandro Tacchella , James A. A. Trussler , Hannah Übler , Joris Witstok , Zihao Wu
 - **Subjects:** Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41489
 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.41489
 - **Abstract**
 JWST has revealed that star formation in early galaxies is bursty, including episodes of violent suppression, so-called mini-quenching. But these were largely serendipitous discoveries in samples of UV-bright, emission-line-dominated galaxies, so that the quiet side of the burst cycle has remained poorly explored. With the JWST Cycle 3 program OASIS (GO 5997), we searched for such galaxies deliberately, using deep 28-hour NIRSpec/PRISM spectroscopy across the full burst cycle at z>4. We identify three mini-quenched galaxies with log(M*/Msun) = 8.3-8.9 and no detectable Balmer emission, at least 1.2-1.7 dex below the star-forming main sequence (SFMS), five lulling galaxies with weak Balmer emission above the quenching threshold, and 17 nearly-main-sequence galaxies just below the SFMS. Finding them required spectroscopy: seven of our ten photometric candidates for suppressed (mini-quenched and lulling) galaxies turned out to be nearly-MS or above the SFMS, and photometric fits overestimate their current star-formation rates and cannot distinguish lulling from mini-quenched ones. The suppressed galaxies are common: five of the eight come from tiers not selected on star-formation state, where they make up 29% of the galaxies at z = 4-5.5 and log(M*/Msun) = 8-9.5, a number density of ~1.6x10^-3 Mpc^-3 dex^-1. Simulations predict them to be an order of magnitude rarer at these masses and to occur mainly at lower masses. The suppressed galaxies are also old: Balmer-break strength and stellar age both increase as galaxies fall further below the SFMS, with light-weighted ages of 5 Myr for galaxies above it, ~30 Myr for the lulling galaxies and above 100 Myr for the mini-quenched ones. They show little stellar reddening (E(B-V) < 0.2), consistent with depleted gas reservoirs. Suppressed phases at z>4 are therefore neither rare nor brief, but a common and lasting part of early galaxy growth.
#### Stellar Bars Reshape the Spatial Distribution of UV-bright Clumps in Nearby Galaxies
 - **Authors:** Zhimin Zhou , Jianfei Liu
 - **Subjects:** Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41355
 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.41355
 - **Abstract**
 Stellar bars redistribute gas and angular momentum in disk galaxies, but whether they mainly change the amount or the spatial organization of recent star formation remains uncertain. We use archival HST/WFC3 F275W imaging to study 70 barred galaxies and 95 unbarred control galaxies matched in stellar mass, optical color, redshift, and F275W background noise. A uniform dendrogram-based procedure identifies 95,486 UV-bright star-forming clumps. We compare their radial and azimuthal distributions, central F275W emission, and luminosity and size distribution functions. Relative to their controls, barred galaxies show a deficit of clumps at intermediate radii broadly corresponding to the bar region, followed by a local recovery near the bar ends. Their clumps are also preferentially concentrated along the bar major axis. These spatial differences are stronger in high-mass galaxies. Normalized bar length is more closely associated with the radial redistribution and central F275W concentration than bar ellipticity, although its relation to the azimuthal distribution is less uniform. When galactocentric radius is normalized by bar length, the recovery occurs near $R/R_{\rm bar}\sim1$, indicating that the bar sets a characteristic scale for the redistribution. Despite these spatial differences, barred and control galaxies have broadly similar clump luminosity and size distribution functions. Our results suggest that stellar bars mainly reorganize where recent star formation occurs, rather than substantially altering the observed luminosities and sizes of individual UV clumps, and that this effect depends on galaxy mass and bar extent.
#### The Size-Mass Relation and Inside-Out Quenching of Massive Quiescent Galaxies at $2<z<3$ with JWST PRIMER and MINERVA
 - **Authors:** Vaidik Prasal , Yogesh Wadadekar , Pralay Biswas
 - **Subjects:** Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40937
 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.40937
 - **Abstract**
 We investigate the morphology, size--mass relations, and spatially resolved star formation histories of 26 massive quiescent galaxies at cosmic noon ($2 < z < 3$) in the Ultra-Deep Survey field using deep JWST NIRCam imaging from the PRIMER and MINERVA surveys. Combining 16 broadband and medium-band filters with empirical point spread function matching, we perform single-Sérsic modeling and bulge--disk decompositions with pysersic across six filters (F115W to F444W), alongside Bayesian size--mass fitting with dynesty. In the rest-frame optical (F200W), the size--mass relation exhibits a steep slope of $α= 0.59 \pm 0.12$ and effective radius of $A = 0.83_{-0.07}^{+0.08}\ \mathrm{kpc}$ at $M_* = 5 \times 10^{10}\ M_\odot$. In the rest-frame near-infrared (F356W), we derive $α= 0.56 \pm 0.13$ and a more compact radius of $A = 0.70_{-0.06}^{+0.07}\ \mathrm{kpc}$. Galaxy sizes shrink by $\approx 22\%$ from F115W to F444W, reflecting negative color gradients and centrally concentrated mass profiles. Two-thirds of the sample are bulge-dominated ($B/T \ge 0.5$), whereas one-third are disk-dominated. Central bulges are ultra-compact ($R_{e,\mathrm{Bulge}} \approx 0.49\ \mathrm{kpc}$) with negligible mass dependence, whereas outer disks extend to $R_{e,\mathrm{Disk}} \approx 1.4\text{--}1.5\ \mathrm{kpc}$. Resolved spectral energy distribution modeling with piXedfit demonstrates positive radial specific star formation rate gradients and inner regions that formed at least $\approx 0.7\ \mathrm{Gyr}$ earlier and quenched faster than their outskirts. These results confirm inside-out quenching, consistent with dissipative gas compaction assembling the dense core, active galactic nucleus feedback halting central star formation, and the compact bulge dynamically stabilizing the surviving disk against fragmentation.
#### From Quantum Field Fluctuations to High-Redshift AGN: Semiclassical Origins of Halo Mass Function Constraints in the JWST Era
 - **Authors:** Elan Yogeswaren , Amir-Homayoon Najmi , Robert K Schaefer
 - **Subjects:** Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO) ; Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40846
 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.40846
 - **Abstract**
 We develop a continuous framework connecting quantum field fluctuations in the early Universe to the abundance and observable properties of galaxies and active galactic nuclei (AGN) at cosmic dawn. Beginning with quantum fields in curved Friedmann Lemaitre Robertson Walker spacetime, we follow inflationary vacuum fluctuations into the primordial curvature spectrum, linear matter power spectrum, mass variance $σ(M,z)$, mass peak height $ν(M,z)$, and the statistics of nonlinear halo formation. The Press-Schechter and Sheth-Tormen formalisms provide an analytic description of halo abundance and its dependence on peak rarity. A complementary high redshift halo mass function calibrated with the GUREFT simulations of Yung et al. (2024) provides a numerical description specifically constructed for the cosmic dawn regime probed by JWST. The comparison between these analytic and simulation calibrated prescriptions provides a direct measure of the sensitivity of predicted halo populations to the observations. We separate this cosmological supply of halos from the astrophysical and observational mappings that produce stars, ultraviolet luminosities, and active galactic nuclei. Controlled numerical experiments show that a percent level perturbation in the primordial fluctuation amplitude produces only a modest change in $σ$ and $ν$, but can strongly amplify the exponentially suppressed high $ν$ tail of the Halo Mass Function. This sensitivity increases toward high halo mass and high redshift, identifying rare cosmic dawn populations as potentially powerful probes of primordial statistics while simultaneously exposing the importance of uncertainties in halo abundance. High redshift AGN population statistics and the Ultraviolet Luminosity Function provide additional constraints on the primordial power spectrum and dynamics of primordial inflation theory.
#### The assembly of dusty galaxies at $z \geq 4$: grain growth and the evolutionary origin of the dust-to-stellar-mass scatter
 - **Authors:** Alessia D'Angelo , Raffaella Schneider , Luca Graziani , Claudia Di Cesare , Rosa Valiante , Gergő Popping , Giulia Rodighiero , Laura Bisigello , Denis Burgarella , Giovanni Gandolfi , Koki Otaki , Tommaso Zana
 - **Subjects:** Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40790
 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.40790
 - **Abstract**
 Understanding dust evolution is essential for interpreting the properties of galaxies across cosmic time. Nearby galaxies have revealed a detailed picture of dust enrichment, but whether the same picture applies in the early Universe remains unclear. Here we explore the origin of interstellar dust in simulated galaxies at $z \sim 5$. Starting from the dust-to-stellar mass relation, we select five simulated star-forming galaxies with $SFR> 1 M_\odot yr^{-1}$ and $ log(M_\star/M_\odot) > 8$. We focus on three low-mass systems with comparable stellar masses ($log(M_\star/M_\odot) \sim 8.7$) but significantly different dust masses ($log(M_{\rm dust}/M_\odot) \sim 5.1-6.2$), to investigate the origin of the scatter in the relation. Our analysis combines outputs from the cosmological hydrodynamical simulation dustyGadget, a semi-analytical model for stellar dust production, and radiative-transfer post-processing with SKIRT. We confirm that stellar dust production alone reproduces only the low-dust branch of the relation. Grain growth is required to recover both its S-shape and the observed scatter, as established for nearby galaxies. The transition to efficient grain growth occurs at lower metallicity than locally, consistent with the denser interstellar medium of high-redshift systems. Differences in dust content reflect different stages of galaxy assembly. Progressively longer star formation histories lead to greater chemical enrichment and denser ISM, promoting increasingly efficient grain growth. We therefore identify the assembly stage as the key regulator of dust growth at high redshift. Mock observations show that JWST multi-band imaging can reveal the structural diversity of galaxies within the scatter, although the reconstruction of clumpy morphologies may remain incomplete. Their dust continuum emission remains below the sensitivity of current ALMA continuum surveys.
#### Cosmic merger rates and population evolution of binary black hole mergers in AGN disks
 - **Authors:** Maria Paola Vaccaro , Alessandro Trinca , Michela Mapelli , Lumen Boco , Raffaella Schneider , Rosa Valiante , Dominika Wylezalek
 - **Subjects:** High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE) ; Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40787
 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.40787
 - **Abstract**
 Active galactic nuclei (AGN) disks are promising sites for hierarchical binary black hole (BBH) mergers, which next-generation gravitational-wave detectors will probe out to high redshift. Predicting their contribution requires linking BBH evolution in AGN disks to the poorly constrained high-redshift evolution of active supermassive black holes (SMBHs). We introduce ARISE (AGN Rates and Intrinsic Stellar-BH Evolution), a framework that embeds semi-analytical models of BBH evolution in AGN disks into a cosmological description of the AGN number density, SMBH mass function, Eddington-ratio distribution, and nuclear metallicity. We consider a conservative AGN-abundance model anchored to observed luminosity functions, as well as Eddington-limited and super-Eddington scenarios based on the CAT cosmological model, compatible with JWST observations of high-redshift AGNs. The merger-rate density is set primarily by the disk viscosity parameter $α$: at $z = 0.2$, we find a rate of $12$--$37$ Gpc$^{-3}$ yr$^{-1}$ for $α= 0.01$ and $0.5$--$6$ Gpc$^{-3}$ yr$^{-1}$ for $α= 0.1$. The redshift evolution depends strongly on how SMBHs grow. The rate density peaks at $z \simeq 1.8$, $3.3$, and $3.9$ ($1.5$, $3.6$, and $3.9$) for $α=0.01$ ($0.1$) in the conservative, Eddington-limited, and super-Eddington models, respectively, and in the super-Eddington model it stays nearly flat out to $z \simeq 10$. For $α= 0.1$, AGN disks account for $\sim 2$--$40\%$ of the LVK-inferred merger rate, consistent with current upper limits from GW--AGN associations. For $α= 0.01$, they match or exceed it. The Einstein Telescope (ET) should detect $\sim 10^{5}$ ($\sim 10^{4}$) AGN-disk mergers per year for $α= 0.01$ ($0.1$). ET will test the AGN channel at redshifts where the AGN population models differ most strongly, providing independent constraints on the growth of early SMBHs.
#### Bridging Scales: Multi-Zone GRMHD-Informed Anisotropic AGN Feedback in Galaxies
 - **Authors:** Kung-Yi Su , Hyerin Cho , Priyamvada Natarajan , Claude-André Faucher-Giguère , Ramesh Narayan , Angelo Ricarte , Antonio J. Porras-Valverde , Cora Prather , Philip F. Hopkins
 - **Subjects:** Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA) ; High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40784
 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.40784
 - **Abstract**
 We present a galaxy-scale black hole (BH) accretion and feedback model incorporating results from multi-zone general relativistic magnetohydrodynamic (GRMHD) simulations spanning the event horizon to the Bondi radius. The model replaces a fixed suppression of the Bondi rate with a nuclear-environment-dependent factor, $κ^{\rm hot}_{\rm cho}$, set by the local ratio of Bondi to gravitational radius, and distributes outflow mass, kinetic energy, and thermal energy according to the angular structure measured in the large-dynamic-range GRMHD calculations. We apply it to idealized isolated simulations of an M87*-like massive elliptical and a Milky Way/Sgr A*-like galaxy, spanning over three orders of magnitude in BH mass without tuning accretion or feedback efficiencies to match observables. In the M87*-like system, the GRMHD-informed angle-dependent feedback model suppresses late-time stellar mass growth more effectively than the isotropic reference model, despite similar long-term BH accretion rates. Varying the uncertain mass loading of the collimated polar component has a modest impact on BH growth and star formation, indicating that feedback anisotropy matters more than its detailed mass loading. We find that $κ^{\rm hot}_{\rm cho}$ varies with nuclear environment and time, yielding BH growth histories markedly different from fixed-suppression models. For Sgr A*, this variable factor yields an accretion rate more consistent with Event Horizon Telescope (EHT) constraints than a fixed, suppressed Bondi model. These results show that nuclear-environment-dependent accretion and feedback angular structure affect BH-galaxy evolution, requiring subgrid models to include more than a universal Bondi suppression factor and a scalar feedback efficiency.
#### When precise inference is wrong: Diagnosing model misspecification with applications to cosmic dawn and reionisation
 - **Authors:** Daniela Breitman
 - **Subjects:** Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO) ; Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40770
 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.40770
 - **Abstract**
 As observations of the cosmic dawn (CD) and epoch of reionisation (EoR) rapidly improve in precision, the approximate forward models used to interpret them might struggle to keep pace. Bayesian inference with a misspecified model, one that cannot represent the processes generating the observations, can yield tightly constrained, yet misleading and biased astrophysics, with different parts of the observations favouring incompatible parameter values. Model misspecification can evade a $χ^2$ goodness-of-fit test, and Bayesian model selection requires a better model, which is not always available. We therefore apply the suspiciousness, a statistic developed to quantify tensions between datasets, to CD/EoR inference as a model criticism tool that tests whether these parts can be explained by the same parameters. We first apply it to HERA 21-cm power spectrum (PS) upper limits, which are too weak to reveal misspecification, as expected, and to HST and JWST UV luminosity functions (LFs). Under our galaxy model, the UV LFs at $z\leq10$ and $z>10$ are in tension at $5.0σ$ in the Gaussian limit, a failure visible in the fits, although doubling the uncertainties of the $z>10$ observations reduces it to $2.1σ$. Finally, we analyse a mock SKA-Low AA$^\ast$ 1080 h 21-cm PS with the model that generated it and with a simpler, misspecified model. We find that the misspecified model silently fails: it fits the PS well, passing visual inspection and, for most noise realisations, a $χ^2$ test, yet its inferred astrophysics is wrong. The Bayesian evidence disfavours it, but only relative to the true model, which is unavailable for real observations. The suspiciousness finds no tension for the well-specified model, but a $\sim3.4σ$ tension for the misspecified one, strongly ruling it out without requiring a more adequate candidate model.

 We present Hubble Space Telescope (HST) imaging of six Local Volume dwarf galaxies identified by the SEAMLESS survey - Cetus B, Hydrus A, Ursa Major C, Hydra B, Grapes, and M83-dw14 - along with Centaurus C (Cen C), discovered serendipitously in the M83-dw14 field. #### Leaking Sound Waves: Gravitational Waves From Dark Sectors Weakly Coupled to the Standard Model
 - **Authors:** Marco Finetti , Helena Kolešová , Daniil Krichevskiy
 - **Subjects:** General Relativity and Quantum Cosmology (gr-qc) ; Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO); High Energy Physics - Phenomenology (hep-ph)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40781
 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.40781
 - **Abstract**
 First-order phase transitions in a dark sector can produce an observable stochastic gravitational-wave background, and many models predicting such signals in current and future experiments have been explored. For moderately strong transitions, hydrodynamical simulations suggest that sound waves generated by the expansion and collisions of true-vacuum bubbles are the dominant source of gravitational waves. It is often assumed that these sound waves propagate only within the dark-sector fluid, while the impact of the visible sector is neglected. In this paper, we investigate the effects of a weak coupling between the dark and visible fluids on the resulting gravitational-wave signal. We adopt a phenomenological hydrodynamical description of two ultra-relativistic fluids, parametrizing their interactions without specifying an underlying microscopic model. We focus on the regime in which the coupling can be neglected during the phase transition but becomes relevant during the subsequent, longer-lasting period of sound wave propagation. Within this framework, we analytically show that, even if the bubble expansion initially affects only the dark fluid, sound waves can subsequently be induced in the visible sector. Using the sound-shell model, we find that, despite the additional sound waves generated in the visible sector, the total gravitational-wave signal sourced by both fluids is generally suppressed compared to the case of a completely decoupled dark sector. This suppression becomes more pronounced for larger interaction rates and a larger ratio of relativistic degrees of freedom in the visible sector to those in the dark sector. The coupling also modifies the shape of the gravitational-wave spectrum.

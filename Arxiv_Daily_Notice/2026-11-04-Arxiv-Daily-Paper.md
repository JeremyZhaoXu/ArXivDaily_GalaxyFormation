#### Beyond just a torus? Re-contextualising the AGN type paradigm using data-driven host galaxy quenching histories
 - **Authors:** Sahyadri D. Krishna , Vivienne Wild , Paul C. Hewett , Victoria A. Fawcett , Omar Almaini , Carolin Villforth
 - **Subjects:** Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05353
 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.05353
 - **Abstract**
 The orientation of a dusty torus within a unified model explains the presence (Type-1) or absence (Type-2) of broad emission lines in Active Galactic Nuclei (AGN) spectra. This orientation-based unified model, however, fails to explain differences in host galaxy properties, such as star formation rates, of Type-1 and Type-2 AGNs. Star-formation histories (SFHs) can shed light on the connection between AGN type and host galaxy properties, but are challenging to extract from Type-1 AGN that outshine their host galaxies. We use mean-field independent component analysis (MFICA) spectrum decomposition to extract the stellar populations of $\sim$3000 optically selected Type-1 and Type-2 AGN host galaxies from the seventh data release (DR7) of the Sloan Digital Sky Survey (SDSS), matched in redshift ($0.16 \leq z \leq 0.4$) and AGN luminosity ($6.5<\log_{10} [L_{[OIII]}/L_\odot] <9.5$). Using MFICA, we find that only $\sim$10 per cent of AGN host galaxies have recently ($\lesssim1$\,Gyr) and rapidly quenched star formation following a starburst, with Type-2 AGN host galaxies having done so at most 100\,Myrs more recently than Type-1 AGN host galaxies. For most ($\sim$90 per cent) AGN hosts with normal recent star formation histories, Type-2 AGNs are associated with slightly younger stellar populations than Type-1 AGNs. The younger stellar populations of Type-2 AGN hosts can be explained by a link between dusty torus covering factors and stellar populations, by host-galaxy obscuration of AGN, or by an evolution in AGN type without major bursts or rapid quenching of star formation.
#### Mapping large-scale structure without galaxy formation - combining fast radio bursts with CMB lensing
 - **Authors:** James M. Sullivan , Kiyoshi Masui , Ryan Raikman , Shion Andrew , Haochen Wang
 - **Subjects:** Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05351
 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.05351
 - **Abstract**
 Fast radio bursts (FRBs) are an emerging precision probe of cosmological fluctuations. The amount and quality of observed FRB data will grow enormously over the next decade. FRB dispersion data directly probe the free electron distribution up to intergalactic scales. Precision models of the free electron distribution are necessary to exploit this exciting new probe of large-scale structure. We show that the free electron distribution almost exactly follows the total matter distribution. We confirm this fact by estimating the electron field-level bias parameters for the first time from the FLAMINGO simulations. With simulation-based knowledge of a handful of electron free parameters, the electron distribution largely acts as a second realization of the matter distribution. Working on large scales, we can leverage this fact to combine FRB correlations with those of Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB) weak lensing to constrain cosmological parameters. Assuming access to localized FRBs with redshifts, we perform cosmological forecasts using these two large-scale matter probes, which have no strong dependence on baryonic feedback models nor on parameters that encode galaxy formation. We show that the parameters $σ_8$ and $Ω_m$ are constrained at a level similar to or better than that obtained by combining FRB dispersion with weak lensing of galaxy shapes under conservative assumptions. These results motivate the use of FRB data as a complementary probe of cosmic structure and will be especially compelling as redshifts become available for localized FRB host galaxies.
#### A detailed star formation history of the distant Milky Way satellite Canes Venatici I: An old dwarf spheroidal forming most of its stars after reionisation
 - **Authors:** Pedro Pérez-García , Carme Gallart , Matteo Monelli , Santi Cassisi , Giuseppina Battaglia , Leticia Carigi , Emma Fernández-Alvar , Peter B. Stetson , Francisco Surot
 - **Subjects:** Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05274
 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.05274
 - **Abstract**
 Canes Venatici I (CVnI) is a distant Milky Way (MW) dwarf spheroidal (dSph) satellite. To date, there is no consensus on its evolution and the properties of its stellar population. This work is aimed at obtaining an accurate picture of the star formation history (SFH) and metallicity distribution of CVnI to reconstruct its evolution in detail, as well as to infer the possible effects of internal and external feedback. We used archival HST/ACS observations to obtain the deepest colour-magnitude diagram (CMD) to date, reaching $\sim$2 mag below the old main sequence turn-off. We derived the SFH and metallicity distribution of CVnI using a state-of-the-art CMD-fitting technique. We found that the SFH is dominated by a single star formation period, which peaked around 11.8 Gyr ago, well after the reionisation epoch, and remained active until $\sim$10.4 Gyr ago. The galaxy's global metallicity, [Z/H], ranges from -2.4 to -1.5 dex. Interestingly, while its star population is old and metal-poor, compared to other MW satellites of a similar size and luminosity that formed most of their stars in a single burst, CVnI experienced a delayed star formation period. We carried out an exhaustive set of tests with synthetic and real populations to ensure the reliability of these results. We suggest that the evolution of CVnI could have been regulated by its mass accretion history. The galaxy would have evolved above (but still close to) a theoretic redshift-dependent critical mass above which it is possible to maintain star formation. The combined effect of SN feedback would have made CVnI's halo mass subcritical at z$\approx$2, definitively quenching its star formation. We cannot rule out the possible influence of gravitational interaction of CVnI with the MW at early ages, as hinted at by the most recent calculations of its orbit.

 - **Abstract**
 The ALMA survey to Resolve exoKuiper belt Substructures (ARKS) presents detailed constraints on structure and properties of a sample of 24 debris discs at millimetre wavelengths, including measurements of vertical thicknesses for a subsample of discs. We estimated sizes and total masses of large bodies that feed collisional cascades producing the observed millimetre dust in 11 ARKS discs with a well constrained vertical thickness. Separately, we estimated the sizes and total masses of large bodies that could be gravitationally stirring these discs, assuming that such stirring is the origin of the vertical thickness. We used a semi-analytic particle-in-a-box collisional model and a semi-analytic model of stirring through gravitational scattering. In agreement with previous work, the observed dust masses can be explained by steady-state collisional models with reasonable disc masses. The measured vertical scale-heights can be explained by gravitational stirring from embedded large bodies of reasonable properties. However, more advanced models are needed to address the observed non-Gaussian vertical profiles, along with the dependence of disc scale-height on particle size and stellocentric distance.
#### Stress-Testing Small-Scale Cosmological Inference With Empirical Galaxy--Halo Connection Models
 - **Authors:** Hanyue Wang , Kate Storey-Fisher , Philip Mansfield , Andrew Hearin , Marcelo Alvarez , Suchetha Cooray , Risa H. Wechsler
 - **Subjects:** Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04419
 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.04419
 - **Abstract**
 Small-scale galaxy clustering contains substantial cosmological information, but extracting it reliably requires robust modeling of the galaxy--halo connection. In this work, we compare two widely adopted families of empirical models, subhalo abundance matching (SHAM) and the halo occupation distribution (HOD), testing their predictions for galaxy clustering and their recovery of the input cosmology. Using Aemulus-$β$, a new multi-cosmology simulation suite with substructure merger trees, we fit multiple SHAM and HOD models to the small-scale clustering of a stellar-mass-selected mock galaxy catalog generated with the more physical semi-empirical model UniverseMachine. We then use cosmological evidence modeling to infer the structure growth parameter $S_8$. Our results show that the SHAM model, regardless of the treatment of numerically disrupted subhalos, is able to offer tight, unbiased constraints on $S_8$ with ~2-3% precision, using simulations of relatively small volumes that span a limited number of cosmologies. In contrast, HOD models, even when accounting for assembly bias, yield weaker and potentially biased cosmological constraints that are sensitive to implementation choices: the inferred $S_8$ degrades in precision to ~6-11% and can be offset by ~1$σ$. This suggests that the excessive parametric flexibility and model misspecification of most commonly adopted HOD models can degrade and bias the resulting cosmological constraints. We further evaluate each model's performance in predicting higher-order statistics not used in the fit and find that SHAM generally reproduces them more accurately than the tested HOD models. These results highlight the potential of leveraging predictive subhalo-level information from simulations to enable robust cosmological inference on small scales.
#### Measuring the ultraviolet background at $z\simeq3$ from the quasar proximity effect in DESI DR1
 - **Authors:** Patrick Shaw , Rupert A. C. Croft
 - **Subjects:** Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04316
 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.04316
 - **Abstract**
 Ionizing radiation from quasars reduces the abundance of neutral hydrogen in the surrounding intergalactic medium (IGM), suppressing Ly$α$ absorption. This absorption deficit, known as the quasar proximity effect, can be used to measure the ultraviolet background (UVBG). We measure the hydrogen photoionization rate of the UVBG in Data Release 1 of the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI). Our sample contains 6,245 quasars over $2.75\leq z<3.25$, selected with rest-frame absolute magnitudes at 1450 Angstroms of $M_{1450}\leq-25.5$ and signal-to-noise ratios of at least 3 measured redward of Ly$α$. We reconstruct quasar continua with principal component analysis trained on Hubble Space Telescope spectra and stack the resulting transmitted-flux profiles. We fit the resulting stacked profile with a halo model that accounts for reduced absorption from quasar radiation and enhanced absorption in the surrounding overdense environment. We constrain the environmental contribution using independent measurements of quasar clustering bias, while the transmission profile constrains the UVBG. To validate our method, we construct mock spectra matched to the luminosity and noise properties of our DESI sample using the Astrid hydrodynamical simulation at $z=3$. We apply the same analysis pipeline to the mock spectra and DESI data, testing the recovery of known input photoionization rates and using the results to calibrate the final UVBG measurement. We obtain $Γ_{\mathrm{HI}}=(1.376^{+0.095}_{-0.076})\times10^{-12}\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}$ at $z\simeq3$. The statistical errors are obtained by bootstrap resampling the DESI sightlines. We also estimate a systematic uncertainty of $^{+0.837}_{-0.521}\times10^{-12}\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}$.
#### Tidal Evolution of Satellite Dwarf Galaxies. I. Tidal Tracks of Stellar Mass and Structure
 - **Authors:** Feihong He , Fangzhou Jiang

 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04208
 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.04208
 - **Abstract**
 Satellite dwarf galaxies are reshaped by the tidal field of their host, a process that may account for part of the structural diversity of the observed dwarf population. We study how the stellar component of a satellite responds to tidal stripping using a suite of 480 controlled $N$-body simulations of satellite galaxies consisting of both dark matter (DM) and stars orbiting a Milky-Way-mass host. The simulated dwarfs span a broad range of DM halo structural and stellar-distribution parameters, including halo concentration, inner density slope, stellar-to-halo mass ratio, compactness of the stellar distribution with respect to the halo, as well as the Sérsic index of the stellar profiles. We present a comprehensive library of tidal tracks, namely, the evolution of the stellar mass, half-mass radius, mean surface density, and line-of-sight velocity dispersion, as functions of the bound mass fraction of the hosting DM subhalo, and provide simple and accurate fitting functions. The Sérsic index and inner DM slope, in turn, govern the late-stage stellar mass-loss slope and the contraction or expansion of the remnant. Our simulations and empirical models provide a framework for modelling the stellar evolution of satellites across a broad range of progenitor structures, useful for semi-analytical models of satellite galaxy evolution.

 - **Authors:** Changjo Seo , Seyoung Jeon , Emanuele Contini , Yohan Dubois , Corentin Cadiou , San Han , J. K. Jang , S. Peirani , Christophe Pichon , Jinsu Rhee , Sukyoung K. Yi
 - **Subjects:** Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03814
 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.03814
 - **Abstract**
 The baryon fraction provides a fundamental diagnostic of baryonic matter distribution within dark matter halos and the baryon cycle. While its cosmic value is well constrained, the baryon content within halos on nearly all scales is significantly lower than the cosmic mean; this is called the ``missing baryon problem.'' Using the high spatial, mass, and temporal resolutions of the NewCluster simulation, we investigate the baryon fraction evolution by tracing halo assembly histories from merger trees and gas tracer particles. We identify three distinct evolutionary phases: an early growth phase driven by cold accretion in low-mass halos, a suppression phase likely dominated by supernova feedback, and a recovery phase in massive halos enabled by deeper potential wells and hot-mode accretion. We further find that the redshift dependence of baryon fraction, particularly in low-mass halos, is influenced by the thermal state of the intergalactic medium, with suppression emerging after the surrounding gas becomes persistently hotter than the halo virial temperature. In addition, the scatter in baryon fraction at fixed halo mass correlates with the large-scale environment, with baryon-rich halos preferentially residing closer to filamentary structures. Overall, our results highlight that baryon fraction evolution is governed by the interplay between feedback and environment, requiring a time-resolved perspective on the baryon cycle.

 - **Abstract**
 Tackling cosmology with Milgromian Dynamics (MOND) has been a challenge ever since its theoretical inception. In a hybrid MOND scheme, a hypothesized light sterile neutrino of $\approx$ 11 eV/$c^2$ rest-mass is able to provide realistic initial conditions, fitting the third peak of the Planck CMB power spectrum as a Hot Dark Matter (HDM) component. At the same time, MOND governs the gravitational dynamics of density perturbations, while the background expansion follows the standard FLRW metric. We conduct and analyze hydrodynamical simulations of the fiducial $Λ$CDM model ($H_0 = 67.8$ km/s/Mpc, $Ω_m \approx 0.31$, $Ω_{\text{CDM}} \approx 0.26$), the progenitor $ν$HDM model ($H_0 = 67.64$ km/s/Mpc, $Ω_m = 0.3, Ω_ν\approx 0.26$), and its optimized version (opt-$ν$HDM model, $H_0 \approx 55.6$ km/s/Mpc, $Ω_m \approx 0.49$, $Ω_ν \approx 0.42$), utilizing the publicly available codes RAMSES and Phantom of RAMSES. We compare for the first time the total matter power spectrum $P(k)$ of the hydrodynamical simulations at various redshifts, examining the systematic differences of the three investigated cosmologies. The redshift evolution of the power spectrum of the $Λ$CDM model compared to the $ν$HDM variants' counterpart offers important insight about the onset of structure formation. The combination of a light sterile neutrino and MOND gravity exceeds the $Λ$CDM model's matter $P(k)$ over all collapsing scales at late times ($z\lesssim 1$). The density and velocity fields reveal a distinct difference between the underlying laws of gravity. Regardless of the modeled dependence in the jointly estimated data from Planck, DES Y1, SDSS, and eBOSS, the $ν$HDM hybrid models seem to produce an excess of power at associated resolution scales due to their cosmological design.

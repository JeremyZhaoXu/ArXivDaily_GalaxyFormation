#### Living near filaments: How environment shapes age profiles in low-mass galaxies
 - **Authors:** Elisa A. Tau , Diego Pallero , Rory Smith , Raúl Baier-Soto , Antonela Monachesi , Silvio Varela-Lavin
 - **Subjects:** Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06632
 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.06632
 - **Abstract**
 U-shaped radial age profiles have recently been reported in low-mass galaxies. This type of profile differs from the usually observed positive radial age gradients of the low-mass regime. We aim to characterise the environment in which low-mass galaxies with U-shaped age profiles are more commonly found. We use the IllustrisTNG-50 hydrodynamical simulations to select a sample of galaxies with stellar masses ranging from $10^9 \, M_\odot$ to $10^{10} \, M_\odot$. Based on their radial age profiles, we classify the sample into galaxies with and without a U-shaped feature. We define a U-shaped profile as one where stellar ages decrease towards a local minimum and subsequently increase, requiring an age contrast of at least 0.4 Gyr on either side of the minimum relative to its value. We find 145 galaxies with U-shaped age profiles ($\sim 11\%$ of the sample). They are mostly central systems ($\sim 72\%$) and $\sim 82\%$ of them reside within low-mass halos ($M_{200} < 10^{12} \, M_\odot$). We also find that galaxies exhibiting this feature are located at large distances from galaxy groups but close to cosmic filaments, with a median distance of $\sim 265$ kpc, whereas those without U-shaped age profiles tend to be located farther away from these structures ($\sim 351$ kpc).
#### AGN Host Galaxies in COSMOS-Web: Linking Structural Evolution and Star Formation Quenching up to $z=5$
 - **Authors:** Xiao Han , Lilan Yang , T. Kelly , Matthew A. Malkan , Luis C. Ho , John D. Silverman , Zi-Jian Li , Andreas L. Faisst , Andrew J. Battisti , Jeyhan S. Kartaltepe , Lauro Moscardini , Rasha M. Samir , Ronaldo Laishram , Tao Wang , Yiyang Zhang , Caitlin M. Casey , Henry Joy McCracken , Jason Rhodes , Brant E. Robertson , Maximilien Franco , Daizhong Liu , Marko Shuntov , Xuheng Ding
 - **Subjects:** Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06443
 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.06443
 - **Abstract**
 The role of active galactic nuclei (AGNs) in the suppression of star formation and structural evolution of galaxies during their transition from star-forming to quiescent systems remains unclear. We analyze 3,955 AGNs selected through five multiwavelength diagnostics at $0<z<5$. Using high-resolution JWST imaging from the COSMOS-Web survey and archival multi-wavelength photometry, we investigate the rest-frame optical size-mass distribution of AGN host galaxies and compare it with those of star-forming galaxies (SFGs) and quiescent galaxies (QGs). We find that the positions of AGN hosts in the size-mass plane depend on both redshift and stellar mass. At $z\gtrsim2$, massive AGN hosts ($M_\star/M_\odot>10^{10.5}$) generally exhibit sizes comparable to those of SFGs despite suppressed star formation. Toward lower redshifts, they increasingly approach the QG size-mass relation, accompanied by declining specific star formation rates (sSFRs) and increasing Sérsic indices. In contrast, lower-mass AGN hosts remain close to the SFG relation across the redshift range. Overall, AGN hosts exhibit relatively mild size evolution, following $R_e\propto(1+z)^{-0.6}$ over $0<z<4$, shallower than that of typical SFGs and QGs. We further examine the relationship between star formation activity and structural compactness in the $Δ\log\mathrm{SFR}_{\mathrm{MS}}$-$ΔΣ_e$ plane. At $z<2.2$, AGN hosts predominantly occupy the compact star-forming and quiescent regions, broadly consistent with the compaction-quenching scenario. At $z>2.2$, however, some AGN hosts exhibit suppressed star formation while retaining relatively extended structures. These findings suggest that some AGN hosts may follow alternative evolutionary pathways in which star formation suppression precedes substantial structural compaction.
#### Net Cooling Tables for Cosmological Simulations under Multiple UV/X-ray Background Models
 - **Authors:** Laura Di Federico , Klaus Dolag , Ildar Khabibullin , Luca Sala , Geray S. Karademir , Milena Valentini
 - **Subjects:** Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06360
 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.06360
 - **Abstract**
 We present three sets of net cooling tables for cosmological hydrodynamical simulations, computed with CLOUDY 23.01 for optically thin gas in photoionization equilibrium with three different UV/X-ray background models. Following the element-by-element approach, and in a format compatible with existing tables, we tabulate cooling, heating, and net rates separately for hydrogen and helium over an extended range of helium mass fractions and for 13 heavy elements, together with hydrogen species fractions, electron abundances, and mean particle masses. For gas of Solar composition, the choice of UV/X-ray background has little impact on the net cooling rates at low redshift, but the results increasingly diverge towards high redshift, particularly in diffuse gas near thermal equilibrium. Compared with widely used previous tables, cooling times in this regime differ by up to an order of magnitude at $z \gtrsim 4.6$, and the calculations disagree on whether the gas undergoes net cooling or heating over nearly 10% of the diffuse density-temperature plane at $z \approx 8$, corresponding to the intergalactic medium. These trends persist for gas composed exclusively of hydrogen and helium. The tables provide a consistent framework for assessing the impact of the radiation background on gas cooling in cosmological simulations.

 - **Abstract**
 The study of transient events, such as Gamma-Ray Bursts (GRBs) and extreme Supernovae (SNe), provides a unique probe of star-forming environments in the early Universe. Traditional global host galaxy studies often fail to capture the local conditions around the progenitor, leading to potential mischaracterizations due to metallicity gradients and spatial inhomogeneities. #### Supermassive black holes and nuclear star clusters in galaxies: constraining formation and co-evolution with SHARP and MICADO
 - **Authors:** E. Dalla Bonta' , E. Portaluri , C. Arcidiacono , E. Bortolas , S. Ciroi , E. M. Corsini , D. Gasparri , L. Morelli , A. Pizzella , M. Signorini
 - **Subjects:** Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06334
 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.06334
 - **Abstract**
 Nuclear star clusters and supermassive black holes are the dominant compact components in galaxy nuclei. They can coexist within the same system, and their relative prominence traces the history of gas inflow, star formation, and dynamical evolution on parsec scales. Key questions remain open concerning the physical channels that assemble nuclear star clusters, the mass regimes in which supermassive black holes become dynamically dominant, and the conditions under which compact stellar nuclei are suppressed, destroyed, or replaced by cusp-like structures. We describe how near-infrared integral-field spectroscopy with SHARP on the Extremely Large Telescope, combined with high-resolution imaging from MICADO, enables quantitative tests of nuclear formation and evolution scenarios within a unified framework. The joint analysis of stellar dynamics, stellar populations, and nuclear structure allows black-hole masses, internal kinematics of nuclear star clusters, and signatures of gas inflow to be measured consistently within the same systems, and defines the mass and distance ranges where robust constraints can be obtained. Recent discoveries of compact active nuclei at high redshift indicate that efficient black-hole growth can occur in systems with weak or compact stellar components. Although nearby galaxies are not evolutionary analogues of early systems, the diversity of nuclear configurations accessible with SHARP and MICADO provides a local laboratory for isolating the physical mechanisms that regulate the coupled evolution of central black holes and nuclear stellar structures.
#### Understanding galaxies and the origin of the golden mass through the study of scaling relations across cosmic history
 - **Authors:** C. Tortora , A. Gallazzi , A. Gargiulo , F. La Barbera , N. R. Napolitano , R. Ragusa , S. Zibetti
 - **Subjects:** Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06333
 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.06333
 - **Abstract**
 The total mass distribution in galaxies strongly influences star formation (SF), both directly via gas availability and feedback, and indirectly via the dark matter (DM) potential well. Star formation efficiency peaks at a characteristic stellar mass of $5 \times 10^{10} M_{\odot}$, often termed the "golden mass", where feedback mechanisms minimally affect SF. This scale also appears in other scaling relations: correlations between stellar population gradients, DM fraction, and total mass density slope versus stellar mass all show pronounced minima at the golden mass. How, why, and when does this golden mass emerge during galaxy evolution? Early cosmic epochs ($z > 1.5$) remain largely unexplored, yet they probe the formative stages of galaxy mass assembly. They provide the full redshift baseline needed to constrain the buildup of stellar mass and the efficiency of gas-to-star conversion. By combining high resolution, multiplexing, and a wide field of view, SHARP on the ELT will address these questions, delivering sharper and deeper data than JWST. In this SHARP White Paper contribution, we present a scientific case exploiting these unique characteristics. SHARP will enable precise measurements of stellar population gradients, DM fractions, and total mass density slopes. Using the mIFU mode, SHARP will obtain spatially-resolved stellar population and dynamical maps for galaxies at $z > 1.5$. Reconstructing these observables for early- and late-type systems will allow us to trace the emergence of the golden mass across cosmic time, and to decode the impact of galaxy processes (mergers, SN, and AGN feedback) by comparing observations with cosmological simulations. #### Quiescent galaxies at cosmic noon: from individual element abundances to star formation histories
 - **Authors:** M. Longhetti , F.R. Ditrani , D. Bevacqua , G. De Lucia , A. Gallazzi , A. Gargiulo , F. La Barbera , C. Maraston , D. Thomas , P. Franzetti , C. Mancini , L. Scholz-Díaz , S. Zibetti

 - **Abstract**
 Understanding the formation and early evolution of massive quiescent galaxies is crucial for a complete picture of galaxy formation in a cosmological context. Such a comprehensive picture can be obtained through measurements of individual elemental abundances, which encode the timescales and physical conditions of their star-formation and assembly histories. While extensive work at low and intermediate redshift has revealed strong correlations between stellar mass, metallicity, and abundance ratios, studies at z ~ 2-3 remain extremely limited. Their faintness and compactness make medium-resolution near-infrared spectroscopy for detailed chemical analysis extremely challenging. We present a science case to measure full elemental abundance patterns in a representative sample of massive quiescent galaxies at cosmic noon, exploiting rest-frame optical absorption features. We show that deriving robust stellar population constraints for these systems requires spectra with S/N ~ 20 per resolution element, together with a spatial resolution comparable to their ~ 0.1 arcsec sizes. We show that such requirements can only be met with a 40m class telescope equipped with a powerful adaptive optics system. In this context, SHARP-NEXUS, in combination with MORFEO-MCAO capabilities, emerges as the only viable ELT instrument capable of enabling this science, providing both the sensitivity and angular resolution needed. Although massive quiescent galaxies are rare at these redshifts, their study can efficiently exploit the instrument's multiplexing by targeting galaxy overdensities and coordinating with surveys of star-forming galaxies. Such synergistic strategies, proven feasible in high-redshift clusters and by multiplex-sharing facilities like 4MOST, would allow SHARP-NEXUS to open a new window on the chemical enrichment of the earliest massive galaxies.

#### Spectroscopy-informed Deep Learning Searches for Photometric Little Red Dots in JWST Surveys
 - **Authors:** Chenxuan Zhang , Huanian Zhang , Qingwen Wu (HUST)
 - **Subjects:** Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06026
 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.06026
 - **Abstract**
 Little Red Dots (LRDs) are a population of compact, red sources discovered at high redshift with JWST that exhibit distinctive V-shaped spectral energy distributions (SEDs). Existing LRD samples are typically identified using a variety of color-based selection criteria, which can lead to differences in the populations recovered by different studies. We develop a cross-modal machine-learning framework that combines matched NIRCam imaging and NIRSpec/PRISM spectroscopy to transfer spectroscopic information into imaging-based representations, enabling LRD identification from JWST/NIRCam imaging alone. Applied to spectroscopically observed high-redshift galaxies previously classified as non-LRDs, our model identifies 35 new LRD candidates. The majority satisfy at least one independent LRD color selection criterion, and among the 13 candidates with medium- or high-resolution NIRSpec grating spectra, 10 exhibit broad H$α$ emission - a hallmark of LRDs. We further apply the classifier to public NIRCam imaging from the Dawn JWST Archive and two independent JWST surveys, yielding 357 and 23 additional LRD candidates, respectively. The candidates recovered by our method span a broader range of observed SED properties than those in existing samples, suggesting that current color-selected samples may not fully capture the diversity of LRD-like systems present in JWST surveys. Our findings highlight the value of spectroscopy-informed photometric classification as a powerful strategy for systematically mining large-area JWST imaging datasets and provide a practical framework for future studies of this emerging population.
#### Insights on the mass composition of ultrahigh-energy cosmic rays from small-scale anisotropy with large-scale structure simulations
 - **Authors:** Ryo Higuchi , Eiji Kido , Shigehiro Nagataki
 - **Subjects:** High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05783
 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.05783
 - **Abstract**
 Ultra-high-energy cosmic rays (UHECRs) above 100 EeV provide important clues to the most energetic processes in the Universe. At the highest energies, it is expected that UHECRs originate only from nearby sources because of energy losses during propagation. In such a regime, small-scale anisotropies in arrival directions could appear if the magnetic deflections are sufficiently small, for example, for a limited fraction of UHECRs with relatively low electric charge. Using the Pierre Auger Observatory open data, we confirm there is no significant small-scale anisotropy above 100 EeV within 3 degrees, indicating an almost isotropic sky distribution. To interpret this, we consider that heavier nuclei have higher photodisintegration thresholds due to their smaller Lorentz factors, resulting in longer propagation lengths. Combination with the nearly homogeneous distribution of galaxies at such distances can explain the absence of anisotropies above 100 EeV. Based on this idea, we perform mock observations of UHECRs above 100 EeV assuming pure-proton, iron, and uranium compositions. We predict the expected UHECR distributions using the Millennium Run semi-analytic galaxy catalog and turbulent extragalactic magnetic fields. By varying the source density and magnetic field strength, we evaluate the anisotropy for each case. Without magnetic fields, the observed isotropy above 100 EeV excludes the pure-proton and pure-iron cases. The pure-uranium case can reproduce the observed isotropy when the source density is high and the magnetic field is turbulent. When the turbulent field is strong, both the iron and uranium cases remain consistent with the data, whereas almost no parameter set can explain the observation for a pure-proton composition. Our results constrain the possible combinations of source density and magnetic field strength across different UHECR compositions.
#### Constraining Ganymede's Interior Structure and Bulk Composition from Its Present-Day Magnetic Field Strength
 - **Authors:** Jun Kimura , Ryohei Kawakami , Taro Matsuo , Sho Sasaki
 - **Subjects:** Earth and Planetary Astrophysics (astro-ph.EP)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05727
 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.05727
 - **Abstract**
 Ganymede is the only Solar System satellite with a global magnetic field driven by a core dynamo. Although its small moment-of-inertia (MoI) factor indicates a differentiated structure of a metallic core, rocky mantle, and outer hydrosphere, its exact bulk composition and layer thicknesses remain highly uncertain. We developed a comprehensive, long-term thermochemical evolution model that couples thermal history with magnetic field generation. Building upon previous theoretical frameworks, this study systematically evaluates how diverse chondritic precursor compositions constrain Ganymede's internal thermochemical evolution and core solidification processes. Based on interior structures initially permitted by a wide range of chondritic compositions and geodetic constraints, we applied the observed equatorial surface magnetic field strength ($\sim$719 nT) as a dynamic filter. Our evolutionary simulations demonstrate that while magnetic dynamos can operate under entire melting, Fe-snow, and FeS-layer regimes, the requirement to sustain the observed field strength strongly restricts the viable interior models. Satisfying this constraint preferentially requires a relatively large core radius and a specific, non-eutectic bulk core sulfur concentration. Consequently, this coupled thermochemical approach highlights the main novelty of this study: characterizing the hydrosphere's density serves as an independent constraint to break the structural degeneracy of Ganymede's bulk composition. Our model indicates that L and CI chondrites yield parameter spaces capable of sustaining comparable field strengths under a low-density hydrosphere scenario (1,200 kg m-3), whereas the H chondrite composition can satisfy this criterion under a high-density scenario (1,300 kg m-3). These predictions provide a critical framework to be tested by the JUICE mission arriving in 2031.

 The observed stellar mass-metallicity relation for ultra-faint galaxies in the Local Group (LG) is in tension with many theoretical predictions, suggesting the need for modified or additional channels of elemental enrichment at low metallicities. We present a suite of FIRE-3 cosmological baryonic zoom-in simulations of isolated ultra-faint galaxies run to z=0 (maximum baryonic resolution ~ 30 Msun, 0.1 pc) and LMC-mass environments (maximum baryonic resolution ~ 110 Msun, 0.5 pc), with different sub-grid models for Population III stars. We also examine the ways in which early-time Pop. III feedback impacts the metallicity distributions and [Mg/Fe] abundance ratios in ultra-faint galaxies, and make a prediction for the stellar mass - [Mg/H] metallicity relation from our simulations.

 - **Abstract**
 The intermediate neutron-capture process ($i$-process) has been invoked to explain the peculiar compositions of carbon-enhanced metal-poor (CEMP) stars enriched in both slow- ($s$) and rapid- ($r$) neutron-capture elements. Recent literature has questioned its operation at higher metallicities, but observational evidence remains limited. We analyse Gemini/GHOST and McDonald/Tull spectra of six $\rm -0.7 < [Fe/H] < -0.1$ stars identified in GALAH by their extreme Ba and Eu (or Sm) compositions ($\gtrsim$10$\times$ solar). We also reanalyse archival spectra of two literature $i$-process candidates. We determine abundances in up to 42 elements (Li through Pb), $^{12}$C/$^{13}$C, and Mg isotopic ratios, and compare to literature barium and CEMP stars and multiple asymptotic giant branch (AGB) model grids. Three stars exhibit extreme [Pb/Fe] (+1.79$\pm$0.19 to +2.50$\pm$0.32 dex), and two extreme [Eu/Fe] (+1.10$\pm$0.11 to +1.30$\pm$0.11 dex), distinguishing them from typical metal-rich barium stars. Two of these three exhibit abundance patterns that are inconsistent with $s$-process models and best explained by existing $i$-process predictions. The remaining sample is either consistent with $s$-process enrichment or suggestive of atomic diffusion. Both literature candidates are well-fit by the $i$-process model, but conventional $s$-process enrichment cannot be excluded. Our findings reinforce that metal-rich AGB stars contribute to the production of elements classically attributed to the $r$-process. These results identify promising candidates for $i$-process enrichment near solar metallicity and motivate additional $i$-process models to understand the contribution of this nucleosynthetic process to the chemical evolution of the Milky Way disk.

#### Mercury (Hg): a relativistic $N$-body simulation for Horndeski gravity
 - **Authors:** Ahmad Nouri-Zonoz , Farbod Hassani , Julian Adamek , Emilio Bellini , Martin Kunz
 - **Subjects:** Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO) ; General Relativity and Quantum Cosmology (gr-qc); Computational Physics (physics.comp-ph)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03637
 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.03637
 - **Abstract**
 We present Mercury, a relativistic $N$-body code for cosmological simulations in the $α$-basis formulation of Horndeski gravity. Mercury extends a particle-mesh scheme by incorporating the dynamics of the additional scalar degree of freedom and the corresponding modified Einstein equations, expressed in terms of the time-dependent functions $α_{\rm K}, α_{\rm B}, α_{\rm M}, α_{\rm T}$ and the effective Planck mass $M_*^2$. This generalises our previous implementation of Horndeski subclass models, namely $k$-essence and kinetic gravity braiding (KGB), to the general linear Horndeski $α$-basis. The code evolves the additional scalar degree of freedom and its effective stress--energy tensor alongside the nonlinear matter and metric fields, so that nonlinear structure formation sources the scalar-field evolution and feeds back on the gravitational potentials. Through its interface with hi_class, Mercury supports both phenomenological $α$-function parametrisations and covariant Horndeski theories; we demonstrate the latter using nKGB, the Cubic Galileon, and Brans--Dicke models. We validate the implementation against hi_class in the linear regime and recover the expected nonlinear $k$-essence and KGB limits. For the luminal Horndeski models considered here, we show how a running effective Planck mass generates gravitational slip through the scalar anisotropic stress and modifies the friction terms governing the vector and tensor metric perturbations. The evolution of $M_*^2$ also modifies the normalisation and scale dependence of the effective dark-energy density contrast, leaving a corresponding imprint on its power spectrum. The present framework provides an intermediate step towards nonlinear Horndeski simulations, in which nonlinear scalar self-interactions and screening effects can be incorporated consistently within the same relativistic framework.
#### Baryonic feedback and intrinsic alignments in profile: a frequentist view of DES Y3 cosmic shear
 - **Authors:** Anoma Ganguly , Elisabeth Krause , Tanvi Karwal , Vivian Miranda
 - **Subjects:** Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03537
 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.03537
 - **Abstract**
 Current cosmic shear surveys primarily constrain $S_8$ and $Ω_\mathrm{m}$, while most other model parameters remain dominated by priors. Consequently, marginal posteriors are vulnerable to prior-volume effects, particularly through degeneracies among $S_8$, baryonic feedback, and intrinsic alignment (IA) model parameters. To isolate data-driven constraints from prior volume effects, we compare profile likelihoods with Bayesian posteriors for DES Y3 cosmic shear under the DES Y3+KiDS-1000 Hybrid model. For cosmic shear alone, the profile global best fit and the marginal posterior mean agree in $S_8$, but the profile likelihood reveals a strong degeneracy between feedback strength and $S_8$, leaving the upper $1σ$ limit on $S_8$ determined by the assumed feedback range. External CMB information on parameters other than $Ω_\mathrm{m}$ and $S_8$ breaks this degeneracy, suppressing prior-volume effects and reconciling the profiles with the posteriors. With CMB data included, small-scale cosmic shear data prefers moderate baryonic feedback strengths consistent with hydrodynamical simulations calibrated to observed gas fractions. Furthermore, additional unconstrained parameters in the tidal alignment and tidal torquing (TATT) model result in weaker constraints, shifting and broadening the $S_8$ profile relative to the nonlinear linear alignment (NLA) model. Adding DES Y3 shear ratios mitigates this degeneracy, bringing profile likelihood and marginal posteriors into close agreement.
#### Thirty years of primordial deuterium abundances: A provenance-aware statistical reanalysis
 - **Authors:** Richard de Grijs , Giuseppe Bono
 - **Subjects:** Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02289
 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.02289
 - **Abstract**
 (ABRIDGED:) Primordial deuterium provides one of the most precise observational tests of Big Bang nucleosynthesis, yet the published literature is built on a comparatively small number of high-redshift absorption systems. We construct a provenance-aware database of 957 published primordial-D/H values from 247 publications spanning 1994--2026 and trace observational entries, wherever possible, to their underlying physical absorbers. Of the 795 observational entries, 80.1 percent represent reused individual or compilation values; only 11.4 percent correspond to new determinations of individual absorption systems. Although 52 distinct absorbers are identified overall, the eight most frequently represented systems account for 62.0 percent of all identifiable absorber mentions. An inverse Herfindahl--Hirschman measure applied to our full database gives an effective representation equivalent to only 16.28 equally represented absorbers, demonstrating that the published literature is strongly concentrated around a much smaller effective subset. Despite this strong internal dependence, the modern consensus value of $10^5({\rm D/H})\simeq2.5$ is robust to alternative absorber-level treatments and survives removal of the eight dominant systems. However, the apparent precision of literature-level compilations is more sensitive to repeated representation than a simple count of published values implies. We also identify a synchronised transition in the compilation literature around 2013--2014, associated with increasingly selective and homogeneous analyses while older individual values continued to circulate. The modern D/H consensus is therefore robust in central value, but the underlying literature is less statistically independent than its publication density suggests. This thus highlights the importance of provenance-aware analysis in precision cosmology.
#### Magnetized Accretion Flows with Low Angular Momentum Around Supermassive Black Holes
 - **Authors:** Razieh Ranjbar
 - **Subjects:** High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02178
 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.02178
 - **Abstract**
 We investigate the global structure of slowly rotating accretion flows around black holes in the presence of large-scale magnetic fields. Using a steady, height-integrated transonic model with radial mass loss, we examine how magnetic pressure, tension, and torque modify the dynamics of Bondi-like and disk-like accretion. Adopting a radial magnetic-field prescription based on results from previous numerical simulations, we compare hydrodynamical and magnetized solutions across a range of plasma-$\rm β$ values, from weak to strong field regimes. We find that magnetic stresses substantially alter the sonic radius and change the angular momentum and mass accretion rate reaching the black hole. For sufficiently strong fields, the inner flow becomes highly sub-Keplerian and magnetically dominated ($ β= P_{\mathrm{gas}}/ P_{\mathrm{mag}}<1$), and magnetic stresses can drive transitions to lower angular momentum. We also show that outflows in this magnetized accretion flow can significantly alter the structure of disk-like accretion. We further perform 2D simulations of magnetized Bondi accretion and torus accretion and find that the radial structure and magnetically dominated, sub-Keplerian character of our semi-analytical solutions are qualitatively reproduced.
#### Simulating winds in the Galactic centre. II. Observational biases on HI clouds and a multi-wavelength comparison
 - **Authors:** N. Peschken , E.M. Di Teodoro , M. Hanasz , L. Armillotta , D. Woltanski , A. Gawryszczak
 - **Subjects:** Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA) ; Instrumentation and Methods for Astrophysics (astro-ph.IM)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02144
 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.02144
 - **Abstract**
 High-velocity neutral hydrogen (HI) clouds found up to several kiloparsecs above the Galactic centre (GC) provide some of the most direct evidence for a nuclear wind in the Milky Way, yet the properties inferred for these clouds are influenced by how they are observed. Starting from a magnetohydrodynamical simulation in which supernova feedback drives a multiphase nuclear outflow, we generate mock HI observations matched to the angular resolution and sensitivity of Green Bank Telescope (GBT) surveys of the GC and analyse them in the same way as the real data. We find that observational effects substantially reshape the cloud population: beam smearing makes clouds appear larger, rounder and more diffuse, while noise erases faint clouds and strips low-density gas from the others. These effects combined, together with confusion with foreground disk emission, prevent the detection of more than 80% of the clouds (up to 95% during more quiescent phases), while those detected appear less massive, less turbulent and less elongated, with column densities underestimated by a factor of two; only their velocities are reliably recovered. Once these effects are included, the simulated clouds broadly reproduce the properties and sky distribution of the GBT population, but lack the fastest (> 250 km/s ) and highest-latitude (b > 10° ) clouds. Mock UV absorption and soft X-ray observations support this picture, with warm-ionized gas column densities at the low end of the observed range and X-ray emission measures too low at b > 10° . Our results imply that the true GC cloud population is more numerous, massive, turbulent and elongated than observed, and that fully reproducing observations may require a stronger recent starburst or additional feedback.

#### foraois: Fast, Flexible Extended Press-Schechter Merger Trees with a General First-Crossing Solver
 - **Authors:** C. Power (ICRAR-UWA), S. Balu (U Seville)
 - **Subjects:** Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01580
 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.01580
 - **Abstract**
 We present foraois (Gaelic for Forest; FAW-reesh), an open-source Python framework for generating Monte Carlo dark matter halo merger tree histories. foraois separates the description of the linear density field, the physics of halo collapse, and the algorithmic construction of halo merger histories, allowing different cosmologies, collapse prescriptions, and tree building approaches to be combined within a common framework. The package implements two approaches to the same merger-tree problem of sampling progenitor masses in the excursion-set picture: the semi-empirical algorithm of Parkinson, Cole, & Helly (2008), which applies a fitted correction to the EPS progenitor distribution, and the numerical solution of the Markovian first-crossing equation developed by Zhang & Hui (2006), which for the default real-space top-hat window is an approximation to the correlated-walk problem. Linear matter power spectra for Cold, Warm, and Fuzzy Dark Matter (CDM, WDM, and FDM) can be supplied, while the modular collapse treatment provides an extension point for alternative barrier models. The framework also supports constrained merger histories based on the Brownian-bridge construction of Nadler et al. (2023). We run extensive validation and scaling tests, and also show an illustrative example of coupling foraois to a light-weight semi-analytical model of galaxy formation.

 - **Abstract**
 We present Aurora, a black hole seeding module for the Mimic semi-analytic framework, in which the seed mass of each newly resolved halo is set by its environment. Motivated by evidence that early quiescent galaxies and overmassive black holes assemble in dense environments, Aurora replaces the fixed seed masses of conventional prescriptions with a continuous spectrum, coupling the seed mass to two environmental parameters, the local overdensity and the radial skewness of the surrounding haloes, and to the halo mass at seeding.

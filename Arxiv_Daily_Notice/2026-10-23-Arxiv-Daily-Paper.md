#### The deepest X-ray view of a kiloparsec scale jet at z=6.1
 - **Authors:** Fabio A. Meanti, Luca Ighina, Alberto Moretti, Kyra Bettwy, Alessandro Caccianiga, Thomas Connor, Fabrizio Tavecchio, Silvia Belladitta, Emmanuel K. Bempong-Manful, Cristiana Spingola
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE); Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA); High Energy Physics - Theory (hep-th)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30259

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.30259

 - **Abstract**
 The mechanism responsible for the high-energy emission observed in kiloparsec-scale jets across cosmic time remains poorly understood. The relativistic jet associated with the z=6.1 AGN PSO J030947.49+271757.31 is the highest-redshift large-scale jet currently resolved in X-rays, providing a unique opportunity to study these structures. We present new Chandra observations, combined with archival data, for a total exposure of 360 ks. The increased exposure allowed us to resolve the extended X-ray emission into two distinct, slightly resolved components and perform spatially resolved spectral analyses. The extended emission has a photon index of Gamma = 2.25 (+0.45, -0.44), steeper than that of the core (Gamma = 1.86 +/- 0.09), and an extended-to-core luminosity ratio of 18%. Simulations using the Chandra image as input show that an AXIS-like mission could increase the detected photon statistics by a factor of approximately 17. We resolve the kiloparsec-scale jet into two main components and constrain its spectral properties. This is the most distant and one of the most X-ray-luminous large-scale jets currently known. Future X-ray missions with arcsecond-scale angular resolution could enable more detailed studies of such systems and reveal a population of fainter high-redshift jets beyond Chandra's reach.
#### The THESAN-ZOOM project: the delayed rise of Lyman continuum escape fractions in bursty high-redshift galaxies
 - **Authors:** Parth Mehrotra, Aaron Smith, Yuan Bian, William McClymont, Ewald Puchwein, Rahul Kannan, Laura Keating, Xuejian Shen, Mark Vogelsberger, Sandro Tacchella, Matthew Hayes, Crystal L. Martin, Cody Carr, Anand Utsav Kapoor, Shyam Menon, Alaina Henry
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA); Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30265

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.30265

 - **Abstract**
 The ionizing radiation that galaxies release into their surroundings depends on whether efficient Lyman continuum (LyC) escape coincides with strong photon production. Stellar feedback can open escape paths as the source population fades, linking the escaping radiation to a galaxy's recent history. We investigate this timing in thirteen high-redshift (z > 3) target haloes from the THESAN-ZOOM radiation-hydrodynamic simulations, post-processed with COLT radiative transfer and sampled at ~10 Myr intervals. The clearest delayed relationship links $\text{SFR}_{10}/\text{SFR}_{100}$ to the LyC escape fraction. Selecting the nearest positive cross-correlation peak gives typical lags of ~16 Myr and peak correlations of 0.4-0.6 in massive haloes, with longer and less coherent delays towards lower masses. The fitted delays scale approximately as $M_\star^{-0.19}$ and $M_\text{halo}^{-0.27}$, although the shortest lags approach the native snapshot spacing. Recurrent variations span multiple amplitude-dependent timescales, consistent with repeated episodes of star formation and feedback. Recent star formation closely tracks both intrinsic and escaping photon rates, while the escape fraction reflects the evolving gas transparency. The H$\alpha$-to-UV luminosity ratio retains a delayed association with escape, offering an observable connection between burst phase and leakage. High transparency during a fading phase can nevertheless yield little escaping radiation. The temporal overlap of photon production and transmission therefore shapes both the amount and timing of escaping radiation, informing the interpretation of observed leakers and their contribution to cosmic reionization.

#### The Milky Way's Early Disk Was Built in Bursts. II. The Multi-component Age-Metallicity Structure of the High-$α$ Disk
 - **Authors:** Tiancheng Sun, Maosheng Xiang, Jifeng Liu, Meng Zhang, Yaqian Wu
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA); Solar and Stellar Astrophysics (astro-ph.SR)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30659

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.30659

 - **Abstract**
 Whether the high-$\alpha$ disk experienced a continuous or episodic star-formation history remains an open question. Here we investigate the age--metallicity distribution of the high-$\alpha$ disk using approximately 145,000 Gaia DR3 subgiants with $[\mathrm{Fe/H}]>-1$, 6.5~kpc $\lesssim R\lesssim9.5$~kpc, and $|Z|\lesssim1.5$~kpc. We adopt a forward-modelling framework that accounts for individual age and metallicity uncertainties and corrects for sample selection effects. The reconstructed distribution reveals two components: a broad, dominant population peaking at $\sim10.6$ Gyr and $[\mathrm{Fe/H}]\simeq-0.38$, and an older, more metal-poor population peaking at $\sim12.4$ Gyr and $[\mathrm{Fe/H}]\simeq-0.79$. These features suggest an episodic history of star formation and chemical enrichment in the high-$\alpha$ disk, qualitatively consistent with cosmological simulations in which recurrent gas accretion drives variations in the star formation rate. The age of the older component is close to the $\sim12.3$~Gyr epoch inferred for the proposed Low-energy--Kraken--Heracles accretion event, suggesting a possible connection between this early interaction and enhanced disk star formation. There is tentative evidence that the dominant population is possibly composed of two sub-components with age distributions peaking at $\sim11.1$ Gyr and $\sim10.0$ Gyr, respectively. Together, these results favour a picture in which the high-$\alpha$ disk was built through recurrent episodes of enhanced star formation rather than a single temporally smooth phase, even for the regime of ${\rm [Fe/H]}>-1$.

#### A cosmic filament catalog for COSMOS-Web Data Release 1 across 0.5 < z < 3.5
 - **Authors:** Qiaoyang Hao, Tao Wang, Weishan Zhu, Hong Guo, Peng Wang, Tiancheng Yang, Luwenjia Zhou
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO); Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA); Instrumentation and Methods for Astrophysics (astro-ph.IM)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31168

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.31168

 - **Abstract**
 Cosmic filaments represent the environments in which most galaxies form and evolve. However, the spatial distribution of filaments and the link between galaxy properties and their positions relative to filaments remains unclear, particularly at high redshifts. In this work, using the deep-field catalog COSMOS-Web Data Release 1, we construct a filament catalog spanning 0.5 < z < 3.5 that can be directly applied to studies of galaxy evolution and the galaxy--large-scale-structure connection at the peak epoch of galaxy and black hole formation. We developed a new procedure based on the DisPerSE method that fully incorporates the probability distribution functions (PDFs) of galaxy photometric redshifts by generating multiple realizations for each tomographic 2D slice. We then stacked these realizations to construct ``filament probability maps'' (w_ fil maps), from which filaments were subsequently extracted. The filament extraction parameters were optimized using mock catalogs generated from TNG simulations by minimizing the discrepancies between 3D and projected 2D filaments. Applying this procedure to the COSMOS-Web data, we detected 12,523 filaments (comprising 136,210 segments) across 0.5 < z < 3.5 and released a filament catalog, a companion segment catalog, and filament probability maps for each slice. Validation against astrophysical properties shows a strong agreement between the extracted filaments and those in the simulations, supporting the robustness of the filament extraction. These data products provide a robust foundation for investigating galaxy--environment coevolution at cosmic noon and beyond.
#### Charged fluid tori with an approximate magnetic self-field around magnetized Schwarzschild black holes: A semi-analytic approach
 - **Authors:** Abylaikhan Tlemissov, Jiri Kovar
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE); General Relativity and Quantum Cosmology (gr-qc)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31577

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.31577

 - **Abstract**
 We study toroidal structures formed by a charged, non-self-gravitating perfect fluid orbiting a Schwarzschild black hole immersed in a poloidal magnetic field. To obtain analytic and semi-analytic solutions, we assume a cold, rest-mass-dominated fluid with uniform specific charge and zero conductivity, so the current is purely convective. This regime is formally opposite to the ideal GRMHD limit. The total magnetic field comprises a prescribed asymptotically uniform external component and an approximate self-field associated with the azimuthal current in the torus. We represent the latter by a regularized dipole approximation to the field of an effective circular current loop. Neglecting the self-field, the method of characteristics yields a general implicit solution of the integrability condition for a function parametrizing the specific-angular-momentum profile. A separately obtained cylindrically dependent class recovers a solution from our earlier work. For the radially dependent class, we integrate the Euler equations, obtain the enthalpy potential, and derive local conditions for equatorially centered tori. We then examine the field-free limit, both magnetic components separately, and their superposition. The external field supports closed tori within analytically derived parameter bounds, whereas no such equilibrium is found for the isolated approximate self-field within the numerically explored domain. In a representative combined-field sequence, the radial extent varies nonmonotonically with relative self-field strength: it increases for weak self-fields and decreases as the self-field becomes comparable to the external field. The results show how an approximate magnetic self-field modifies the morphology and domain of existence of charged-fluid tori.
#### A Robust Estimator for the Lyman-$α$ Forest 1D Power Spectrum at High Redshift
 - **Authors:** Molly Wolfson, Joseph F. Hennawi, Frederick B. Davies, Zarija Lukić
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO); Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31626

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.31626

 - **Abstract**
 The Lyman-$\alpha$ (Ly$\alpha$) forest flux power spectrum $P(k)$ is sensitive to a range of astrophysical and cosmological effects, including the thermal state of and large-scale fluctuations in the intergalactic medium and the nature of dark matter. Measuring $P(k)$ becomes increasingly challenging at high redshift, where fewer quasar spectra are available and higher Ly$\alpha$ optical depths result in lower transmitted flux. In addition, observational effects and missing data complicate standard Fourier-transform estimators. Here, we validate a likelihood-based estimator for $P(k)$. The method constructs the pixel-level covariance matrix of the transmitted flux field using precomputed $k$-space integrals that account for instrumental resolution and pixelization, enabling direct maximum-likelihood inference of $P(k)$. We test the estimator using mock Ly$\alpha$ forest spectra at $z \geq 5$, including analytic Gaussian realizations generated from known power spectra and forward-modeled simulation spectra incorporating observational effects relevant to high-redshift quasar surveys, such as finite spectral resolution, pixel sampling, noise, and missing data. We compare the recovered power spectra with both the input spectra and standard Fourier-transform estimates. We find that the likelihood-based estimator recovers the input power spectrum across the tested scales well within expected error and remains robust to the observational effects considered. This work establishes a framework for measuring Ly$\alpha$ forest clustering with a robust, nearly optimal power spectrum estimator, with particular focus on quantifying large-scale fluctuations that may arise from ultraviolet background fluctuations or the late stages of reionization.

#### Alignment-driven binary evolution in warped circumbinary discs: 3D moving-mesh hydrodynamics of eccentric and inclined binaries
 - **Authors:** Lazaros Souvaitzis, Magdalena Siwek, Jorge Cuadra
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE); Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA); Solar and Stellar Astrophysics (astro-ph.SR)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31688

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.31688

 - **Abstract**
 We present three-dimensional moving-mesh hydrodynamical simulations (\arepo{}) of equal-mass binaries in circumbinary discs, spanning binary eccentricity ($e = 0.0,\,0.3,\,0.6$) and inclination ($i = 0^\circ$--$60^\circ$), using a locally isothermal $\alpha$-disc ($H/r = \alpha_{\rm visc} = 0.1$) with fixed Keplerian orbits. Discs above the critical inclination $i_{\rm crit}(e)$, decreasing with $e$, drive their interiors near-polar, so eccentricity alone alters the outcome at fixed $i$. Discs reach polar alignment ten times faster than they align toward the binary plane, settling long before the binary's orbit responds. Alignment dictates how the disc acts on the binary. First, a disc tilting back toward the binary plane spends nearly all its torque reorienting the binary's orbit, whereas a disc driven polar redirects it into the orbit itself, which shrinks 10--20 times faster. Second, at $e = 0.6$ the turn to polar is a single transient reshaping the accretion flow, cavity and orbit, with accretion enhanced ($i = 45^\circ$) or suppressed ($i = 60^\circ$). Third, misalignment suppresses coplanar eccentricity pumping: the attractor at $e \approx 0.4$ survives only below $i \approx 15^\circ$, so misaligned binaries circularise and may enter the gravitational-wave regime less eccentric than coplanar models predict. Around a circular binary, the familiar $\approx 4.5$-orbit accretion modulation vanishes while the disc is strongly warped, returning as it realigns, a time-domain signature of misaligned massive black-hole binaries. Finally, observed polar discs around stellar binaries, such as HD~98800~BaBb's, are likely in their final state; intermediate misalignments reflect slower alignment toward the binary plane.
#### Image Morphology of Misaligned Magnetically Arrested Disks
 - **Authors:** Samuel Feyan, Cora Prather, Dominic O. Chang, Daniel C. M. Palumbo, Koushik Chatterjee, Michael D. Johnson
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31754

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.31754

 - **Abstract**
 Horizon scale images probe the plasma dynamics and spacetime in the vicinity of accreting black holes. Interpreting these images requires models that connect image morphology to the structure of the underlying accretion flow. Disk misalignment is expected in many systems, yet most horizon scale image analyses assume that the emitting flow is aligned with the black hole spin. We investigate whether tilted accretion flows produce image features distinguishable from axisymmetric flows and whether their effects can be absorbed by changes in aligned model parameters. Using general relativistic magnetohydrodynamic simulations of tilted magnetically arrested disks spanning black hole spins $a^*=-0.9375$ to $0.9375$ and initial disk tilts $0^\circ$ to $30^\circ$, we generate synthetic images and quantify their morphology through fits to an aligned, semi-analytic emission model. Using this approach, we show that high spin prograde models show little sensitivity to disk tilt, as the inner emitting flow aligns with the black hole spin. In contrast, retrograde tilted disks develop warped, non-axisymmetric inner structure that distorts the inner shadow and strongly alters image morphology. While the aligned semi-analytic model reproduces most prograde images well, retrograde tilted disk images are fit less accurately and yield biased inferred parameters, especially for spin. The image morphologies indicate that tilt affects horizon scale images only when misalignment survives into the near horizon emitting region, motivating image models that include disk tilt as an explicit degree of freedom when alignment is uncertain.
#### Semi-Analytic Predictions for Strong-Lensing Perturbers in Cold and Self-Interacting Dark Matter
 - **Authors:** Jonathan Tekverk, Duncan K. Adams, Simon Birrer, Rouven Essig, Daniel Gilman
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO); High Energy Physics - Phenomenology (hep-ph)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31881

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.31881

 - **Abstract**
 Strong gravitational lensing is sensitive to the abundance and internal structure of dark matter halos, providing a probe of gravothermal evolution in self-interacting dark matter (SIDM). We combine semi-analytic cold dark matter (CDM) subhalo histories with a model for gravothermal evolution in SIDM to generate CDM and SIDM subhalo populations across SIDM parameter space. We predict their projected enclosed masses and deflection-based projected density slopes and compare them with three perturbers inferred through gravitational imaging in PJ011646, SDSS J0946+1006, and JVAS B1938+666. For SIDM model parameters $\sigma_0/m_\chi=147.1$ cm$^2$/g and $w=120$ km/s, SIDM subhalos reproduce all three perturbers more often than CDM subhalos. For PJ011646, we find ~0.6 cored SIDM subhalos per host matching the perturber, compared with ~0.002 in CDM, while core-collapsed subhalos are too steep. For SDSS J0946+1006, we find ~3 core-collapsed and ~0.004 cored SIDM subhalos per host, compared with none in 900 CDM realizations, although baryons, neglected here, may matter for such massive subhalos. For "detection V" in JVAS B1938+666, we find ~220 core-collapsed and ~20 cored SIDM subhalos per host within the $2\sigma$ posterior region, compared with ~0.2 in CDM; the CDM candidates require exceptionally early accretion, at median redshift $z\sim13$. Simultaneously reproducing all three perturbers requires $w\gtrsim10^2$ km/s, so that the massive (~$5.6\times10^{10}M_\odot$) progenitors of SDSS J0946+1006 candidates can collapse, and is achieved for $\sigma_0/m_\chi\gtrsim10^2$ cm$^2$/g in the parameter sets studied. Our results show how semi-analytic models can quantify how often subhalos reproduce individual perturbers in different dark matter models, and our modular framework can be readily updated as models of tidal stripping and late-stage gravothermal evolution in SIDM improve. #### Probing Red Giant Interiors with g-Dominated Mixed Modes II: The Cases of KIC 6117517, KIC 9475697, KIC 10123207 and KIC 10200377
 - **Authors:** Xinyi Zhang, Tanda Li, Shaolan Bi
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Solar and Stellar Astrophysics (astro-ph.SR)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32357

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.32357

 - **Abstract**
 Building on Paper~I, which constrained core overshooting to $f_{\rm ov} < 0.01$ for $M < 1.4\,M_{\odot}$ and found $\alpha_{\rm MLT} \simeq 2.0$ for metal-rich red giants, we extend this analysis to four lower-mass \textit{Kepler} red giants spanning a broad metallicity range: KIC~6117517, KIC~9475697, KIC~10123207, and KIC~10200377. Two complementary model grids are constructed: Grid~A fixes $\alpha_{\rm MLT} = 2.0$ while varying metallicity; Grid~B adopts spectroscopic metallicities with $\alpha_{\rm MLT}$ as a free parameter. Grid~A successfully reproduces both asteroseismic and spectroscopic constraints for the metal-rich targets (KIC~6117517 and KIC~9475697), confirming $\alpha_{\rm MLT} = 2.0$ for metal-rich red giants. For the metal-poor targets (KIC~10123207 and KIC~10200377), Grid~B yields larger mixing-length parameters of $\alpha_{\rm MLT} \approx 2.16$--$2.18$. Although only four stars are analyzed with unequal sensitivity to $\alpha_{\rm MLT}$, these results suggest a tentative increase in convective efficiency toward lower metallicity, consistent with 3D hydrodynamical simulations but opposite to some 1D empirical calibrations. The best-fitting overshooting parameters ($f_{\rm ov} = 0.006$--$0.007$) independently support the Paper~I constraint and are consistent with a mass-dependent overshooting relation. The parameter uncertainties reach $0.6$--$2.5\%$ in mass, $0.2$--$0.9\%$ in radius, $2$--$13\%$ in age, and below $0.6\%$ in helium-core mass and below $0.2\%$ in helium-core radius. Surface-effect corrections are negligible for g-dominated mixed modes, validating $\chi^2_{l=1g}$ as a robust fitting metric. For KIC~9475697, the core rotation rate is determined to be $\Omega_{\rm core} = 0.6945 \pm 0.0190\,\mu$Hz from model-based trapping parameters.
#### Introducing the GION Lightcone Simulation Suite for Multi-Wavelength Large-Scale Structure Cosmology
 - **Authors:** Ken Osato, Satoshi Tanaka, Tomoaki Ishiyama, Hironao Miyatake, Takahiro Nishimichi, Masato Shirasaki
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32388

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.32388

 - **Abstract**
 We present the GION (Ginkaku Lightcone Multi-wavelength Simulation Project) simulation suite: 10 full-sky high-resolution cosmological lightcone simulations and their associated products for multi-wavelength large-scale structure cosmology. We use the TreePM $N$-body code GINKAKU, which is highly optimized in the massively parallel environment, and integrate the on-the-fly lightcone construction technique. Each lightcone simulation is run with $6144^3$ particles in a volume of $(1.5 \, h^{-1} \, \mathrm{Gpc})^3$, which corresponds to a particle mass of $1.28 \times 10^9 \, h^{-1} \, \mathrm{M}_\odot$, and extends to $z = 5$. The fine mass resolution and the depth of the lightcone simulations allow us to resolve dark matter halos down to $10^{11} \, h^{-1} \, \mathrm{M}_\odot$ in the high-redshift Universe, which is crucial for applications to the Stage-IV cosmological surveys. The fundamental statistics of the simulations, including the matter power spectrum, halo power spectrum, and halo mass function, are validated against theoretical predictions. We also generate various large-scale structure observables from the lightcone outputs: the dark matter halo catalogs, mock galaxy samples, weak lensing convergence maps, and thermal and kinematic Sunyaev-Zel'dovich (tSZ and kSZ) maps. The angular power spectra of these observables, including the cross-correlation between weak lensing and the tSZ effect, are in good agreement with theoretical predictions, demonstrating that the suite provides mutually consistent multi-wavelength observables on the same lightcone. The GION simulation suite will be publicly available and can be used for various cosmological analyses to address the observational systematic effects and validate theoretical models.
#### Local Analogs of Little Red Dots: Clues to the X-ray Weakness
 - **Authors:** Shi-Jiang Chen, Jun-Xian Wang, Zhen-Ya Zheng, Ruqiu Lin, Luis C. Ho, Zhen-Yi Cai, Chang-Hao Chen, Zsofi Igo, Jia-Lai Kang, Ruancun Li, Teng Liu, Wenke Ren, Zhen-Bo Su
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA); Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO); High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32430

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.32430

 - **Abstract**
 A major question about JWST's little red dots (LRDs) at $z\sim4-8$ is the origin of their apparent X-ray weakness. Even deep Chandra stacks yield only tentative detections or broad-band limits and cannot distinguish among super-Eddington accretion, intrinsic X-ray weakness, and Compton-thick (CT) obscuration. Local analogs offer a powerful complementary route. At $z\sim0.3$, similarly luminous sources require $\sim10^{3-4}$ less exposure for comparable signal-to-noise, suffer less source confusion, and directly probe the diagnostic rest-frame soft X-rays. Here we present a pilot Einstein Probe/FXT study of six "local little red dots" (LLRDs), V-shaped broad-line green pea galaxies selected as low-redshift counterparts of LRDs. These sources show X-ray diversity: "X-normal" LLRDs resemble type 1 AGNs in X-ray luminosity and optical variability, whereas the "X-weak" stack lies $\sim0.5-0.7$ dex below standard unobscured-quasar scaling relations while remaining compatible with the representative LRD stack and 95% of published individual limits. The X-weak LLRDs also lack detected optical variability and have redder optical continua than X-normal LLRDs, making their V-shaped SEDs closer to those of high-redshift LRDs. The detection of the X-weak stack at $\sim3.3\sigma$ with the soft-X-ray-sensitive EP-FXT provides rest-frame $\sim2-3$ keV constraints difficult to reconcile with a typical unobscured super-Eddington model or a local-AGN-like CT geometry examined here, although modified variants remain possible. The simplest alternative is a mildly X-ray-weak corona ($\Delta\alpha_{\mathrm{ox}}\sim-0.1$ to $-0.2$) with Compton-thin absorption ($N_{\mathrm{H}}\sim10^{22-23}$ cm$^{-2}$), consistent with the independent Balmer decrement/break diagnostics. Together with growing samples of local LRD analogs, our results offer a practical path for testing the physical nature of LRD X-ray weakness.
#### Star clusters at cosmic dawn I: the emergence of the initial cluster mass function
 - **Authors:** Fred S. Thompson, Julien Devriendt, Adrianne Slyz, Taysun Kimm, Cheonsu Kang
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32497

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.32497

 - **Abstract**
 JWST observations of lensed galaxies reveal unusually bright star-cluster candidates within the first billion years after the Big Bang. However, the physical processes shaping the initial cluster mass function (ICMF) remain uncertain. We study the formation of a galaxy embedded in a dark matter halo whose virial mass reaches $10^{10} \mathrm{M}_\odot$ at $z=6$, using cosmological radiation-hydrodynamics zoom-in simulations. Its star clusters, represented by sink particles, accrete gas and form stellar populations that supply radiation and supernova feedback, thereby regulating their own growth. As we do not prescribe an efficiency per free-fall time or a fixed star-cluster formation duration, the ICMF emerges self-consistently. We assess the effects of numerical resolution and stellar radiation. Schechter fits to the central-galaxy ICMF give truncation masses near $6 \times 10^6 \mathrm{M}_\odot$, with substantial uncertainty. At fixed resolution, including stellar radiation makes the low-mass slope of the ICMF shallower, from $-1.60$ to $-1.34$, while doubling the spatial resolution steepens it back to $-1.54$. Stellar radiation also reduces the abundance of low-mass clouds, particularly those with lower gas densities, and shortens the formation time of star clusters to about a Myr, i.e. well before their first supernovae go off. On average, more rapidly fed star clusters accrete for longer, making their final masses rise faster than linearly with the initial accretion rate and contributing to a flatter ICMF. At fixed host-galaxy star formation rate, our brightest simulated clusters reach UV luminosities comparable to those of observed lensed star-cluster candidates.
#### Magnetic Reconnection Modifies Cosmic-Ray Transport in the Interstellar Medium
 - **Authors:** Antoine Baldacchino-Jordan, Michal Hanasz, Vinod Pisharody, Dominik Wóltański, Artur Gawryszczak
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE); Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32716

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.32716

 - **Abstract**
 We present numerical magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) simulations of the interstellar medium in gravity-stratified boxes with the Piernik MHD code. The system contains thermal gas, spectrally resolved cosmic-ray (CR) nuclei within the CRESP algorithm, and spectrally unresolved CR protons. CR protons drive gas outflows and undergo Coulomb and hadronic losses. CR nuclei undergo Coulomb losses. We also include the spallation of primary Carbon by thermal gas atoms, thereby decaying Car- bon into secondary stable Boron isotopes. We use the resistive-MHD approximation as a subgrid model for unresolved turbulent magnetic dissipation in current sheets. We perform a parameter study of the magnetic diffusivity {\eta}. The Boron-to-Carbon ratio B/C and hadronic {\gamma}-ray luminosity density are computed as diagnostics. We find that non-zero magnetic diffusivity modifies the magnetic topology, leading to a more horizontal field in the halo, thereby accumulating CRs in the disk region and signifi- cantly increasing the CR escape time. As a consequence, the local turbulent conditions and turbulent reconnection in the interstellar medium should be considered when interpreting B/C and hadronic {\gamma}-ray luminosity observed at Earth. Our results suggest that the escape time and diffusion coefficient of CRs deduced from observational data in the vicinity of the Solar System cannot be extrapolated to the whole Galaxy.
#### Probing the star-forming environment of GRB across cosmic ages with high-resolution X-ray spectroscopy: From \textit{XMM-Newton}/RGS and \textit{XRISM}/Resolve to \textit{NewAthena}/X-IFU
 - **Authors:** Luigi Piro, Aishwarya Linesh Thakur, Alfredo Luminari, Fabrizio Nicastro, Yair Krongold
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32786

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.32786

 - **Abstract**
 Optical and infrared GRB absorption spectroscopy provides valuable insight into host-galaxy properties across cosmic time. However, the star-forming region embedding the progenitor, on scales $\lesssim 100~\mathrm{pc}$ is heavily ionized by the GRB emission, making X-ray spectroscopy the sole probe of its physical and chemical conditions. We characterize the GRB-embedding medium as a function of progenitor type and cosmic epoch via high-resolution X-ray spectroscopy. We extend our study to high-redshift GRBs to evaluate how effectively X-ray absorption signatures can distinguish between pristine, Pop~III-, and Pop~II-enriched environments. Using the time-dependent photoionization code \texttt{TEPID}, we self-consistently calculate the ionization structure of the absorber, the energies and the strengths of the resulting absorption lines. We analyze a flux-selected sample of GRBs showing strong evidence for time-dependent photoionization, and search for predicted narrow features in the RGS spectra. We also assess the performance of \textit{XRISM}/Resolve and \textit{NewAthena}/X-IFU in detecting these features. In the single archival case with sufficient RGS signal-to-noise ratio (GRB~090618 at $z=0.54$), we find $\approx 3\sigma$ combined evidence for narrow, highly ionized Fe~L absorption lines. For bright nearby GRBs, \textit{XRISM}/Resolve can detect Fe~K absorption lines even in its current gate-valve-closed configuration ($\sim 3$ expected events/yr). \textit{NewAthena}/X-IFU will enable systematic population studies of GRB environments, delivering redshifts with precision comparable to optical/IR spectroscopy, unaffected by dust obscuration and Ly-$\alpha$ forest absorption. Crucially, it will precisely diagnose the density, size, metallicity, and abundance patterns, ultimately revealing whether high-$z$ GRBs originate in pristine, Pop~III-, or Pop~II-enriched media.
#### A Population Synthesis Model for Odd Radio Circles Produced by Shock-Bubble Interactions
 - **Authors:** Yiting Wang, Sebastian Heinz, Alexander Tchekhovskoy
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA); High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32916

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.32916

 - **Abstract**
 Odd Radio Circles (ORCs) are edge-brightened rings of diffuse radio emission of roughly arcminute size whose physical origin remains debated. One proposed mechanism is vortex formation through Shock-Bubble interaction (SBI), in which a merger or accretion shock re-energizes a fossil radio lobe into an edge-brightened ring. We test whether this mechanism can reproduce the observed ORC abundance using a Monte Carlo population-synthesis model that combines the galaxy stellar mass function, the radio-AGN incidence relation, dark-matter halo merger histories, and the inflation and buoyant survival of AGN-inflated bubbles, with synthetic radio fluxes and ring geometries drawn from a grid of magnetohydrodynamic SBI simulations. Applying survey-specific selection to the ASKAP-EMU Pilot Survey (944 MHz) and the LOFAR Two-metre Sky Survey (LoTSS; 144 MHz), we predict $2.7^{+3.9}_{-1.6}$ detectable ORCs in the EMU Pilot, matching the three found there, and $268^{+331}_{-148}$ in LoTSS, of which $7.5^{+9.5}_{-4.2}$ have a counterpart at 1.4 GHz. The predicted hosts are mostly central galaxies in group-scale halos. The predicted rings are physically smaller and lie at lower redshifts than the galaxies identified as hosts of the known ORCs, suggesting that SBI produces mainly the nearby part of the ORC population, or that some ORCs are associated with the wrong host. The population synthesis model also predicts a large population of low surface brightness steep-spectrum ORCs in the LoTSS band, implying a large hidden population and predicting that deeper low-frequency surveys should uncover many more ORCs than are currently known. These results indicate that shock-bubble interactions can account for at least part of the observed incidence of ORCs.

#### Hidden Disruption in Milky Way-like Satellite Galaxy Populations
 - **Authors:** Gray Thoron, Nora Shipp, Alexander H. Riley, Arpit Arora, Christine M. Simpson, Rebekka Bieri, Peter S. Ferguson, Jaclyn Jensen, Facundo A. Gómez, Robert J. J. Grand, Rüdiger Pakmor, Freeke van de Voort
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22481

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.22481

 - **Abstract**
 Determining whether a satellite galaxy is tidally disrupting from photometric observations alone remains a fundamental challenge in near-field cosmology. Cosmological simulations of Milky Way-mass galaxies predict that satellite populations should be highly disrupted, yet the observed Milky Way satellite population appears largely intact. Understanding this tension requires either uncovering disruption that has gone undetected in Milky Way satellites, or identifying limitations in the simulations themselves. We use the Auriga suite of cosmological simulations to evaluate several commonly used indicators of disruption. We find that, in the simulated satellite population, the shape, size, and proper motion alignment of a satellite's bound component are unreliable indicators of its present-day disruption state despite their widespread use in the literature. In contrast, the extended stellar distribution, specifically the ratio of stellar mass between $3r_{1/2}$ and $10r_{1/2}$ to that within $r_{1/2}$, is strongly correlated with disruption. We introduce the extended mass ratio $\mu_{\rm EMR}$ as a metric for comparing extended stellar distributions across satellites of varying masses and exploring their connection to disruption in Milky Way observations. Applying this metric to a sample of Milky Way satellites with measured extended stellar distributions, we find that many are consistent with significant ongoing disruption, even among systems traditionally classified as intact. These results suggest that tidal disruption may be substantially more widespread in the observed Milky Way satellite population than currently recognized, and establish extended stellar distributions as a promising new tool for uncovering this hidden disruption.
#### The bar-driven central complex of NGC 6946: A panchromatic and spatially resolved view of its star formation history and interstellar medium
 - **Authors:** Santiago Álvarez-Córdoba, Jacopo Fritz, M. A. Lara-López, Bernardo Cervantes Sodi, L. S. Pilyugin, Viviana Casasola, Sami Dib, Omar López-Cruz, L. E. Garduño, G. Valé, I. A. Zinchenko, M. E. De Rossi, M. M. Puebla, Vidhi Tailor, Gustavo Bruzual, J. Zaragoza-Cardiel, Margarita Rosado
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22483

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.22483

 - **Abstract**
 We present a multi-wavelength analysis of the nearby spiral galaxy NGC 6946, combining optical IFS, infrared, and radio observations. We aim to characterise both the integrated and spatially resolved properties of the stellar populations and ISM, and to interpret them in the context of bar-driven galaxy evolution. We used the SED fitting code SINOPSIS to derive stellar population properties, and different prescriptions to derive ISM properties including atomic and molecular gas masses and the dust mass,temperature, and emissivity index. We find that the central 2.6 kpc hosts $40\%$ of the stellar mass, as well as the bulk of the molecular gas and dust, and has sustained enhanced star formation over cosmic time. A major star formation episode occurred 0.6 Gyr ago. The spatially resolved KS relation shows that molecular gas alone explains only part of the current SFR variance, while alternative prescriptions incorporating stellar mass and dynamical equilibrium pressure yield only modest improvements. We also find radial enhancements in the effective oxygen yield and gas mass fraction relative to surrounding regions, consistent with sustained gas accumulation. Together with the molecular gas streaming motions previously detected kinematic studies, our results are consistent with, and provide independent support for, the inward transport of gas driven by a system of nested bars and with a scenario in which a recent episode of bar-driven gas inflow enhanced the central molecular gas reservoir and sustained elevated star formation. These findings point to efficient HI-to-H$_2$ conversion, likely promoted by the combined effect of the secondary bar and the large dust content, ultimately regulating star formation. We also find indications consistent with a young stellar bar. The current decline in star formation despite large gas reservoirs suggests recent bar-driven quenching.
#### Combining Rubin and Roman photometry and its implications for photometric redshift estimation
 - **Authors:** Tae-hyeon Shin, Rachel Mandelbaum, Chun-Hao To, Axel Guinot, Andrew Hearin, Arun Kannawadi, Chihway Chang, Federico Berlfein, Kaili Cao, Nihar Dalal, Michael Gabe, Christopher Hirata, Katherine Laliotis, Sidney Mau, Michael A. Troxel, Masaya Yamamoto, Roman HLIS Cosmology Project Infrastructure Team
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Instrumentation and Methods for Astrophysics (astro-ph.IM)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22547

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.22547

 - **Abstract**
 We perform a joint Roman--Rubin photometry analysis using the LSST Science Pipelines (LSP) on simulated images, by using the Roman F158 band as the reference band and applying forced photometry on the other bands. We find that the LSP CModel photometry for Roman bands is on average unbiased, but with a bright-end systematic uncertainty floor of 7-8%. The pgauss photometry as part of the Metadetection weak-lensing shape measurement pipeline also exhibits a similar floor despite a substantially different flux-bias behavior. However, this systematic uncertainty floor becomes negligible for the color residuals and both algorithms recover unbiased colors for Roman as well as Rubin. We also show that not accounting for correlated pixel noise in Roman IMCOM coadded images results in underestimation of flux errors by a factor of ~4. Based on the measured photometry, we build a machine-learning algorithm that maps the true galaxy properties to empirical flux errors, providing a convenient method to reproduce realistic photometry errors without having to rerun expensive image simulations and photometry pipelines. We apply this empirical error model to a new mock galaxy catalog with improved realism to investigate the impact of different survey depths and configurations on photometric redshift (photo-z) estimation performance. We find that 1) Rubin survey depth significantly impacts the photo-$z$ precision, 2) additional systematic uncertainties on photometry could significantly degrade the photo-z performance especially towards high redshifts, therefore eroding the Roman--Rubin synergy, and 3) Roman photometry becomes important above z~1.5 while adding Rubin photometry to that of Roman improves photo-z performance across all redshifts. Our results provide a foundation for understanding how realistic uncertainties in galaxy photometry may propagate into the Roman--Rubin synergy in photo-z analyses.

 - **Subjects:** Subjects:

 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22598

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.22598

 - **Abstract**
 The interstellar medium (ISM) is magnetized at all scales and phases in our Galaxy and other galaxies across the Universe. Spectro-polarimetric millimetre observations are essential to probe the role of the interstellar magnetic fields in the dynamics of the cold universe, and how this affects the star formation process, and planet formation in nearby star forming regions. Such observations are also essential for understanding magnetic fields in active stars and the shaping and evolution of the circumstellar environments of evolved stars. While ALMA has been revolutionary in advancing our knowledge about magnetic fields in the ISM, the limitations in calibration, especially for circular polarization, and sensitivity for large samples, have hindered robust measurements of field strengths, which are essential for fully assessing their role in ISM dynamics. An ALMA2040 that broadens spectro-polarimetric capabilities to include robust circular calibration and increases sensitivity with more collecting area will enable crucial observations of magnetic fields across the Universe and over a wide range of spatial scales to fully ascertain the influences of magnetic fields from galaxy assembly to forming planets.
#### Dust in Galaxies at $z=10-15$: Attenuation and Re-emission Predictions from the FirstLight Simulations
 - **Authors:** Yurina Nakazato, Akio K. Inoue, Rachel S. Somerville, Daisuke Toyouchi, Kosei Matsumoto, Daniel Ceverino
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22766

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.22766

 - **Abstract**
 Dust masses and grain properties in $z>10$ galaxies remain poorly constrained, as JWST UV attenuation and ALMA dust re-emission are rarely modelled consistently. We perform post-processed 3D dust radiative transfer on 208 snapshots of 99 galaxies at $z=10$-15 from the FirstLight simulations ($M_*=10^{6.5}$-$10^{9.5}\,M_\odot$), applying twelve dust models per snapshot (2496 runs): four dust-to-metal (DTM) prescriptions and three grain size distributions. Metallicity-dependent DTM models reproduce ALMA dust-mass upper limits at $z\gtrsim10$ and detections at $z\sim7$, as dust-to-stellar mass ratios rise sharply near $Z\sim0.2\,Z_\odot$, typically at $M_*\gtrsim10^{8.5}\,M_\odot$, where grain growth by metal accretion becomes dominant. A constant DTM of 0.4 instead violates more than half of the upper limits. For dust attenuation, SMC-like dust is favored by the observed $M_{\rm UV}$-$\beta_{\rm UV}$ relation. The observed UV slopes ($\beta_{\rm UV}\simeq-2.3$) favor small grains ($a\lesssim\lambda_{\rm UV}/2\pi\simeq0.025\,\mu{\rm m}$) producing steep UV extinction, together with a low abundance of carbonaceous grains or PAHs responsible for the 2175 $\mathrm{\mathring{A}}$ bump, consistent with silicate-rich dust. Large-grain stellar dust ($a_0=0.1\,\mu{\rm m}$) instead yields nearly grey extinction and UV slopes close to the intrinsic value ($\beta_{\rm UV,0}\simeq-2.6$). SMC and MW attenuation curves flatten at $A_{\rm V}\gtrsim0.3$, showing that relatively flat attenuation does not necessarily imply large grains. For dust re-emission, dust peak temperatures reach 60-80 K, exceeding the 40-50 K commonly assumed in ALMA dust-mass estimates. Predicted IR continuum flux increases with UV luminosity; at current ALMA sensitivities (10-20 $\mu$Jy), detections generally require $M_{\rm UV}\lesssim-21.5$. Roman wide-field surveys may identify such rare UV-bright galaxies.
#### How bright can a galaxy be? Extreme value predictions for high-redshift survey observables
 - **Authors:** Christopher C. Lovell, Theodore Martin, Sandro Tacchella, Christian K. Jespersen, Ian Harrison
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA); Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23016

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.23016

 - **Abstract**
 Assessing the significance of extreme galaxy candidates has typically relied on redshifts and stellar / halo masses inferred through inverse modelling, which carries substantial systematic uncertainty. We combine the halo mass function with a conditional model for galaxy emission and apply the Extreme Value Statistics (EVS) formalism to predict the distribution of the observed flux of the brightest galaxy expected in a given survey area, redshift interval and photometric band. We find the choice of parametric star formation history (SFH) dominates the modelling uncertainty, introducing a spread approaching $\sim 1 \, \mathrm{dex}$ by $z \sim 18$, whilst the stellar population synthesis model, initial mass function and metallicity each contribute $\lesssim 0.3 \, \mathrm{dex}$. The luminous $z \gtrsim 9$ candidates in COSMOS-Web cluster about the median prediction for the area they were drawn from, and lie at least an order of magnitude below the $3\sigma$ limit, a margin comparable to the SFH systematic. Of the observational samples reaching beyond $z \sim 13$, the two spectroscopically confirmed galaxies at $z \simeq 14$ lie close to the median prediction for their area, whereas photometric candidates at $z \gtrsim 16$ reach $+4\sigma$ (a probability to exceed of $\sim 10^{-5}$), the brightest lying at the limit obtained by converting all halo baryons into stars. Since the prediction bounds the flux far more tightly than the photometry bounds the redshift, such offsets are most naturally resolved by errors in the inferred redshifts. This approach allows extreme candidates to be assessed directly in the observational plane, making explicit the modelling assumptions common to forward and inverse approaches.
#### Galaxy bias of radio AGN and star-forming galaxies from MIGHTEE and CMB lensing
 - **Authors:** Joel Hamlett, Catherine L. Hale, Matt J. Jarvis, David Alonso, S. Lyla Jung, Natalia Stylianou, Rohan G. Varadaraj
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO); Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23567

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.23567

 - **Abstract**
 Radio galaxies play a significant role in galaxy evolution through feedback mechanisms, making it important to characterize the environments in which they reside. We measure the cross-correlation between the positions of radio sources in the MIGHTEE survey and cosmic microwave background lensing measurements from the Atacama Cosmology Telescope over $20\ {\rm deg}^2$ to flux densities $S_{\rm 1.4\,GHz}>40\ {\rm \mu Jy}$, detecting the signal at $\sim4\sigma$ significance. By combining this with auto-correlation measurements of low- and high-redshift radio samples, we constrain the redshift evolution of the galaxy bias parameter, $b(z)$. Fitting different models for the redshift evolution of the bias, we find that both a model inversely proportional to the linear growth factor, $b(z) = (1.63^{+0.19}_{-0.22})/D(z)$, and a model where bias grows linearly with redshift, $b(z) = (0.49^{+0.44}_{-0.32}) + (1.62^{+0.37}_{-0.51})z$, provide comparably good fits to the data, with the current measurements unable to distinguish between them. Using a radio luminosity function, we estimate the fraction of sources that are active galactic nuclei (AGNs) or star-forming galaxies (SFGs) as a function of redshift, and model the individual contributions of AGNs and SFGs to the total bias. This approach allows us to measure the bias of AGNs and SFGs without individual source classifications. Though no single model is favoured significantly, the best-fitting model gives $b_{\rm AGN}(z) = (2.60^{+0.65}_{-0.91})/D(z)$ compared to $b_{\rm SFG}(z) = (0.52^{+0.47}_{-0.34})/D(z)$. Incorporating SFG bias measurements from previous work as a prior tightens these constraints to $b_{\rm AGN}(z) = (1.96^{+0.37}_{-0.40})/D(z)$ and $b_{\rm SFG}(z) = (0.90^{+0.06}_{-0.06})/D(z)$.

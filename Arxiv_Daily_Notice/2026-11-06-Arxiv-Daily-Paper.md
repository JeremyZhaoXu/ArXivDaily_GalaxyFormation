#### The box within the Universe: anisotropies in periodic cosmological simulations I. Dark-matter-only simulations
 - **Authors:** Gábor Rácz , Till Sawala
 - **Subjects:** Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08223
 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.08223
 - **Abstract**
 The vast majority of cosmological simulations employ periodic boundary conditions to achieve statistical homogeneity and to mimic the infinite spatial extent of our Universe. In a cubic periodic domain the Green function breaks full rotational invariance and introduces a geometry-induced anisotropy in the gravitational interaction. We quantify how this intrinsic anisotropy affects cosmic structure growth and investigate the extent to which it biases the commonly used large-scale structure and halo-based observables in dark-matter-only simulations. We analyse an ensemble of 498 cosmological dark-matter-only $N$-body simulations in cubic $T^3$ topological manifolds, evolved to $z=0$, spanning box sizes from $L=6.25\,\mathrm{Mpc}$ to $L=100\,\mathrm{Mpc}$ at fixed mass and force resolution. We compare anisotropy-sensitive statistics across the suite, including the matter power spectrum, halo abundance, merger histories, shapes and alignments, and filament orientations. Finite periodic boundaries imprint orientation-dependent signatures aligned with the box geometry. These signatures propagate beyond the largest modes and can induce systematic shifts in both field-level and halo-level statistics. The detectable effects propagate to scales much smaller than the box size. Our results demonstrate that periodic-box anisotropy is a non-negligible source of systematics for small simulation volumes, including those commonly employed in modern hydrodynamical simulations.
#### Octavius: A High-Performance Analysis Toolkit for Galaxy Simulations
 - **Authors:** Jean-Paul Duminy , Jakub Szpila , Romeel Davé
 - **Subjects:** Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA) ; Instrumentation and Methods for Astrophysics (astro-ph.IM)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08115
 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.08115
 - **Abstract**
 Increased computing power and more efficient codes have allowed cosmological simulations of galaxy formation to grow massively in scale: the analysis of their outputs has consequently become a big data computational challenge. We present Octavius, a high-performance, hybrid-parallel simulation analysis toolkit. Octavius ingests a simulation snapshot with associated halo catalogue, locates galaxies with a 6D friends-of-friends algorithm, then produces an HDF5 catalogue of properties for haloes, subhaloes, and galaxies. The analysis is fast and memory efficient: we compare the performance against the widely used predecessor toolkit, Caesar, and show Octavius delivers order-of-magnitude improvements in speed with a factor of >5 reduction in peak memory. We demonstrate near-linear strong scaling of compute and linear peak memory scaling with the number of halo-assigned particles. Compute times scale with the superlinear computational load of larger haloes; we note the pipeline is presently I/O-limited. The code is written in Python and uses a declarative, plugin-based architecture so as to be easily extensible. Octavius can currently analyse SIMBA, SWIFT-KIARA, SWIFT-EAGLE, SWIFT-COLIBRE, and TNG snapshots; AHF, HBT-HERONS, and SUBFIND halo catalogues are supported. Properties computed include masses, galaxy photometry with dust attenuation, and kinematic, radial, morphological, and virial quantities. Output catalogues are easily accessed through a provided user API. The code is open sourced at https://github.com/jp-duminy/octavius
#### Violent relaxation in hierarchical stellar systems: pathways toward massive black hole formation in the early Universe
 - **Authors:** Dominik R.G. Schleicher , Francesco Flammini Dotti , Matías Liempi , Tommaso Zana , Andrés Escala , Muhammad A. Latif , Roberto Capuzzo-Dolcetta , Marcelo C. Vergara , Lorenzo Paparella
 - **Subjects:** Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07546
 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.07546
 - **Abstract**
 Massive stellar systems have been detected in the high-redshift Universe via the James Webb Space Telescope using gravitational lensing techniques. Such stellar systems may evolve on short dynamical timescales, despite a formally large 2-body relaxation time. If the stellar system consists of a large number of gravitationally bound clusters, a 2-body relaxation time can be defined for the star clusters in analogy to the 2-body relaxation time for stars, implying a faster evolution determined by the number of stars per cluster. At their formation, clusters will go through a process of "cold collapse" as part of their early, non-collisional, evolution. This phase enhances the mass segregation between the more massive and less massive population of stars. We derive a criteria based on the mass of the two components that determines under which conditions the massive component may even decouple from the cluster itself. Mergers of clusters and multiple mergers on average tend to produce systems with larger cluster radii. For the formation of massive black holes via dense stellar systems, the most promising pathway consists in the cold collapse of a dense massive cluster, after few mergers (or possibly none). In such a cluster with a sufficient number of massive stars, the massive population of stars will dynamically decouple in the central region and go through core collapse, leading to the formation of a central massive object. In the case of multiple mergers, massive but more spatially extended stellar systems will form.
#### Confirming HSC strong lens candidates with DESI Spectroscopy. II. Analysis of 80 galaxy-scale systems with lens redshifts above 0.8
 - **Authors:** Shen Li , Yiping Shu , Lin Nie
 - **Subjects:** Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO) ; Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07524
 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.07524
 - **Abstract**
 The total mass distribution of galaxies provides a sensitive probe of the fundamental physical processes governing galaxy evolution, including gas inflow/star formation, AGN/SN feedback, galaxy merging, and the nature of dark matter. State-of-the-art cosmological simulations have therefore made extensive predictions for the evolution of the total-mass density slope. Interestingly, their predictions diverge significantly at high redshift, primarily due to differences in how these key physical mechanisms are implemented. However, measurements of the total-mass structure evolution have been largely confined to low and intermediate redshifts, leaving the regime where model predictions differ most significantly (z>0.8) largely unexplored. In the first paper of this series, we reported the discovery of 84 candidate strong-lens systems with lens redshifts above 0.8. In this work, we perform lens modeling of the 80 galaxy-scale systems to assess their lensing nature. Of these, 71 systems (approximately 90%) exhibit clear lensing features that are well reproduced by our lens models and are therefore classified as secure strong lenses. Another eight systems are classified as probable (5) or possible (3) strong lenses, based on the prominence of their lensing features and the quality of the lens modeling. One system lacking reliable lensing features in current imaging data is classified as a non-lens. Our analysis nearly triples the number of known galaxy-scale strong lenses at z_lens > 0.8, making statistical studies of the total mass structure of high-redshift galaxies possible for the first time. This expanded sample provides an unprecedented opportunity to test the predictions of cosmological simulations and discriminate between competing physical prescriptions.
#### Double Neutron Star Mergers and Fast Blue Optical Transients: A Circumbinary Disk Connection
 - **Authors:** Aldana Grichener , Ruggero Valli , Mathieu Renzo , Ilya Mandel , Kaitlin M. Kratter , Magdalena Siwek , Koushik Sen , Neev Shah
 - **Subjects:** High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE) ; Solar and Stellar Astrophysics (astro-ph.SR)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07326
 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.07326
 - **Abstract**
 Post-common-envelope evolution (CEE) circumbinary disks (CBDs) form when part of the ejected envelope remains bound and circularizes around the stripped core and its companion. Leveraging results of scale-free hydrodynamical simulations, we develop a framework to model the effect of such disks on subsequent binary evolution, and implement it in the population synthesis code COMPAS. Applying our framework to double neutron star (DNS) merger progenitors, we find that CBDs generally induce substantial orbital shrinkage, changing the merging DNS population and lowering the overall merger rate. We explore an extensive grid of binary-evolution and disk models, and find that CBD interactions reduce the DNS merger rate by up to $28\%$, primarily through 'premature' mergers between the neutron star (NS) and the giant's core. These mergers may power luminous fast blue optical transients (LFBOTs), occurring at a fiducial rate of $\simeq11 \:\rm Gpc^{-3} \rm yr^{-1}$. Our results indicate that natal kicks contribute little to LFBOT offsets, and that they should be associated with young stellar populations, though not necessarily active star-forming regions. Since LFBOT progenitors would otherwise produce short-delay DNS mergers, CBDs further challenge DNS mergers as the source of early r-process enrichment. For surviving DNS mergers, accretion from the disk leads to higher chirp masses, potentially explaining the puzzling mass of GW190425. Our framework provides a general tool for studying CBD-binary interactions in post-CEE systems.
#### Dust Survival in Lensed Quiescent Galaxies at $z>1$ Depends on How They Quench
 - **Authors:** Sarah Bodansky , Katherine E. Whitaker , Gourav Khullar , Michael D. Gladders , Keren Sharon , Viraj Manwadkar , Yunchong Zhang , Jacqueline Antwi-Danso , Dylan Berry , Rachel Bezanson , Håkon Dahle , Carlos Garcia Diaz , Darko Donevski , Alex J. Geiger , Massissilia L. Hamadouche , Joel Leja , Guillaume Mahler , Alexandra Pope , Justin Spilker , Ezra Sukay , Francesco Valentino
 - **Subjects:** Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07237
 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.07237
 - **Abstract**
 The interstellar medium of massive quiescent galaxies holds key clues to how they quench, yet remain poorly understood. Although dust is expected to be depleted rapidly ($\lesssim$100 Myr) after quenching, some quiescent galaxies host substantial dust reservoirs, raising the question of whether dust survives quenching or is replenished afterward. We present ALMA Band 6 continuum observations ($λ_{\mathrm{rest}}$$>$250$μ$m) of eight strongly-lensed, spectroscopically-selected quiescent galaxies at z$\sim$1-2.5 from the COOL-LAMPS and REQUIEM surveys, plus five archival REQUIEM systems. The sample spans more than two orders of magnitude in dust-to-stellar-mass ratio ($f_{\mathrm{dust}}<10^{-5}$ to $>10^{-3}$), and two galaxies host extended, resolved dust. Comparing $f_{\mathrm{dust}}$ with time since quenching, roughly half the sample, preferentially at higher redshift, is consistent with rapid quenching and harsh dust destruction, while lower-redshift, more slowly quenched systems are consistent with milder destruction, possibly reflecting an evolving dominant quenching mode. The two most dust-rich galaxies exceed even the mildest model destruction tracks, with J1201-3952 retaining the highest $f_{\mathrm{dust}}$ $\sim$1.8 Gyr after quenching. If these systems are truly quiescent, their dust masses approach maximum stellar yields, pointing to ISM grain regrowth and/or TP-AGB dust production. However due to our single-band continuum data, $f_{\mathrm{dust}}$ is uncertain ($\pm$0.3 dex systematic uncertainty from the unknown dust temperature) and we cannot rule out obscured star formation in these two galaxies. Our results suggest that dust survival depends on how galaxies quench: rapid, harsh quenching efficiently destroys dust, while slower, milder quenching can leave dust reservoirs that persist or are replenished long after star formation ceases.
#### CosmicGrain: An On-the-fly Dust Physics Module
 - **Authors:** E. Huscher , K. Finlator
 - **Subjects:** Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07190
 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.07190
 - **Abstract**
 We present CosmicGrain, a dust physics module built on Gadget-4 that models explicit dust superparticles that experience resolved gas-dust drag and radiation pressure, undergo collisional heating and cooling with gas, and are further shaped by subgrid treatments for grain growth, thermal sputtering, coagulation, shattering, supernova shock destruction, and astration. We run an eleven-rung physics ladder (S0-S10) at 1024^3 resolution to isolate the contribution of individual processes to a Milky Way-mass halo's dust population. Because dust is evolved as active on-the-fly particles, individual grains retain distinct histories producing results inaccessible to passive dust models such as the strongly asymmetric silicate-to-carbon shattering destruction ratio driven by silicate grains' smaller birth size. Gas-dust drag is the dominant driver of ISM dust enrichment, increasing dust levels by ~100 times while every other process changes the R200 dust abundance by only 10-30%. We find reasonable agreement with the Milky Way's radial dust-to-gas and dust-to-metal ratios, with carbon dust preferentially enriching the CGM relative to silicates. The code is freely available at https://cosmicgrain.space.

 We argue for a significant investment of JWST time into an extremely deep medium-resolution NIRSpec MSA survey targeting a large, representative sample of high-redshift $(z>1.5)$ galaxies, specifically intended to obtain high-SNR continuum detections. #### The HST-Hyperion Survey: X-ray AGN Enhancement in the Hyperion Proto-supercluster
 - **Authors:** Ashlen Coburn , Finn Giddings , Derek Sikorski , Roy Gal , Brian C. Lemaux , Ben Forrest , Emmet Golden-Marx , Lu Shen , Devontae C. Baxter , Olga Cucciati , Joel Diamond , Denise Hung , Lori Lubin , Ekta Shah , Sandro Bardelli , Monica Natalia Isla Llave , Hyewon Suh , Laurence Tresse , Fabio Vito , Giovanni Zamorani , Elena Zucca
 - **Subjects:** Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07047
 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.07047
 - **Abstract**
 Dense environments can influence both galaxy evolution and supermassive black hole growth, but whether they enhance or suppress (or both) active galactic nuclei (AGN) activity at high redshift ($z\gtrsim2$) remains uncertain. As one of the most massive structures identified in the early universe, the Hyperion proto-supercluster at $z\approx2.5$ provides a unique opportunity to investigate this connection during an epoch near the peak of both cosmic star formation rate and AGN activity density. To examine the impact of galaxy environment on AGN activity, we measure the fraction of X-ray-selected AGN in Hyperion as a whole, its highly overdense peaks, and its outskirts, comparing these measurements to a coeval field sample. We identify X-ray AGN using the deep Chandra COSMOS-Legacy Survey and combine optical/near-infrared (NIR) photometry and photometric redshift information from COSMOS2020 with extensive ground-based spectroscopy and HST-grism observations. Redshift information is incorporated statistically using a Monte Carlo framework. We find a higher X-ray AGN fraction in Hyperion ($7.4^{+3.2}_{-1.8}\%$) than in the coeval field ($3.2^{+0.6}_{-0.5}\%$), corresponding to a $\sim2.3σ$ difference. Strikingly, this enhancement is concentrated in the overdense peaks, where the X-ray AGN fraction reaches $12.3^{+5.5}_{-3.4}\%$ ($15.0^{+6.9}_{-4.4}\%$ in the most massive peaks versus $6.2^{+11.9}_{-2.1}\%$ in the less massive peaks), while the lower-density Hyperion outskirts ($2.1^{+3.6}_{-1.1}\%$) show no evidence of enhancement relative to the field. These results add to the limited but growing evidence for enhanced AGN activity in high-redshift protostructures, suggesting that the dense environments at these epochs may preferentially trigger AGN during the early stages of cluster assembly.
#### Latest $ν$s from the Earliest Galaxies: Probing neutrino self-interactions with JWST
 - **Authors:** Ranjini Mondol , Priyank Parashari , Anirban Das , Vera Gluscevic , Ranjan Laha
 - **Subjects:** Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO) ; High Energy Physics - Phenomenology (hep-ph)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07013
 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.07013
 - **Abstract**
 Our Universe serves as a complementary laboratory for particle physics interactions. One intriguing possibility is that neutrinos may have beyond-the-Standard-Model (BSM) self-interactions, which can delay their free streaming and have an observable effect across different redshifts. Such interactions can induce scale-dependent changes in the matter power spectrum. The James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) has opened up a new window onto the early Universe by discovering a large population of high-redshift galaxies up to $z \sim 15$ and various probable galaxy candidates at even higher redshifts, between $z \sim 15-30$. Such high-redshift observations can probe the matter power spectrum on small scales, since structure formation is hierarchical and the structures hosting these high-redshift galaxies are therefore expected to be small. We, $\textit{for the first time}$, use the UV luminosity function data from JWST and combine them with the $\textit{Planck}$ CMB data to probe neutrino self-interactions ($ν$SI). If only one neutrino flavor experiences such BSM interactions, then $\textit{Planck}$ + JWST data give a 95% C.L. upper limit on $\mathrm{Log}_{10}(G_{\mathrm{eff}}/\mathrm{MeV}^{-2})$ $\approx$ $-2.75$ and when all three flavors are interacting, the corresponding constraint becomes $\lesssim -3.41$; both stronger than those obtained using only $\textit{Planck}$ data. Our results highlight the power of this new observable, the high-redshift UV luminosity function from JWST as a complementary probe to terrestrial experiments, showing that small-scale observables can open a new window onto smaller neutrino self-interaction strengths.

 - **Abstract**
 JWST has revealed an abundant population of faint active galactic nuclei and a new class of compact, high-redshift sources known as Little Red Dots (LRDs), whose physical nature remains debated, with accreting massive black holes among the leading explanations. The emerging demographics of early black holes, with their high inferred number densities and, in some systems, the apparent excess of black hole mass relative to stellar mass, challenge simple scenarios of black hole seeding and growth. Despite these observational breakthroughs, the origin of early massive black holes remains uncertain, because "black holes have no memory": current samples cannot separate the imprint of seeding from subsequent growth.

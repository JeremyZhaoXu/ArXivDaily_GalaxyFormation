#### Impact of Decay-Chain Feeding by Long-Lived Heavy Nuclei on Nuclear Cosmochronology
 - **Authors:** Qiu-Hong Chen, Meng-Hua Chen, Xianlin Yang, En-Wei Liang
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14526

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.14526

 - **Abstract**
 Nuclear cosmochronology provides an independent method for determining the ages of astrophysical objects without relying on Galactic chemical evolution models. Long-lived radioactive actinides synthesized via the rapid neutron-capture process ($r$-process) particularly thorium and uranium serve as ideal chronometric nuclei. However, predicting the initial abundances of these nuclei and their subsequent long-term evolution remains a major source of uncertainty in nuclear cosmochronology. In this work, we investigate the impact of decay-chain feeding from long-lived progenitor nuclei on nuclear cosmochronology and estimate the corresponding age corrections. We develop a self-consistent radioactive decay network that includes all relevant decay pathways and couple it to $r$-process nucleosynthesis calculations. By tracking the long-term evolution of $r$-process nucleosynthesis based on neutron star merger trajectories, the dominant long-lived nuclei shaping the evolution of the chronometric elements Th and U are identified as $^{232}$Th, $^{235}$U, $^{236}$U, $^{238}$U, and $^{244}$Pu. It is found that delayed feeding substantially modifies the long-term evolution of actinides particularly for $^{232}$Th, which receives significant late-time contributions from heavier progenitor nuclei such as $^{244}$Pu and $^{236}$U. As a consequence, the U/Th and Th/X chronometers exhibit significant sensitivity to delayed feeding from the progenitor nuclei $^{244}$Pu and $^{236}$U, with age corrections reaching $\sim5$~Gyr. These results suggest that decay-chain feeding from long-lived progenitor nuclei plays a crucial role in nuclear cosmochronology and should be properly accounted for in age determinations based on actinide chronometers.
#### The NIVARIA-Local Group simulations: Unveiling the origin of rotation curve diversity in dwarf galaxies and its connection to galaxy morphology
 - **Authors:** A. Contreras-Santos, A. Di Cintio, A. Negri, J. Sarrato-Alós, S. Cardona-Barrero, G. García-Bethencourt, C. Brook, N. I. Libeskind, A. Knebe, A. Macciò
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15071

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.15071

 - **Abstract**
 Dwarf galaxies exhibit a large diversity in rotation curve shapes, challenging galaxy formation models within the $\Lambda$CDM paradigm. We present NIVARIA-LG, a new suite of cosmological simulations studying dwarf galaxy evolution in realistic Local Group environments. This intermediate-resolution pilot lays the groundwork for the full-scale NIVARIA project. The simulations combine constrained Local Group initial conditions from HESTIA with the NIHAO galaxy formation model. The simulated galaxies reproduce observed stellar-to-halo mass relations, match the Baryonic Tully-Fisher relation, and capture the distribution of stellar effective radii, including compact and diffuse galaxies. We analyse the structural and kinematic properties of isolated dwarf galaxies with $10^7 \lesssim M_\star/M_\odot \lesssim 10^{10}$, focusing on rotation curve diversity. NIVARIA-LG naturally produces a broad range of rotation curve shapes, including slowly rising systems underrepresented in previous simulations and steep rotation curves. We find that rotation curve diversity is primarily associated with gas extent and inner dark matter structure: more extended gas and shallower inner density profiles correspond to more slowly rising curves, while stellar effective radius shows little independent association. Individual galaxies show that rotation curve shapes change rapidly as gas is redistributed during assembly. Recent accretion events are also associated with more slowly rising curves in the most massive dwarfs. Together, these results suggest that rotation curve diversity is linked to the baryonic cycle, which shapes the inner dark matter distribution. The Local Group environment and its filaments influence this evolution by regulating gas accretion and redistribution, producing the observed diversity in rotation curve shapes.

 - **Authors:** Qinhao Shao, Jianhui Lian

 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15295

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.15295

 - **Abstract**
 This study investigates the chemical evolution history and radial migration in the Milky Way utilizing high-precision age measurements based on large-scale stellar spectroscopic surveys. Recent observational data have revealed a distinct break in the age-metallicity relation of stars in the solar neighborhood, suggesting that stellar populations of the Milky Way can be divided into two evolutionary sequences. Based on galactic chemical evolution models, we confirm that this break in the age-metallicity relation can be explained by a recent major gas accretion event, which diluted the metallicity in the disk and triggered a starburst, leading to the formation of an independent age-metallicity sequence. To assess the impact of stellar radial migration, we incorporate a simplified treatment of this process into our multi-zone chemical evolution model. Radial migration is modeled as a probabilistic diffusion process, characterized by a Gaussian distribution. This result is lower than previous estimates of stellar radial migration, suggesting that the role of radial migration in shaping the chemical structure of the Milky Way is less important than previously estimated.

We present a catalogue of spectroscopically derived physical properties for ~12,000 galaxies at z>2, with archival JWST/NIRSpec PRISM spectroscopy and JWST/NIRCam imaging from the DAWN JWST Archive. #### Constraints on the r-process Delay Time from Dwarf Spheroidal Galaxies
 - **Authors:** Lauren E. Henderson, Evan N. Kirby, Mithi A. C. de los Reyes, Rebecca Surman
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15946

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.15946

 - **Abstract**
 The ancient stellar populations and simple structure of the local dwarf spheroidal galaxies (dSphs) make them excellent laboratories for studying the chemical evolution of the early universe. In this paper, we fit one-zone galactic chemical evolution models to C, Mg, Si, Ca, Fe, Sr, Y, Ba, and Eu abundances in the dSphs Sculptor and Draco. By including the s- and r-processes in our model, we are able to place constraints on the timescale of r-process enrichment in these dSphs. We find that neutron star mergers cannot be the only source of the r-process in Sculptor and Draco, and that a prompt r-process source is required to explain the observed stellar abundances. When neutron star mergers are the only source of the r-process in our models, their minimum delay time must be just 14-16 Myr. The neutron-capture abundances in Sculptor and Draco can also be fit by models with a longer neutron star merger minimum delay time combined with a prompt r-process source that operates on the same timescale as massive star evolution, such as magneto-rotational supernovae. Additionally, the Sr and Y abundances in the dSphs set limits on the Sr and Y yields per r-process event. We find that theoretical r-process yields overestimate Sr and Y production by a factor of up to 100.

 - **Abstract**
 We examine whether the unusual spatial flattening and orbital-plane coherence of the Milky Way's classical satellites are connected to the Galaxy's location in the cosmic web. We compare the 11 classical satellites with the 11 most massive satellites of 11,922 Milky Way-mass hosts in the TNG300-1 hydrodynamical simulation.  - **Authors:** Martin Rosenlyst, Vinh Tran, Oliver Zier, Xuejian Shen, Mark Vogelsberger, Thejs Brinckmann, Mads T. Frandsen, Jesús Zavala, Frederik Holt Dihver Christensen, Mikkel Theiss Kristensen

 - **Abstract**
 We develop a unified theory-to-simulation framework for self-interacting dark matter (SIDM), connecting microscopic dark-sector physics to nonlinear structure formation. We systematize particle-physics ingredients of representative SIDM models and map microscopic parameters and interactions onto structure-formation simulation inputs. These include internal-state populations and reaction networks, velocity- and angle-dependent cross sections, inelastic and dissipative energy exchange, decay rates and recoil kinematics, and modifications to cosmological initial conditions. The resulting modular framework connects dark-sector models to nonlinear astrophysical predictions, enabling exploration of microscopic interactions beyond standard isotropic, elastic SIDM.
#### Interpreting the stacked kinetic SZ signal II: the degeneracy between satellite galaxies and baryonic feedback
 - **Authors:** Lurdes Ondaro-Mallea, Boryana Hadzhiyska, Raul E. Angulo
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO); Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17200

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.17200

 - **Abstract**
 The kinetic Sunyaev-Zel'dovich (kSZ) signal stacked around a galaxy sample traces the gas around halos and is a powerful probe of baryonic feedback. Its interpretation, however, depends on the satellite galaxies in the sample. Using the FLAMINGO hydrodynamical simulations and DESI-like samples of luminous red galaxies (LRGs), bright galaxies survey (BGS), and emission-line galaxies (ELGs), we show that satellites and feedback have similar effects: both suppress the small-scale signal. This degeneracy arises because stacking on satellites, which are offset from the centre of their host haloes, mimics the extended gas distribution produced by feedback. Although satellites make up only 20-30% of each sample, they account for 50-80% of the signal because they occupy more massive halos than centrals; thus their imprint on the stacked profiles can be as large as that of feedback. At the precision of current DESI$\times$ACT data, neglecting satellites in the modelling biases the inferred gas fractions low, by up to a factor of two, and overestimates the feedback strength, particularly for the LRG and BGS samples. Remaining agnostic about the satellite population avoids this bias but reduces the constraining power, so robust constraints would require knowing the satellite fraction to better than 10%, for instance from lensing and clustering data of the same galaxy samples. Finally, a further contribution from satellites will become statistically significant with upcoming surveys: satellites trace the anisotropic gas distribution around their host haloes, an effect that contributes 10% of the signal, and will likely require simulation-based models to capture.
#### Untangling Similarities and Differences in Star Formation Histories Across Cosmological Simulations
 - **Authors:** Alicia M. Savelli, Matthew A. Kustec, Joshua S. Speagle, Kartheik G. Iyer, Eshal Ahmed, Zehao Peng, Norman Murray, Cissy Kuang
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17359

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.17359

 - **Abstract**
 How galaxies assemble depends on processes ranging from small-scale baryonic physics to large-scale cosmological assembly, which are difficult to disentangle with observations alone. Using $\sim$63,000 simulated galaxies from seven cosmological simulations spanning hydrodynamical, semi-analytic, and semi-empirical models, we explore how specific star formation histories (sSFHs) vary across modelling assumptions. Using non-linear dimensionality reduction (UMAP), we find that sSFH shape variation organizes along a few smooth axes in a 2D embedding, broadly corresponding to (1) peak timing, (2) peak sharpness, and (3) subsequent quenching rate. We identify sSFH shapes that many simulations share ("common"), well described to first order by dark matter halo assembly, and a few tied to individual simulations ("unique"), which likely reflect subgrid and parametrization choices such as merger-triggered burst prescriptions. These results provide avenues for theoretical and observational follow-up connecting star formation histories to subgrid physical models.

The black holes accrete residual gas, remaining from the first star formation event, and form accretion disks generated by gas angular momentum induced by the black-holes' own curved orbits. Assuming initial black hole masses below the pair-instability mass gap $\leq 55\,{\rm M}_{\odot}$, the gap gets populated with black-hole masses growing to $\sim 10^2\,{\rm M}_{\odot}$, and even reaching several hundred solar masses for sufficiently dense and massive proto-clusters. We predict a consistent spin-mass relation with low black hole spins $\lesssim 0.2$ for low black hole masses $\lesssim 20\,{\rm M}_{\odot}$, spanning all intermediate spins for higher masses and saturating to high spins $\gtrsim 0.8$ for high masses $\gtrsim 70\,{\rm M}_{\odot}$. We find evidence in favour of such an accretion-driven subpopulation of black holes in the GWTC-5.0 released by the LIGO-Virgo-KAGRA collaboration.
#### The Connection Between the Growth of Massive Black Holes and Their Dynamical Histories
 - **Authors:** Lee A. Whitaker, Michael Tremmel, Angelo Ricarte, Priyamvada Natarajan
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18659

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.18659

 - **Abstract**
 Massive black holes (MBHs) grow through both gas accretion and mergers and it remains unclear how variations to MBH growth and dynamical histories contribute to the spread in empirical scaling relations between MBHs and their host galaxies. We examine the ex- and in-situ growth of MBHs within large-scale cosmological simulations and compare the evolution of an outlier population of under-grown MBHs to the general population. We use the Romulus25 uniform volume cosmological simulation and the RomulusC cluster zoom-in simulation to examine the growth and merger histories of MBHs. We identify central MBHs that are undergrown relative to other MBHs in similar mass galaxies and investigate their accretion, merger, and orbital histories within the simulations. The contribution of ex-situ growth increases with MBH mass, with mergers becoming increasingly important in the most massive galaxies. We find no difference between cluster and field environments in terms of the importance of ex-situ growth, which is generally very low. Under-grown MBHs have experienced fewer mergers and have had complex dynamical histories, spending significant time 'drifting' within the galaxy or 'competing' with another MBH. Other under-grown MBHs have experienced a 'late entrance' into a galaxy which until then had no central MBH. Our results demonstrate that MBH assembly is dominated by in-situ growth, which in turn is impacted by the dynamical histories of MBHs within assembling galaxies. Prolonged off-centre evolution can suppress accretion and contribute to the formation of under-grown central MBHs at $z=0$.

 - **Abstract**
 Stellar ages are an important parameter for studying the chemical evolution of the Galaxy. To improve age estimates, various complementary methods to the conventional isochrone-fitting method have been implemented in the last decade. The [Y/Mg] ratio has been proposed as a chemical clock for solar-metallicity stars, due to its strong dependence on stellar age. In this work, we study the behaviour of the abundance ratio [Y/Mg] in metal-poor stars, along with other solar-type disk stars, as a function of stellar age determined through isochrones. #### Field-level modeling of halo and galaxy alignments
 - **Authors:** Nickolas Kokron, Mikhail M. Ivanov, James M. Sullivan
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO); Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36027

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.36027

 - **Abstract**
 We develop an effective field theory (EFT) of intrinsic alignments at the level of spin-2 field fluctuations of large-scale structure tracers, such as the shapes of galaxies and dark matter halos. Our perturbative model includes the non-linear treatment of the Zel'dovich displacement, and contains all necessary shape operators through the third order. We apply our field-level model to the full 3D distribution of galaxy shapes, whose sky projections are a known contaminant of cosmic shear measurements. Our field-level EFT model achieves a better description of the helicity fields of dark matter halo shapes than the standard non-linear alignment (NLA) and tidal torquing + tidal alignment (TATT) models in the literature, as evidenced by improved correlations with the simulations' initial conditions. We perform precision measurements of field-level bias parameters by means of a generalized least-squares problem, for which we derive an optimal estimator and analytic covariance matrices. We measure the full set of linear, quadratic, and cubic shape bias coefficients as a function of halo mass in the $\texttt{Quijote}$ N-body simulations. We then study the intrinsic alignments of galaxies in the $\texttt{FLAMINGO}$ hydrodynamical simulation, focusing on observationally relevant samples: spirals, ellipticals, luminous red galaxies (LRG) and emission-line galaxies (ELG). We detect linear and nonlinear alignment coefficients at high significance for LRG, spiral and elliptical galaxies. We also find evidence for non-zero density-weighted tidal bias coefficient of the simulated ELG. This work supports the notion that EFT-based models of alignments will be paramount in optimally extracting constraints from future shape correlations, and lays the foundation for the development of simulation-based priors for future galaxy shape samples.
#### Does the Escape Fraction Prescription Shape Reionization History and Morphology?
 - **Authors:** Pulkit Sheoran, Ankita Bera, Sultan Hassan
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO); Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36046

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.36046

 - **Abstract**
 The escape fraction of ionizing photons, $f_{\rm esc}$, is one of the least constrained quantities of the Epoch of Reionization (EoR), and the opacity of the intergalactic medium prevents its direct measurement at high redshifts. We constrain three power-law parameterizations, $f_{\rm esc}(z)$, $f_{\rm esc}(M_{\rm h})$, and $f_{\rm esc}(z, M_{\rm h})$, against JWST UV luminosity functions and EoR observables (the ionizing emissivity, the neutral hydrogen fraction, and the Thomson optical depth) using Markov Chain Monte Carlo, with priors that allow $f_{\rm esc}$ to either increase or decrease with redshift and halo mass. We then propagate the best-fit models into the semi-numerical simulation SimFast21 and compare their ionization morphology and 21 cm power spectra with those of a constant-$f_{\rm esc}$ model. All models reproduce the observations with nearly identical global reionization histories. The data favor an escape fraction that decreases with increasing redshift, from $\approx 0.8$ at $z = 5$ to $\approx 0.35$ at $z = 15$. The halo-mass-only model prefers $f_{\rm esc}$ increasing with halo mass, but this trend acts as a proxy for redshift evolution. When we vary both dependencies jointly, the data prefer a mass-independent $f_{\rm esc}$. At matched global neutral fraction, the ionization morphology is closely similar for all four prescriptions, and the 21 cm power spectra agree to within $\approx 10\%$. These results suggest that, once calibrated against the same observations, the details of the $f_{\rm esc}$ prescription, which are set by the physics of the interstellar medium, largely average out in both the global observables and the 21 cm power spectrum. Distinguishing them may require statistics beyond the power spectrum, such as sightline statistics of the Ly$\alpha$ and 21 cm forests, or forecasts that exploit their small differences in reionization timing.

#### CROMA: a GPU-accelerated Fokker--Planck solver for modeling non-thermal emission in post-processing cosmological simulations
 - **Authors:** Kosuke Nishiwaki, Claudio Gheller, Gianfranco Brunetti, Katsuaki Asano
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE); Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO); Instrumentation and Methods for Astrophysics (astro-ph.IM)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36074

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.36074

 - **Abstract**
 Numerical modeling of cosmic-ray (CR) spectra and the resulting non-thermal emission is important for understanding the physical mechanisms of CR acceleration and transport in astrophysical environments. Galaxy clusters and cosmic filaments provide particularly important environments for studying these processes, where shocks, turbulence, magnetic fields, and CRs jointly shape diffuse synchrotron emission. Spectrally resolved modeling of CRs, including cooling, re-acceleration, and hadronic interactions, requires solving time-dependent Fokker--Planck (FP) equations. An increasing number of studies employ FP solvers coupled with magnetohydrodynamical (MHD) simulations to follow the evolution of CRs in dynamically evolving environments. We present a new C/C++-based FP solver with GPU acceleration for post-processing outputs from cosmological MHD simulations. The code evolves CR proton and electron spectra along Lagrangian tracer trajectories and consistently includes re-acceleration and secondary-particle production processes. With the Chang--Cooper finite-difference scheme, the FP equation for each tracer is reduced to an independent tridiagonal system, which is solved on NVIDIA GPUs using a CUDA implementation of the parallel cyclic reduction (PCR) algorithm. The code uses sub-stepping between tracer snapshots, with the timestep constrained by the relevant CR cooling or re-acceleration timescale, and a batching algorithm that groups tracers with similar sub-stepping patterns for efficient execution. We find that the GPU acceleration increases the per-node throughput by a factor of $\approx$10 on the Leonardo Booster system operated by CINECA. The fast FP solver is useful for high-resolution simulations dedicated to comparison with next-generation radio observations and future multi-messenger applications combining radio, gamma-ray, and high-energy neutrino observations.
#### Heavy Seeds and the Multi-scale Fueling of Early Supermassive Black Holes and Little Red Dots
 - **Authors:** Junehyoung Jeon, Volker Bromm, Boyuan Liu, Stella S. R. Offner, Anthony J. Taylor, Steven L. Finkelstein
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:

 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36089

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.36089

 - **Abstract**
 The formation of supermassive black holes (SMBHs) in the early Universe has been a longstanding mystery due to the brief period available to assemble them. More recently, the unexpected discovery with the James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) of Little Red Dots (LRDs), compact sources exhibiting signatures of active galactic nuclei (AGN), has accentuated this challenge. Cosmological simulations investigating their formation and evolution confront the limitation that the compact scale of SMBH/LRD physics cannot be simultaneously resolved with the larger-scale cosmological environment. We address this limitation by utilizing a cutout technique, selecting a targeted region around an SMBH seed from a cosmological parent simulation and re-running the cutout at higher (sub-pc) resolution for shorter periods.  Characterising the assembly history of the inner Milky Way is a key goal of upcoming spectroscopic surveys, yet disentangling its overlapping stellar components remains challenging. We investigate the chemodynamics of the centre of a Milky Way analogue in the Auriga Superstars cosmological hydrodynamical simulation suite, focusing on a high-resolution halo (~ 800 M_sun star particles) with a realistic galactic bar and accretion history.  - **Abstract**
 The escape velocity is a key probe of the Milky Way (MW) potential, but standard estimates can be biased by unrelaxed substructures. Using MW analogs from the TNG50 cosmological simulation, we investigate the impact of the dominant, highly radial Gaia-Sausage-Enceladus (GSE) debris on escape-velocity measurements. We introduce an adaptive selection strategy that balances statistical reliability with sample size to identify the power-law decline regime of the local halo velocity distribution. #### Three-flavor neutrino Boltzmann transport and flavor instabilities in long-lived neutron-star merger remnants
 - **Authors:** Harry Ho-Yin Ng, Jiabao Liu, Arthur Offermans, Shoichi Yamada, Elias R. Most, Luciano Rezzolla
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36486

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.36486

 - **Abstract**
 Binary neutron-star (BNS) mergers are multimessenger sources whose kilonova emission and nucleosynthesis depend sensitively on neutrino microphysics and flavor conversion. Most BNS merger simulations assume $\mu$-$\tau$ neutrino degeneracy in a two-flavor treatment. We investigate the fast flavor instability (FFI) and the collisional flavor instability (CFI) in long-lived BNS merger remnants using axisymmetric general-relativistic three-flavor Boltzmann neutrino transport with a comprehensive treatment of muons and muonic interactions, following a handoff from 3D grey moment-based simulations. Our simulations largely reproduce the parent 3D simulations, recovering trapped weak equilibrium in the core, muonic non-equilibrium in the semitransparent outer disk due to prolonged demuonization, and cold weak equilibrium in transparent regions. Linear stability analysis reveals strong and widespread CFI in all three flavor sectors, including narrow resonance-like structures. Muonic interactions reshape the FFI distribution, shifting the $e\tau$-sector instability toward denser disk regions compared with the extended FFI outside the disk in the two-flavor model, while disk demuonization produces a strong $\nu_\mu$ excess and one-sided muon-lepton-number angular distributions that suppress FFI in the $e\mu$ and $\mu\tau$ sectors. Local three-flavor quantum kinetic equation (QKE) calculations reveal nonlinear coupling among flavor sectors that excites coherence even in an initially stable sector. Diagonal collisional repopulation is decisive for the nonlinear evolution and asymptotic flavor populations, and neglecting it overestimates flavor equilibration. We establish the necessity of three-flavor transport from both classical and quantum perspectives, and underscore the importance of consistently coupling flavor conversion with diagonal collisional repopulation in future CFI subgrid models.

 - **Authors:** Charlie T. Mpetha, Benedikt Diemer
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36531

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.36531

 - **Abstract**
 In the conventional spherical overdensity definition of a dark matter halo, masses and radii, e.g. $M_{200{\rm m}}$ and $r_{200{\rm m}}$, are equivalent. However, halos do exhibit a physical boundary at the splashback radius $r_{\rm sp}$, which manifests as a sharp drop in density. Splashback radii are now routinely measured in stacked weak lensing profiles, but the expected trend of decreasing $r_{\rm sp}/r_{200{\rm m}}$ with mass has not been verified in observed galaxy groups or clusters. In this work, we show that several observational effects conceal the predicted mass-size relation. In particular, we quantify the impact of systematics on the mean density and lensing profiles of mock-observed halo samples, which are created by applying a realistic cluster-finding procedure to dark matter-only simulations. We reproduce the known impact of projection: preferentially observing systems in overdense regions. But, differing from previous works, we show that the same projection effect, combined with scatter in observed luminosities, causes a significant Eddington bias in the derived mass-observable relation and a complex suppression in mean density profiles over all radial scales. The magnitude of this effect is mass-dependent and coupled to the cluster-finding procedure and survey properties, making it challenging to model simply and motivating full forward-modeling of observed group and cluster samples. However, we demonstrate that a simple mitigation approach, effectively a weak lensing mass calibration, does well in approximately recovering the true mass-size relation. Finally, we show that only severe miscentering can bias the measurement of the halo size, but any miscentering can increase the measurement uncertainty.

 - **Abstract**

 - **Authors:** Amanda Stoffers, Sandro Tacchella, Benjamin D. Johnson, Brant Robertson, Roberto Maiolino, Andrew J. Bunker, Emma Curtis-Lake, A. Lola Danhaive, Qiao Duan, Francesco D'Eugenio
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:

 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37643

 - **Abstract**
 JWST surveys find more UV-bright galaxies at $z>10$ than galaxy-formation models predict, but the roles of recent star formation, stellar mass and dust remain uncertain. We analyse 1365 spectroscopically confirmed JADES galaxies at $3\leq z\leq9$, fitting NIRCam photometry and NIRSpec emission lines with \ceri. #### The Spatially-Resolved Recent Star Formation History of the M81 Disk
 - **Authors:** Vaishnav V. Rao, Eric F. Bell, Adam Smercina, Tobin M. Wainer, Andrew Dolphin, Benjamin Williams, Julianne J. Dalcanton
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37661

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.37661

 - **Abstract**
 The nearby M81 galaxy---a tidally interacting $L^*$ spiral disk---offers a unique perspective on the interplay of secular and external processes in shaping galaxy properties. We present the most detailed, panoramic map of M81's stellar populations to date, using archival Hubble Space Telescope ACS photometry from the ANGST survey spanning the full optical extent of its disk. From color-magnitude diagram fitting, we recover spatially-resolved star formation histories (SFHs) over the last $\sim180\,$Myr. M81's global SFH is remarkably steady in the last $\sim180\,$Myr with slight enhancements seen $\lesssim15\,$Myr ago. The average SFR surface density rises toward the center before falling off within the innermost $\sim4\,$kpc. The disk is also notably lopsided, with the eastern half having formed $\sim50\%$ more stars than the western half over the same area---a trend missed by FUV-based SFR indicators due to dust systematics. #### Improving cosmological distance measurements at high redshift using Lyman-Alpha forest BAO reconstruction

 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37664

 - **Abstract**
 The Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI) measures baryon acoustic oscillations (BAO) in the Lyman-$\alpha$ (Lyman-alpha) forest and provides a robust probe of the expansion history in the matter-dominated era. Yet, non-linear structure formation broadens and shifts the BAO peak. To undo these effects, we extend standard Zel'dovich reconstruction, routinely applied to galaxy surveys, to the Lyman-alpha forest. We validate our methodology on large-volume idealized Lyman-alpha forest mocks painted on top of the \textsc{AbacusSummit} $N$-body simulations, using linear control variates to suppress sample variance and thereby reduce the error bars by a factor of up to $\approx \sqrt{50}$. In fully sampled periodic boxes, reconstruction halves the radial BAO damping scale and reduces the uncertainties on the radial ($\apar$) and transverse ($\aperp$) scaling parameters by $\approx25\%$ and $\approx15\%$, respectively. For this mask, we compare two approaches to estimate the displacement field: either directly from the Lyman-alpha forest or using an external tracer with a number density of $\bar n \simeq 10^{-3}\,h^3\mathrm{Mpc}^{-3}$, such as Lyman-break galaxies and Lyman-$\alpha$ emitters---both key targets for DESI-II. Both approaches give comparable gains and reduce the uncertainties on $\apar$ and $\aperp$ by $\approx 27\%$ and $\approx 10\%$, respectively, with mild improvements for futuristic high-density surveys with $160\,\mathrm{deg}^{-2}$.

 - **Abstract**
 Heavily dust-obscured active galactic nuclei (AGN) are crucial for understanding supermassive black hole growth. Using multi-band MIRI photometry from the MINERVA survey, we report the discovery in the UDS field of an extremely mid-infrared bright Compton-thick AGN (CT AGN) at $z \sim 3.8$, \textit{el Ascua} (the Ember). The AGN and/or its host galaxy is detected at 870\,$\mu$m with ALMA and at 1.4\,GHz with the VLA. With UV-to-radio spectral energy distribution modeling, we estimate that the dusty host galaxy has a stellar mass of $\log\left( M_\star / M_\odot \right)=10.82 \pm 0.57$ and a star formation rate of ${\rm{SFR}} =157 \pm 8 \,M_\odot\,{\rm yr^{-1}}$, and we confirm the presence of a powerful dusty AGN with an intrinsic accretion luminosity of $\log\left( L_{\rm{acc}} /{\rm erg\,s^{-1}} \right)=47.12 \pm 0.58$. We additionally find evidence for a radio excess by comparing its total IR-to-radio ratio to those of typical star-forming galaxies and radio-quiet AGN, implying that \textit{el Ascua} could be a rare radio-loud CT AGN. With an extremely high ratio between black hole accretion rate and SFR, the AGN is likely nearing the peak of a deeply obscured phase of black hole growth before AGN feedback blows away the obscuring material. Our work demonstrates the power of MIRI to reveal heavily obscured AGN missing from our supermassive black hole census.

#### How Steep is your Dust? Direct Measurements of Nebular Attenuation Curves at Cosmic Noon with JWST
 - **Authors:** Gabriel Maheson, Sirio Belli, Sandro Tacchella, Letizia Bugiani, Joel Leja, Rebecca Davies, Minjung Park, Amir H. Khoram, Matteo Sapori, Caterina Liboni, Benjamin P. Collins
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33166

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.33166

 - **Abstract**
 Tracing the shape of the nebular attenuation curve, which is crucial for accurately measuring intrinsic emission-line fluxes, has proven challenging both in the local universe and at high redshift. Here we present nebular dust attenuation curves measured directly for 24 star-forming galaxies ($9.3<\log(\rm M{\star}/M_\odot)<11.1$) at cosmic noon ($1.8<z<3.3$) from the Blue Jay survey. Using deep JWST/NIRSpec spectra, we measure up to eight hydrogen recombination lines from H$\delta$ to Pa$\beta$ per galaxy and find little diversity in the curve shape, with most galaxies agreeing with the \citet{calzetti_dust_2000} attenuation curve. For over $90\%$ of the sample, the nebular reddening from the Balmer lines is consistent with that from the Paschen lines. Using \texttt{Prospector} spectral energy distribution (SED) fits, we obtain the stellar reddening and find that the difference between stellar and nebular reddening increases with stellar mass, and that each reddening measurement is independent of inclination, which disagrees with the two-component dust model. We then measure the impact of using the derived nebular attenuation curve or a literature curve when determining metallicity using the strong-line method, and find that for some diagnostics (e.g., S32, S3, S23, and S3O3), the difference can be as large as $5\sigma$. Caution is needed when using these diagnostics if the nebular attenuation curve cannot be directly measured, since the resulting uncertainty can exceed the measurement uncertainty on the emission-line fluxes even with high signal-to-noise Balmer decrement measurements. These results show that the shape of the nebular attenuation curve is poorly understood and that constraining it is important for reducing systematic uncertainty in galaxy measurements.
#### Spatial variability of CH4-to-CO conversion pathways in irradiated gas giant atmospheres: implications for chemical relaxation and 1D quench schemes
 - **Authors:** Maria Zamyatina, Éric Hébrard, Harry Baskett, Roméo Veillet, Duncan A. Christie, Nathan J. Mayne, Denis E. Sergeev, Krisztian Kohary
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Earth and Planetary Astrophysics (astro-ph.EP)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33643

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.33643

 - **Abstract**
 Close-in gas giant atmospheres are in chemical disequilibrium. Solving 3D kinetics to capture one source of this disequilibrium, transport-induced quenching, is computationally demanding, so simplifications are used. General circulation models (GCMs) relax abundances on the timescale of a single conversion pathway. 1D models place a quench level and hold abundances vertically uniform above it. We test both simplifications on CH4 against aerosol-free, solar-composition GCM simulations with thermochemical kinetics for HAT-P-11b, HD 189733b, HD 209458b and WASP-17b. We find ten CH4-to-CO conversion pathways, varying with depth, longitude and planet. A single pathway controls CH4 quenching on the three cooler planets; on WASP-17b two compete. The CH4 quench level is 3D: where the atmosphere is hotter, or a faster pathway takes over, the conversion timescale shortens, the quench level rises and the quench-level abundance falls. On WASP-17b the pathway switch lifts the quench level over the substellar tropics, deepening equatorial CH4 depletion. A 1D quench treatment with the Zahnle & Marley timescale typically locates the quench level within a factor of two in pressure for CH4 and about four for CO on every planet. It fails above the quench level, where every quench scheme holds abundances vertically uniform. The minor carbon carrier, CH4 or CO depending on the planet, is wrong by up to two orders of magnitude, on HD 189733b in opposite directions at different latitudes. Quenching sets an abundance at the quench level but does not fix it thereafter; even without photochemistry, transport and conversion keep that abundance spatially structured.
#### FastPM: Fast Pretrained Model for Cosmological Simulation-Based Inference
 - **Authors:** Masaya Yamamoto, Adrian E. Bayer
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO); Instrumentation and Methods for Astrophysics (astro-ph.IM)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33955

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.33955

 - **Abstract**
 Modern cosmology increasingly relies on large ensembles of expensive simulations to model the non-linear Universe and extract information from upcoming surveys. However, generating high-fidelity simulations---whether gravity-only N-body or full hydrodynamical---requires substantial computational resources. In this work, we demonstrate that transfer learning from fast low-fidelity simulations can dramatically reduce the cost of training machine learning models, in particular for simulation-based inference (SBI). We train a Fast Pretrained Model (FastPM) on a set of Fast Particle-Mesh (FastPM) simulations to infer $\Omega_m$ and $\sigma_8$ from the power spectrum, and finetune on a smaller set of high-fidelity CAMELS N-body and hydrodynamical simulations. To reach a fixed posterior accuracy, the pretrained model requires only 5--15\% of the high-fidelity simulations compared to the number of simulations needed when training on CAMELS alone, reducing the total training simulation compute cost, including that of the pretraining simulations, by up to an order of magnitude in CPU hours. Remarkably, the gains persist for the total matter power spectrum of full hydrodynamical simulations: although FastPM contains no baryonic physics, pretraining on FastPM runs that together cost less than a single hydrodynamical simulation still reduces the total training simulation compute by a factor of $\sim$5. Moreover, pretraining on more accurate FastPM runs yields more accurate posteriors than training on CAMELS alone achieves with any number of simulations we consider. These results demonstrate that transfer learning from fast simulations is a simple and powerful route to high-fidelity SBI, and highlight the promise of simulation-based foundation models in cosmology.
#### Halo formation and distribution around cosmic web
 - **Authors:** Hongfei Yu, Huiyuan Wang, Yangyao Chen, Ziwen Zhang, Hao Li
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34042

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.34042

 - **Abstract**
 We use the ELUCID simulation to study how the local environment affects the formation of dark matter halos and their spatial distribution around the cosmic web. We describe the environment by local density and cosmic-web type. At fixed web type, the halo mass function (HMF) depends strongly on the local density, whereas at fixed density it depends only weakly, though significantly, on the web type; the character of both dependencies changes around the mean density. The halo formation redshift is primarily driven by local density rather than cosmic web. We then introduce the three-dimensional cosmic-web cross-correlation function (3D-WCCF), which expresses the distribution of halos around cosmic web in the local tidal eigenframe. The 3D-WCCF provides numerous anisotropic characteristics. Around all web types, halos are most strongly correlated along the axis of weakest compression, $\mathbf{e}_3$, and least along the axis of strongest compression, $\mathbf{e}_1$. Nearly all cluster-sized halos reside in knots and lie preferentially in the plane perpendicular to ${\bf e}_1$. The 3D-WCCF also provides important information on the geometry and size of filaments, sheets and voids. Moreover, early-forming halos are more strongly correlated with the web than late-forming ones, and this assembly bias is anisotropic around filaments, sheets, and voids, being strongest along $\mathbf{e}_3$, but nearly isotropic around knots.

 - **Abstract**
 The radioactive isotope 26Al is believed to be mainly produced by massive stars and ejected into the interstellar medium through their winds and supernovae. Due to its lifetime of 1.04 Myr, it traces recent nucleosynthesis and star formation. 60Fe is a similarly long-lived (lifetime 3.8 Myr) isotope, and only originating in ccSNe. Stellar evolution models in combination with Galactic Chemical Evolution (GCE) models tend to over-predict the 60Fe/26Al ratio. We are using 20 yr of INTEGRAL/SPI observations of the gamma-ray line at 1.8 MeV to test different assumptions on the radial structure of 26Al and compare the resulting mass distribution to different GCE models. For our baseline analysis, all tested source models prefer substantial nova contributions, with best-fit fractions of 77-84%. This alleviates tension in the 60Fe/26Al ratio and lowers the SFR from 26Al measurements accordingly to 1-2 Msun/yr. While it is difficult to gauge both contributions directly, next generation MeV telescopes can potentially identify individual massive stars, such as Gamma-2-Velorum.

 - **Authors:** Seok-Jun Chang, Max Gronke, Jorryt Matthee, Charlotte Mason

 - **Abstract**
 JWST has uncovered a population of compact high-redshift galaxies, 'little red dots' (LRDs), exhibiting broad Balmer emission lines that often show narrow absorption features and strong Balmer breaks - spectral signatures attributed to dense gas surrounding rapidly growing black holes, but whose small-scale structure remains unknown.  - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35673

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.35673

 - **Abstract**
 The formation of supermassive black holes (SMBHs) is a key open problem in galaxy evolution, with light- and heavy-seeding models predicting distinct black hole occupation fractions (BHOFs). Using multiwavelength observations from the LSST Deep Drilling Fields, we measure the AGN fraction and estimate the BHOF across $0<z<1$. Applying a hierarchical Bayesian model, we find that the inferred BHOF remains $100\%$ for host galaxy masses below $10^9 M_\odot$ at lower redshifts. These findings are consistent with light-seed scenarios. Additionally, we computed the black hole masses for several galaxies in the W-CDF-S to test the mass-weighted black hole occupation distributions from semi-analytic models (SAM). Using a reduced-$\chi^2$ analysis, we find that the data are consistent with the main-sequence accretion model regardless of the assumed seeding mechanism. These results provide new observational constraints on SMBH seeding and highlight the value of combining deep extragalactic surveys with probabilistic modeling.
#### pbhalo: computing primordial black hole and minihalo populations from a single primordial perturbation spectrum
 - **Authors:** B.A. Rudenko
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35703

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.35703

 - **Abstract**
 An enhancement of the primordial curvature power spectrum on small scales can give rise to two populations of compact objects: primordial black holes (PBHs), which form during radiation domination, and dark matter minihalos, including ultracompact minihalos (UCMHs), which form from the same modes during matter domination. We present pbhalo, an open-source Python package that computes the PBH mass function, the halo mass function and the UCMH abundance from a single spectrum in a given cosmology and applies observational constraints on the PBH abundance and on the spectrum jointly. The package components are validated against colossus (a code for halo mass functions and cosmological quantities), pbhstat (a code for the PBH mass function from a given spectrum) and published results; joint constraints are illustrated for lognormal peaks.

#### TNGC-SIDM I: Evolution of massive cluster mergers under the self-interacting dark matter paradigm
 - **Authors:** Wonki Lee, Dylan Nelson, Myungkook J. Jee, Giulia Despali, Mark Vogelsberger, Annalisa Pillepich, Cian Roche, Kyle Finner, Hyungjin Joo, Daisuke Nagai, John ZuHone
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO); Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18957

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.18957

 - **Abstract**
 Merging galaxy clusters provide an ideal laboratory for testing the self-interacting nature of dark matter. In this paper, we present a pilot study of magnetohydrodynamical cosmological zoom-in simulations of two cluster mergers selected from TNG-Cluster, with $M_{\rm 200c} \sim 9\times10^{14}~M_\odot$ and $M_{\rm 200c}\sim 2\times10^{14}~M_\odot$. The simulations are performed with both standard collisionless cold dark matter (CDM) and self-interacting dark matter (SIDM), assuming an isotropic, velocity-independent cross-section of $\sigma_{\rm T}/m_\chi = 1~\rm cm^2~g^{-1}$, with an additional $0.5~\rm cm^2~g^{-1}$ run for the low-mass system, and the mass resolution and galaxy formation model are unchanged from TNG-Cluster. We show how dark matter self-interactions produce an isothermal core in the dark matter halo, generate unique substructures in the intracluster light, and introduce variation in the multi-wavelength observables of the intracluster plasma. Using these simulations, we test the validity of observational probes for dark matter self-interactions in cluster mergers. We find that the shock-to-shock separation shows similar evolution in the self-interaction models tested here, providing a reference for distinguishing the $\sim100-200~\rm kpc$-scale difference in halo separation induced by self-interactions. Moreover, the dark matter halo centroid of the main cluster in the massive cluster merger is offset from the brightest cluster galaxy by as much as $\sim20~\rm kpc$, larger than the offset in the sub-cluster. This offset persists beyond 4 Gyr after the first pericenter passage. Our results indicate that sample selection plays a key role in testing the possible self-interacting nature of dark matter using cluster mergers, and that double radio relic cluster mergers and massive clusters with evidence of an ancient major merger offer a promising testbed for this purpose.
#### Galactic Outflows and Star-Formation Quenching: A JWST Perspective
 - **Authors:** Rebecca L. Davies, Sirio Belli
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19182

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.19182

 - **Abstract**
 Outflows driven by active galactic nuclei (AGN) are widely considered a key mechanism for regulating and quenching star formation in massive galaxies. However, direct observational evidence linking outflows to quenching has remained elusive for decades. This article reviews recent findings for massive galaxies at cosmic noon ($z\sim 2$), based on sensitive James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) observations tracing both star formation histories (through Balmer absorption lines) and neutral outflows (through blueshifted NaID absorption). Neutral outflows are found in $\sim$ 25 - 50% of massive ($\log(M_*/M_\odot)>$ 10) galaxies, independent of star formation rate. Outflows from star-forming galaxies are consistent with being powered by star-formation and/or AGN activity, whereas outflows from quiescent systems are generally attributed to current or previous AGN activity. There is growing evidence that recent rapid quenching of star-formation is associated with powerful AGN-driven outflows with mass loading factors $\eta \gtrsim$ 1 - 10. The neutral gas dominates the outflow mass budget but in most cases its velocity is below the halo escape velocity, indicating that this gas may form part of a galactic fountain. Detections of neutral gas inflows and residual cold gas reservoirs in quiescent galaxies suggest that other feedback mechanisms such as AGN heating are required to keep galaxies quenched over long timescales. We highlight the potential of multiphase outflow measurements to constrain feedback models, describe the main systematic uncertainties in mass outflow rate measurements, and discuss how the $z\sim 2$ results inform our understanding of the formation of the earliest massive quiescent galaxies.
#### The Contribution of Mergers and Accretion to Dark Matter Halo Assembly: An Excursion Set Theory Approach
 - **Authors:** Hamed Kameli
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA); Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19312

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.19312

 - **Abstract**
 We study the relative contributions of mergers and accretion to the assembly of dark matter haloes within the framework of Excursion Set Theory (EST). We extend a variance-discretized merger-tree algorithm, which recursively reconstructs all branches of a complete merger tree and introduces a merger-ratio criterion for distinguishing mergers from accretion. The method is validated by comparing the resulting progenitor distributions with the analytical conditional first up-crossing distribution, showing excellent agreement over a wide range of halo masses and redshifts. We find that the accretion contribution depends strongly on the adopted merger-ratio threshold. For large merger ratios ($MR=0.1$--$0.25$), accretion accounts for up to $\sim70\%$ of the mass growth of massive haloes, whereas including all resolved mergers ($MR=10^{-6}$) reduces the accretion contribution to $\sim40\%$. These values are in good agreement with previous N-body simulation results. The accreted mass exhibits a nearly self-similar distribution in the mass-redshift plane, with the dominant contribution arising from progenitors in the range $10^{-3}M_0 \lesssim M \lesssim 10^{-1}M_0$ and predominantly at $0.5 \lesssim z \lesssim 2$. Our results demonstrate that EST-based merger trees provide an accurate and computationally efficient framework for investigating halo assembly and quantifying the relative roles of mergers and accretion in hierarchical structure formation.
#### Large-scale Environmental Dependence of AGN Activity Revealed by JWST Pa$α$ Emitters
 - **Authors:** Danyang Jiang, Linhua Jiang, Shuqi Fu, Zijian Zhang, Jie Chen, Chenxi Bao, Yihe Wang, Junya Arita, Darshan Kakkad, Jan-Torge Schindler
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19320

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.19320

 - **Abstract**
 The role of the large-scale environment in regulating AGN activity remains an open question. We investigate the Mpc-scale environments of 70 AGNs at $z=1.1$-$1.5$ using a highly complete sample of 3,419 Pa$\alpha$ emitters with accurate spectroscopic redshifts from JWST slitless spectroscopic survey. This dataset allows us to robustly trace the environments and quantify the environmental dependence of AGN properties. We measure the eighth-nearest-neighbor surface density of Pa$\alpha$ emitters around each AGN, probing $\sim$1.5 pMpc scale environments. We find that this environmental density exhibits a weak positive correlation with radio luminosity. Radio-bright AGNs tend to reside in statistically denser environments than their radio-faint counterparts. The environmental density also has a positive correlation with the Eddington ratio and a weak negative correlation with the central black hole mass, implying more efficient black hole accretion in dense environments. We construct Pa$\alpha$ luminosity functions (LFs) in AGN environments and find that they are systematically elevated relative to the field Pa$\alpha$ LF. In addition, their faint-end slopes are shallower, suggesting accelerated galaxy growth and enhanced quenching of star formation. As Pa$\alpha$ emitters trace gas-rich, star-forming galaxies, our results support a scenario in which the large-scale cold gas promotes AGN activity at cosmic noon.
#### Simulations of Dust Content and Attenuation in $z=5-15$ Galaxies
 - **Authors:** Dhruv T. Zimmerman, Jed McKinney, Desika Narayanan, Caitlin M. Casey, John Chisholm, Alex M. Garcia, Michelle C. Jecmen, Jessica Kelley-Derzon, Federico Marinacci, Julian B. Muñoz, Laura V. Sales, Ethan Savitch, Paul Torrey
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19379

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.19379

 - **Abstract**
 Correctly modeling the dust attenuation law in galaxies is essential to properly inferring their growth and physical properties. With the rapid discovery of $z>6$ galaxies by JWST and lack of constraints on the properties of dust at these redshifts, we must turn to theoretical models to predict the properties of dust at these redshifts. In this paper, we develop a theoretical foundation for the nature of high-redshift attenuation laws. We analyze $107$ cosmological zoom-in simulations of galaxies run with the SMUGGLE galaxy formation model. SMUGGLE includes an explicitly modeled multiphase interstellar medium and a state-of-the-art dust model with a self-consistently evolving dust grain size distribution. We forward model these simulations with the radiative transfer package POWDERDAY to produce synthetic spectra and attenuation curves for a set of $z=5-15$, $M_*\sim10^{6.5}-10^{10}\; \mathrm{M}_{\odot}$ galaxies. We find evidence for flatter attenuation curves than the commonly used locally-calibrated literature attenuation laws assumed when modeling high-redshift galaxies. The vast majority of our attenuation curves also lack the 2175 Angstrom absorption feature. Our attenuation laws do not evolve strongly over $z=15-5$, which we attribute to relatively constant fractions of small grains up to $z=4$ in our simulations. We present a set of simulation-informed priors on dust attenuation law parameterization that could be implemented in SED fitting codes. There is an anti-correlation between galaxy mass and attenuation curve slope and positive correlation between galaxy mass and $A_V$, which we attribute to dust growth becoming efficient in our massive galaxies with $\log{M_*/\mathrm{M}_{\odot}}\gtrsim9$ and with ISM conditions above a critical metallicity threshold.
#### Barflow: Modeling Gas Kinematics of Bar-Driven Flows
 - **Authors:** Seungwon Baek, Woong-Tae Kim, Taehyun Kim, Dajeong Jang, SungWon Kwak
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19567

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.19567

 - **Abstract**
 We present self-consistent $N$-body/hydrodynamical simulations of isolated, Milky Way-like galaxies with stellar and gaseous disks. By varying the initial gas fraction, we find that gas delays bar formation and, in the most gas-rich model, suppresses it by exciting higher-order spiral modes and promoting early central mass growth. Once a bar forms, continued gas inflow and gravitational torques weaken the bar and inhibit its secular slowdown, keeping bars in the gas-bearing models shorter and predominantly in the fast-bar regime, while the gas-free bar evolves into the slow-bar regime. Stars and gas respond differently to the same barred potential. While the mean stellar velocity field is well described by a smooth bisymmetric pattern, the cold and warm gas follow elongated, $x_1$-like streamlines with sharp shock-induced velocity transitions, producing sawtooth-like radial and archlike tangential velocity profiles. Despite these strong noncircular motions, their azimuthally averaged tangential velocities closely follow the circular velocity over most of the disk. By contrast, the hot gas is dominated by stellar feedback and thermal pressure and is largely decoupled from the ordered bar-driven flow. To describe these bar-driven gas flows, we introduce the barflow model, a phenomenological description of non-sinusoidal gas streaming motions with a radially varying shock-front phase. Mock line-of-sight velocity-fitting experiments show that the barflow model recovers the intrinsic rotation curve, bar-driven velocity perturbations, and radial gas transport more accurately than the conventional bisymmetric model.

 - **Abstract**
 The aim of this study is to explore methods for how to identify new quasars with different properties with regard to reddening, redshift, the presence of broad absorption lines from outflows, or other rare properties affecting the broad-band colours. We use Gaia-based astrometric pre-selection to study only sources that have zero parallax and zero proper motion within errors. We subsequently match these sources with information from wide-area photometric surveys and build colour-colour diagrams. Based on these diagrams we select targets for follow-up spectroscopy. In the subsequent analysis we also apply observations from SPHEREx to firmly establish redshifts. We secure spectroscopic observations for 24 sources. Of these, three are found to be stars and the remaining 21 are classified as quasars spanning a redshift range from z=0.139 to z=3.732. The majority of the sources located away from the normal quasar locus in colour-colour diagrams are found to be broad-absorption-line quasars. A single source, the lowest redshift source at z=0.139, is reminiscent of the so-called Little Red Dots found at high redshifts with JWST. Using the simple procedure followed here it is possible to locate previously undiscovered quasars with interesting properties, in particular Broad Absorption Line quasars and quasars with unusual extinction.
#### specwizard: A Public Python Framework for Creating Synthetic Absorption Spectra from Hydrodynamical Simulations
 - **Authors:** Andrés Arámburo-García, Tom Theuns, Joop Schaye, Alexey Boyarsky
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO); Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA); Instrumentation and Methods for Astrophysics (astro-ph.IM)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19862

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.19862

 - **Abstract**
 We present specwizard, a publicly available modular Python package for generating synthetic absorption spectra from hydrodynamical simulations. The code combines atomic data from the National Institute of Standards and Technology database with ionisation fractions computed using the cloudy spectral synthesis code for multiple ionising backgrounds, enabling consistent modelling of bound--bound and bound--free absorption for resonant transitions of all ions. specwizard employs a column-density--conserving projection scheme, includes natural line broadening for the Lyman series, and offers optional treatments of instrumental resolution and noise for direct comparison with high-resolution quasar spectra. As example applications of the code, we shoot sightlines through the eagle cosmological simulation to generate a z = 3.2 synthetic quasar spectrum containing H i Lyman-series transitions, including damping wings and Lyman-limit absorption. Additionally, we compute metal-absorption lines probing the circumgalactic medium of simulated galaxies, and investigate the correlated absorption of H i and Si iii. We demonstrate that specwizard applied to outputs from eagle accurately reproduces key observational diagnostics of the Lyman-alpha forest, including the redshift evolution of the mean transmission, the transmission PDF and the transmission power spectrum. We take advantage of the fact that the eagle simulation also reproduces the measured evolution of the temperature-density relation to infer the evolution of the amplitude of the ionizing background, finding excellent agreement with recent theoretical models and measurements.
#### Sleeping or Dead? Probing the Quenching Mechanism of JWST Dormant Galaxies at z > 3 with ALMA
 - **Authors:** Mengyuan Xiao, Songnan Qi, Pascal A. Oesch, Longji Bing, Miroslava Dessauges-Zavadsky, Andreas L. Faisst, Mahsa Sanati, Rashmi Gottumukkala, Gabriel Brammer, David Elbaz, Robert Feldmann, Yoshinobu Fudamoto, Lucas C. Kimmig, Benjamin Magnelli, Rui Marques-Chaves, Anurag Amol Sawarkar, Sune Toft, Francesco Valentino, Christina C. Williams
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19926

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.19926

 - **Abstract**
 JWST has revealed a population of "dormant'' galaxies at z>3 with strong Balmer breaks and weak emission lines, whose star formation appears to have declined only recently. Whether these galaxies are undergoing a lasting transition toward quiescence or merely experiencing a temporary suppression of star formation remains unclear. We investigate their molecular gas content using archival ALMA observations of [CII]158um and CO rotational transitions. Starting from all publicly available JWST/NIRSpec prism observations and using joint spectrophotometric fitting, we construct a parent sample at z>3 and log(Mstar/Msun)>9.5 in the ALMA-accessible COSMOS, UDS, and GOODS-South fields. Our final sample comprises 7 dormant galaxies and 15 quiescent galaxies with ALMA constraints on their molecular gas content (mostly 3 sigma upper limits). We find that dormant galaxies exhibit systematically lower star formation rates averaged over the past 10Myr (SFR10Myr) than over the past 100Myr (SFR100Myr), supporting a recent rapid decline in their star formation. For the ~70% of the sample with sufficiently constraining ALMA observations, their molecular gas fractions lie at least ~0.3-2dex below those expected for main-sequence galaxies. Moreover, the molecular gas fractions of dormant galaxies show no systematic difference from those of quiescent galaxies in our sample. This indicates that dormant galaxies have already reached a gas-poor state shortly after quenching and favors a scenario in which the rapid shutdown of star formation is associated with depletion or removal of the molecular gas reservoir, rather than primarily with suppressed star formation efficiency while retaining a substantial molecular gas reservoir. Our results suggest that, if no substantial gas replenishment occurs in the future, dormant galaxies are more consistent with becoming "dead'' than with being temporarily "sleeping''.

 - **Authors:** Tianmu Gao, J. Trevor Mendel, Letizia Bugiani, Minjung Park, Sirio Belli, Joel Leja, Claudia del P. Lagos, Amir H. Khoram, Rebecca L. Davies, Razieh Emami, Gabriel Maheson, Anita Zanella, Benjamin P. Collins

 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19937

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.19937

 - **Abstract**
 JWST observations of massive quiescent galaxies (MQGs) at $z\sim2$ have revealed diverse star formation histories (SFHs). In this letter, we investigate whether these variations are linked to the environment in which MQGs reside, as quantified by the host dark matter halo mass. We identify 12 MQGs from a representative sample of galaxies at $1.6<z<3.5$ obtained by the Blue Jay survey, a JWST Cycle 1 program, and infer their SFHs via modelling of the deep NIRSpec medium-resolution spectra along with multi-band photometry. #### Galactic Morpholography: Linking present-day galaxy compactness to assembly history and relic formation in the COLIBRE simulations
 - **Authors:** Maximilian Zemsch, Claudia D.P. Lagos, Chiara Spiniello, Michele Cappellari, Ángel Chandro-Gómez, Katy L. Proctor, Evgenii Chaikin, Johanna Hartke, Filip Huško, Sylvia Ploeckinger, Alexander J. Richings, Mahsa Sanati, Joop Schaye, James W. Trayford
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19952

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.19952

 - **Abstract**
 Compactness is widely used to identify candidate relic galaxies, the largely untouched survivors of the first compact massive galaxies. Yet relicness is an evolutionary rather than structural property. Using the COLIBRE cosmological hydrodynamical simulations, we test whether present-day structure traces galaxy formation and assembly histories. Six complementary tracers (stellar age, metallicity, [Mg/Fe], sSFR, velocity dispersion and ex-situ mass fraction) vary coherently across the stellar mass-size plane and independently identify a characteristic compactness scale of $\log_{10}(\Sigma_{1.5,\mathrm{crit}}/(\mathrm{M}_\odot\mathrm{kpc}^{-1.5}))=9.75\pm0.10$. We term this mapping between present-day structure and assembly history morpholography. Combining this threshold with the Degree of Relicness ($\mathrm{DoR}>0.6$), which quantifies early and rapid star formation, we distinguish compact from ancient galaxies and identify 267 relic candidates at their intersection. Ancient galaxies span nearly the full ex-situ-fraction range, from predominantly in-situ formation to substantial merger-driven growth. Relic candidates are concentrated at stellar masses of $\approx 10^{10}$-$10^{11}$ solar masses, host black holes that lie $\approx 0.25$-$2.5$ dex above the median black-hole-stellar-mass relation, and are predominantly satellites (76%), preferentially in cluster environments. Only $\approx 3$% show evidence of strong tidal stripping, suggesting that environment primarily preserves rather than creates relic-like systems. Approximately one third (87/267) are direct descendants of the $z=2$ massive quiescent galaxies identified in COLIBRE. Present-day structure thus retains a statistical fossil record of assembly history, supporting morpholography as a link between nearby galaxies and the early Universe.
#### Galaxy-to-galaxy stochasticity has a limited impact on reionization and the 21-cm signal at fixed mean emissivity
 - **Authors:** Shihai Zhu, Yuxiang Qin
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20184

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.20184

 - **Abstract**
 Galaxy-to-galaxy stochasticity may be less important for reionization, IGM heating, and the 21-cm signal than commonly assumed. We find that most of its apparent impact arises because introducing or removing scatter changes the mean emissivity of the galaxy population, rather than from redistributing radiation at fixed mean emissivity. We use the Meraxes semi-analytic model to separate these effects. For ionising sources, we vary the scatter in the escape fraction and in the SFR-halo mass relation across three source prescriptions. For the X-ray heating sources, we introduce luminosity scatter and also separately remove SFR scatter. Without recalibration, these modifications shift the mean emissivities, changing the timing of reionization and IGM heating and producing substantial differences in the 21-cm signal. Once the relevant mean emissivities are restored, however, the global ionizing and thermal histories converge and the power-spectrum differences fall below the forecast sensitivity of a deep SKA-Low observation. There are some weak changes in ionisation morphology, depending on which galaxies dominate the ionising emissivity and on their response to photoheating. Within the models and scales considered here, calibrating the mean radiative emissivities is therefore substantially more important than resolving their galaxy-to-galaxy scatter.
#### Rotochemical heating of neutron stars in 2D
 - **Authors:** Mikhail V. Beznogov, Jerome Novak, Dany Page, Adriana R. Raduta
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20423

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.20423

 - **Abstract**
 The matter inside a spinning-down neutron star (NS) undergoes a slow compression that may drive it out of chemical equilibrium and, consequently, trigger non-equilibrium nuclear reactions that can heat up the star. This phenomenon is called rotochemical heating, and it is relatively well-investigated in the literature. Historically, it has been studied almost exclusively via 1D spherically symmetric models. However, rapidly rotating NSs are not spherically symmetric. We present pioneering simulations of rotochemical heating in full General Relativity in 2D axially symmetric models without relying on the slow rotation approximation. We explicitly follow thermal, mechanical, and chemical evolution of rotating NSs as they spin down taking into account both radial and angular distributions of temperature, composition, and mechanical structure. The simplest model is employed: only nucleonic composition, only the non-equilibrium modified Urca process as the main heating/cooling mechanism, and no superfluidity. We analyze the impact of the magnetic field strength as well as the effects of the heat blanketing envelope, initial rotation frequency, NS mass, and core EOS. Most of our conclusions coincide with the results of previous works on this topic, with one important exception. We have found a strong dependence of the cooling curves during the rotochemical heating phases on the core EOS, whereas in the literature it was regarded as very weak. The most likely explanation for this discrepancy is that the authors of the previous works have not considered EOSs with wide enough range of variation of the symmetry energy as a function of density.
#### Joint Line Intensity Recovery from Multi-Tracer Cross-Correlations
 - **Authors:** Carlos E. Sierra, Kirit S. Karkare
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO); Instrumentation and Methods for Astrophysics (astro-ph.IM)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20467

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.20467

 - **Abstract**
 Line intensity mapping (LIM) provides a unique window into the high-redshift universe, but the separation of signals from overlapping emission lines and bright continuum foregrounds remains a central analysis challenge. At millimeter wavelengths in particular, [CII], [CI] and numerous CO transitions overlap with comparable brightness, leading to significant spectral confusion. This work presents a joint likelihood framework that incorporates multi-tracer cross-correlations both between distinct LIM tracers and with external galaxy surveys. By jointly fitting all auto- and cross-spectra between the LIM and galaxy data, this approach combines the spatial and spectral information in each dataset to break degeneracies between overlapping lines and recover individual bias-weighted line intensities at fixed cosmology, even at redshifts where no direct cross-correlation is available. We validate this framework on realistic mocks of an extended SPT-SLIM-like survey spanning 125-175 GHz over 100 deg$^2$, combined with an LSST Y1-like photometric galaxy catalog, and recover CO(2-1) through CO(6-5) and [CI](1-0) over $0.3\lesssim z\lesssim4$ in the presence of bright continuum foregrounds. We demonstrate that adding the photometric galaxy data tightens line constraints by a factor of $\sim$3-4 where the galaxies overlap the lines in redshift, and by $\sim$20-50\% at higher redshifts beyond the galaxy coverage, where the gain comes indirectly from constraining the low-redshift interlopers. These results highlight external tracer overlap as a primary consideration when optimizing the frequency coverage and footprint of near-future LIM experiments.
#### A Step-Like Transition Underlying the Life and Death of Galaxies
 - **Authors:** Zhicheng He, Enci Wang, Luis C. Ho, Yingjie Peng, Huiyuan Wang, Yong Shi, Tao Wang, Yongquan Xue, Tinggui Wang
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA); Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20505

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.20505

 - **Abstract**
 How galaxies stop forming stars is a complex and central problem in astrophysics. Numerous physical mechanisms have been proposed, yet a simple, unified framework connecting these mechanisms remains lacking. Here, we derive a step-function quenching response from the fundamental principle of gas mass conservation, governed by the competition between gas-supply and gas-depletion timescales. This response also enables the gas-supply timescale to be inferred from the observed quenched fraction. Our work establishes a unified physical framework in which a simple step-function response captures how diverse physical processes regulate galaxy quenching and the resumption of star formation through their effects on the competition between gas-supply and gas-depletion timescales.

#### Environmental dependence in the halo model informed by the FLAMINGO simulations
 - **Authors:** G. Martín Girardi, D. Rana, H. Hoekstra, J. Schaye, M. Schaller
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23997

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.23997

 - **Abstract**
 Galaxy-galaxy lensing (GGL) measurements around galaxy samples in different environments provide valuable information about the galaxy-halo connection. In such studies the halo model is commonly used to estimate physical parameters from the lensing signal, but the impact of environment is generally not considered. Our aim is to adapt the halo model to allow a density-split GGL analysis and to understand the environmental dependence of the elements of the model. We use the FLAMINGO suite of cosmological hydrodynamical simulations to construct samples of halos in underdense and overdense regions. Environment density is assigned to halos based on halo counts within an aperture, employing different aperture sizes to probe both local and large scales. We utilize spherical apertures with radii between $1 h^{-1} {\rm Mpc}$ and $10 h^{-1} {\rm Mpc}$. We find a strong dependence on environment for the halo mass function (HMF) and the halo bias. To account for this, we propose an empirical modification that can be applied to existing HMF prescriptions. We find that after a density split, the halo bias is approximately constant with mass. There is also an environmental dependence for the stellar-to-halo-mass relation (SHMR), the conditional stellar mass function, and the mass-concentration relation, all of which can be addressed with existing models. We evaluate the impact of overlooking environment on halo occupation distribution (HOD) parameter inference, finding that it leads to biased estimations of the SHMR and the satellite fraction. These results emphasize the need to review the assumptions adopted in the halo model when working with samples of galaxies binned by environment, and consequently, by galaxy properties that correlate with environment, such as morphology and color.
#### Consistently Reproducing Early Galaxy Observations and 21-cm Signal during the Epoch of Reionization
 - **Authors:** Michele Bianco, Sambit K. Giri, Arghyadeep Basu\orcidlink, Tommaso Boschi, Aurel Schneider, Enrico Garaldi, Garrelt Mellema
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24064

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.24064

 - **Abstract**
 In the standard cosmological model, galaxies forming within dark matter haloes are the primary drivers of the Epoch of Reionization (EoR). The complex physics of galaxy formation introduces scatter in the relation between halo mass and star-formation activity, with implications for the production and escape of ionising photons. In this work, we implement a stochastic source model in our cosmological radiative transfer simulations. We calibrate our model against the high-redshift ultraviolet luminosity function observed by JWST and observational constraints on Lyman-continuum leakage to constrain the ionising escape fraction. We find that stochastic star formation redistributes ionising emissivity toward lower-mass haloes, producing a more fragmented ionisation topology and delaying the growth of the largest ionised regions relative to the deterministic, observationally calibrated model. However, when the two models match total ionising photon production, their global neutral fractions and 21-cm power spectrum remain nearly indistinguishable, despite small differences in the morphology of individual ionised regions. Additionally, we establish a unified framework that lets us apply source models derived from computationally expensive hydrodynamical simulations (SPICE, FIRE-2 and Thesan) and investigate their impact on large-volume reionisation simulations, extending their prescriptions to scales of >100 Mpc. We find that these prescriptions produce distinct ionisation topologies and 21-cm statistical signatures, even when their global reionisation histories are similar. Our results highlight the importance of combining physically motivated galaxy formation prescriptions from hydrodynamical simulations with large-volume radiative transfer simulations to interpret forthcoming 21-cm observations and constrain the sources of cosmic reionisation.
#### Can Blue UV-Bright Galaxies Drive Reionization at Cosmic Dawn? An $M_{\rm UV}=-19.6$ Galaxy with $f_{\rm esc}\approx0.85$ at $z=9.244$
 - **Authors:** Yunjing Wu, Sijia Cai, Yongda Zhu, Masami Ouchi, Yuichi Harikane, Zheng Cai, Mengtao Tang, Khee-Gan Lee, John D. Silverman, Fengwu Sun, Xiaohui Fan, Zuyi Chen, Minami Nakane, Hiroto Yanagisawa, Angela Adamo, Fuyan Bian, Jaclyn B.  - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA); Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24092

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.24092

 - **Abstract**
 We revisit EBG-1, a gravitationally lensed galaxy at $z = 9.244$, using newly obtained deep (14 hr) JWST/NIRSpec G235M ($R \sim 1000$) spectroscopy. EBG-1 is one of the bluest galaxies observed at $z > 9$. Along with its remarkably blue continuum of $\beta = -2.99 \pm 0.15$, we find very weak nebular emission from EBG-1. We obtain a $3\sigma$ upper limit of EW $< 8.4$ Angstrom for C III], a marginal H$\beta$ measurement (EW $= 256.2 \pm 122.2$ Angstrom), and a $5\sigma$ detection of [O III] $\lambda 5007$ (EW $= 882.8 \pm 180.6$ Angstrom). These lines suggest an oxygen abundance of $12 + \log(\mathrm{O/H}) = 7.16 \pm 0.25$ ($\simeq 3\% Z_{\odot}$) and $\log(\mathrm{C/O}) < -0.88$ ($3\sigma$), placing EBG-1 on the C/O-metallicity sequence traced by lower-redshift galaxies. To constrain the escape fraction ($f_{\rm esc}$), we compare the observed UV slope and emission-line equivalent widths with synthetic spectra generated using BEAGLE. The models favor $f_{\rm esc} \gtrsim 0.75$, with $f_{\rm esc} \approx 0.85$ providing the best overall agreement with the observations. These results suggest that relatively UV-bright galaxies like EBG-1 can achieve the exceptionally high ionizing efficiencies supported by late reionization scenarios.
#### A 4-20 GHz Superconducting 90deg Hybrid Using Capacitively Loaded Coupled Lines for 2SB Heterodyne Receivers
 - **Authors:** François Joint, Max Behrens, Denis Meledin, Cristian Lopez, Magnus Strandberg, Sven-Erik Ferm, Mathias Fredrixon, Leif Helldner, Alexei Pavolotsky, Erik Sundin, Igor Lapkin, Victor Belitsky, Vincent Desmaris
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Instrumentation and Methods for Astrophysics (astro-ph.IM); Instrumentation and Detectors (physics.ins-det)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24859

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.24859

 - **Abstract**
 We report the design and cryogenic characterisation of a broadband superconducting 90deg intermediate-frequency (IF) hybrid covering 4 to 20 GHz for sideband-separating (2SB) heterodyne receivers. The hybrid is implemented in thin-film niobium using capacitively loaded edge coupled transmission lines, avoiding the air-bridge interconnections required in Lange-coupler layouts while retaining a compact superconducting implementation with low loss and good quadrature balance. Cryogenic measurements using Vector Network Analyser (VNA) show amplitude and phase imbalance better than 0.6 dB and 6deg, respectively, across the full band. When used in a 2SB receiver, this level of IF balance is consistent with the observed image-rejection performance in the 10-20 dB range, for the full receiver. The design supports the development of wide-instantaneous-bandwidth 2SB receivers for millimeter and submillimeter observatories, improving spectral grasp for broadband line surveys and high-redshift line searches.
#### Connecting galaxy structure, quenching, and halo environments during cosmic noon
 - **Authors:** Ziwen Zhang, Cheng Jia, Huiyuan Wang, Enci Wang, Yangyao Chen, Houjun Mo, Xiong Luo, Cheqiu Lyu, Jie Song
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25446

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.25446

 - **Abstract**
 The connection between galaxy structure, star formation activity, and large-scale environments provides important insights into the evolution of massive galaxies. However, the relative importance of different galaxy properties in predicting quenching and the environmental dependence of structurally distinct galaxy populations remain poorly constrained at cosmic noon ($1\leq z<3$). In this work, we investigate massive galaxies with $\log M_*/{\rm M}_{\odot}>10.5$ at $1\leq z<3$ using deep JWST/NIRCam imaging from the COSMOS-Web survey, combining measurements of central stellar mass surface density ($\Sigma_1$), star formation activity, and galaxy clustering. We find that $\Sigma_1$ is the most informative predictor of quenching among the considered properties, including stellar mass, effective radius, formation redshift, and local overdensity. The quenched fraction shows a strong dependence on $\Sigma_1$, increasing rapidly above a characteristic $\Sigma_1$. We further show that compact quenched galaxies exhibit a tendency toward stronger clustering than compact and extended star-forming galaxies, while compact and extended star-forming galaxies show similar clustering properties. These results highlight the importance of $\Sigma_1$ in tracing quenching and reveal that structural diversity among star-forming galaxies is not strongly reflected in their large-scale environments, providing new constraints on the interplay between galaxy structure, quenching, and halo environments during cosmic noon.
#### The DARWIN Project: Cosmological Simulations of Galaxy Formation down to Dwarf Scales in Diverse Environments
 - **Authors:** Jaehyun Lee, Kyungwon Chun, Taysun Kimm, Yonghwi Kim, Jihye Shin, Ena Choi, Sungwook E. Hong, Myoungwon Jeon, Hannah Jhee, Hyeonyong Kim, Ji-hoon Kim, Juhan Kim, Oh-Kyoung Kwon, Hyunmi Song, Yohan Dubois, Ji-Hoon Ha, Daniel Han, San Han, Jun Yong Park, Christophe Pichon
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25786

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.25786

 - **Abstract**
 We present the DARWIN project, a suite of cosmological simulations with RAMSES to study dwarf-galaxy formation and evolution across diverse environments. DARWIN-1 is a full-box simulation in a cubic volume of $(65.5\,{\rm cMpc})^3$ with a minimum cell size of 500pc, while DARWIN-2 comprises zoom-in simulations of 24 field regions and a Milky Way analog connected to a filament, reaching 62.5pc resolution. The simulations employ baryonic physics tailored to their aims. For DARWIN-2, we include radiative transfer in three photon energy bands to trace hydrogen and helium ionization. DARWIN-1 reproduces the cosmic star formation rate density and galaxy stellar mass functions reasonably well at $z>1$. Both simulations broadly reproduce observed stellar mass-halo mass relations, chemical abundances, star-formation surface densities, and black hole-galaxy scaling relations. At lower redshift, DARWIN-1 shows an excess in the number density of massive galaxies, although the inferred stellar content of massive halos depends on the definition of galaxy stellar mass. Other systematic differences arise from spatial resolution, feedback efficiency, and environmental selection. To facilitate comparison with observations, we generate mock images using the radiative transfer codes SKIRT and Powderday. We also explore a machine-learning approach to mitigate resolution-driven biases in the dwarf galaxy properties of DARWIN-1 using the higher-resolution DARWIN-2 simulations. The DARWIN simulation suite therefore provides a comprehensive numerical framework for studying the formation, evolution, and internal physics of dwarf galaxies in a cosmological context.

 - **Abstract**
 Following recent observations of unexpectedly massive galaxies at high redshift by the James Webb Space Telescope, the revision of Dark Matter (DM) models --specifically regarding how DM couples to gravity-- has become a central topic in Cosmology. In this paper, we develop an axion model for Cold Dark Matter (CDM) with a non-minimal coupling to gravity. From this framework, we evaluate the gravitational instability in the hydrodynamic limit. #### Linear gas mass density: a unifying descriptor of Cosmic Filaments
 - **Authors:** Daniela Galárraga-Espinosa, Daisuke Nagai, Isabel Medlock, Mathieu Langer, Nabila Aghanim, Kentaro Nagamine
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26306

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.26306

 - **Abstract**
 Cosmic filaments connect the nodes of the large-scale web and are thought to harbour a substantial fraction of the low-z baryon budget, yet a physically motivated framework for organising their diverse gas properties has been lacking. Here we show that the linear mass density $\Lambda$ = M_gas/L_fil, measured within the entropy radius r_K (the peak of the radial gas entropy profile) plays this role, analogous to mass in halo physics. Using TNG300-1 at z=0, we characterise the structural, thermodynamic, and chemical properties of 5637 filaments spanning more than two decades in $\Lambda$. Filaments form a continuous thermodynamic sequence with $\Lambda$, ranging from long, thin, cool, metal-poor structures to shorter, wider, hotter, and more enriched ones, with core temperatures spanning two orders of magnitude across the full WHIM window. The ratio of mass- to volume-weighted temperature reveals a shift in heat distribution: low-$\Lambda$ filaments have their hotter gas preferentially associated with denser material, whereas in high-$\Lambda$ filaments the hotter gas is diffuse. Well-defined scaling relations connect $\Lambda$ to temperature, electron density, pressure, entropy, and metallicity, with the T-$\Lambda$ scaling qualitatively consistent with gravitational heating. We present the first measurement of the gaseous linear mass density function, revealing a broad distribution with a flat low-$\Lambda$ regime and a steep exponential decline at high $\Lambda$, analogous to the halo mass function. The integrated Compton-y parameter $Y^{\rm int}$ scales systematically with $\Lambda$, providing a tSZ proxy accessible to current surveys. Finally, galaxy richness per unit length correlates tightly with $\Lambda$ (Pearson r=0.80), enabling $\Lambda$-based classification of filaments from spectroscopic surveys alone and guiding targeted searches for the missing baryons in the cosmic web.
#### Star formation history of Draco revealed by DESI DR1: suppressed early star formation efficiency for Milky Way dwarf galaxies
 - **Authors:** Yujiao Yang, Maosheng Xiang, Yang Huang, Meng Zhang, Lanya Mou, Chao Liu, Jifeng Liu
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26375

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.26375

 - **Abstract**
 Dwarf galaxies serve as fossils for understanding the early star formation history of the local Universe. In this work, we use stellar abundances for individual stars derived from the first data release of the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI DR1) to investigate the star formation history of the dwarf spheroidal galaxy Draco. We simultaneously fit the stellar metallicity distribution function (MDF) and the [Mg/Fe]-[Fe/H] trend using the galactic chemical evolution (GCE) model flexCE. Our best-fit models imply a low star formation efficiency (SFE) of $0.003$ to $0.007\,\mathrm{Gyr}^{-1}$, which is about ten times lower than values reported in the literature, but consistent with recent SFE estimates for ultra-faint dwarf galaxies. By combining recent literature measurements, we find that the SFEs of Milky Way dwarf galaxies scale with stellar mass as $\mathrm{SFE}\propto M_\star^{0.24}$. A low star formation efficiency is associated with a strong outflow efficiency; we estimate a large wind mass-loading factor $\eta_{\mathrm{wind}}$ of approximately 172-260. This is well in line with the observed outflow-stellar mass relation and with the Feedback in Realistic Environment (FIRE) cosmological simulations. Our results demonstrate the power of large-scale, low-to-medium resolution stellar spectroscopic surveys to deliver robust constraints on the early formation history of galaxies in the local Universe.
#### Mean-Field Backreaction in Single-Field Inflation
 - **Authors:** Matteo Braglia, Gabriele Franciolini, Nadir Ijaz, Marco Peloso
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO); General Relativity and Quantum Cosmology (gr-qc)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26669

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.26669

 - **Abstract**
 The validity of linear perturbation theory and the role of backreaction in single-field inflationary models that enhance power at small scales have recently been under debate. We investigate scalar backreaction semi-analytically in a mean-field approach within the decoupling limit, considering popular models with a transient ultra-slow-roll (USR) stage or an oscillatory feature (OF) in the potential. We find that ultraviolet (UV) modes in the Bunch--Davies vacuum can substantially affect backreaction estimates unless removed by adiabatic subtraction. Our mean-field treatment can reproduce recent lattice results, but reveals a spurious sensitivity to UV contributions in some cases. This sensitivity is reduced in the OF model when copious particle production separates the amplified physical modes from the UV vacuum tail, making lattice results more reliable in this regime. In USR, instead, adiabatically subtracted spectra yield milder backreaction on the background evolution than previously inferred.

#### Inferring the shapes of low-mass galaxies at $z>6$ from FIREbox$^{HR}$. Where are the disks?
 - **Authors:** Haojie Geng, Robert Feldmann, Jorge Moreno, Xuejian Shen, James Bullock
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26969

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.26969

 - **Abstract**
 Context. The three-dimensional shapes of galaxies provide clues to their formation and evolution. Inferring intrinsic shapes from projected sizes and axis ratios requires assumptions about the population distribution of shape parameters. Aims. We test commonly adopted assumptions in Bayesian shape inference for high-redshift galaxies. Methods. We analyze FIREboxHR galaxies with $\log(M_\star/M_\odot)=6.5-9.5$ at $z\sim6-15$. We measure intrinsic shapes from stellar particles using the minimum covariance determinant (MCD) estimator. We also create mock JWST/NIRCam images, measure half-light radii and axis ratios, and infer intrinsic distributions from the joint distribution of projected axis ratio $q$ and size $\log a$. Results. Low-mass galaxies are predominantly prolate, with a substantial spheroidal component. Intrinsic ellipticity $E$ and triaxiality $T$ are well described by Johnson SB distributions, while intrinsic size $A$ is approximately log-normal. We therefore model $(\mathrm{logit}(E),\mathrm{logit}(T),\log A)$ with a multivariate normal distribution and allow general correlations. This model recovers the particle-measured shape distributions more faithfully than a multivariate normal model in $(E,T,\log A)$. Triaxiality-related parameters remain difficult to constrain from projected data without large samples or informative priors. Conclusions. Bayesian forward modeling can recover intrinsic shape distributions when its population model adequately describes the underlying galaxies. An unsuitable distribution can bias inferences about morphological evolution at high redshift.
#### The ALMA REBELS Survey: The [C II] Luminosity Function of UV-Selected Galaxies at $z\sim7$
 - **Authors:** Thomas Herard-Demanche, Pascal A. Oesch, Rychard Bouwens, Jacqueline A. Hodge, Hiddo S. B. Algera, Manuel Aravena, Karin Cescon, Elisabete da Cunha, Pratika Dayal, Andrea Ferrara, Rebecca Fisher, Yoshinobu Fudamoto, Kasper E. Heintz, Hanae Inami, Andrea Pallottini, Lucie E. Rowland, Renske Smit, Laura Sommovigo, Mauro Stefanon, Michael W. Topping, Paul van der Werf, Ivana F. van Leeuwen
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27052

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.27052

 - **Abstract**
 Luminosity functions (LF) are some of the most basic quantities providing insights into galaxy evolution across cosmic time and enabling a direct comparison between observations and simulations. Here, we present the first [CII]158$\mu$m LF at $z\sim7$ from a sizeable UV-selected ALMA sample. Our analysis makes use of 27 galaxies with significant [CII] emission line detections in the epoch of reionization from the REBELS (Reionization Era Bright Emission Line Survey) ALMA Large Program and its pilot programs. We used these line detections and similar from ALPINE to calibrate UV luminosity-$L_{\mathrm{[CII]}}$ and stellar mass-$L_{\mathrm{[CII]}}$ relations at $z\sim7$. Given the challenge in precisely defining a selection volume for the REBELS sample itself, we rely on published UV LF and stellar mass function (SMF) determinations at $z\sim7$ and three complementary approaches to derive the [CII] LF. The UV LF-based method provides a lower limit on the [CII] LF, due to its missing the most dust-obscured systems. Our SMF-based estimate yields a Schechter function with a characteristic luminosity of $L^*_{\mathrm{[CII]}}=10^{9.11^{+0.11}_{-0.27}} L_\odot$ and a steep faint-end slope. From $z\sim7$ to $z\sim5$, our inferred [CII] LF shows an evolution of +0.3 dex in normalisation and +0.1 dex in $L^*_{\mathrm{[CII]}}$. Our results indicate a general underprediction from models of the abundance of [CII] emitters at high-redshift and thus potentially the number densities of massive yet UV-faint systems implied by our SMF-based method. Additionally, they suggest that 63% of the star formation rate density occurs in massive [CII]-bright systems and that $\sim$60-90% of [CII] emitters at $z\sim7$ may be missed by bright UV selections. This highlights the importance of surveys focusing on especially massive, UV-faint sources at $z>6$.
#### How does a galaxy's quenching probability depend on its black hole's mass?
 - **Authors:** Antonio J. Porras-Valverde, John C. Forbes
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27061

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.27061

 - **Abstract**
 We develop a parametric toy model to explore how black hole mass scatter $\sigma_{\rm BH}$ and the dependence of the quenching probability per unit time on black hole mass, parameterized as a power law with index $q$, jointly control the faint-end slope $\alpha$ of the stellar mass function of quenched central galaxies. By varying $\sigma_{\rm BH}$ and $q$, we identify two regimes. At low scatter, $\sigma_{\rm BH} \lesssim 0.4$ dex, $\alpha$ is strongly sensitive to $q$. Gradual quenching, represented by low $q$, produces shallow slopes, while sharp thresholds, with high $q$, produce steep mass functions, making $\alpha$ a powerful diagnostic of AGN feedback prescriptions. At large scatter, $\sigma_{\rm BH} \gtrsim 0.6$ dex, models with vastly different $q$ converge to similar quenched mass functions, because the intrinsic scatter erases the imprint of the feedback implementation. Comparing to cosmological hydrodynamical simulations and semianalytic models, only models with $\sigma_{\rm BH} \gtrsim 0.4$ dex match the observed faint-end slope of $\alpha \approx 0.7$. {\sc SAGE}, {\sc Dark Sage}, and {\sc COLIBRE}, with $\sigma_{\rm BH}$ of 2.0, 0.6, and 0.4 dex, respectively, reproduce this slope, whereas {\sc IllustrisTNG} and {\sc EAGLE}, with $\sigma_{\rm BH} \lesssim 0.2$ dex, produce steeper slopes. Current observational estimates of $\sigma_{\rm BH}$ allow a wide range of $q \gtrsim 0.8$, so the observed slope alone cannot identify the quenching prescription. If $\sigma_{\rm BH}$ is small, simultaneous measurements of $\alpha$ and $\sigma_{\rm BH}$ can distinguish between competing AGN feedback models.
#### ASTROLABE: A 3D Particle-Level Surrogate for Cosmology Simulation with Physical Flow Matching

 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27068

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.27068

 - **Abstract**
 The microphysics of dark matter (DM) is imprinted on cosmological structure across an enormous dynamic range. Nonlinear cosmological observables are most reliably modeled with N-body and hydrodynamic simulations. However, systematic N-body scans over DM theory space are prohibitively expensive. Machine learning emulators provide a possible solution, but are currently limited by either low resolution or only the ability to emulate specific observables. We introduce Astrolabe, a flow-matching-based generative emulator designed to be free of both constraints. Astrolabe acts directly on the 6N phase-space coordinates of N individual particles. With no voxel grid in the output, there is no voxel-resolution ceiling, and in principle any observable can be measured from inference. Astrolabe is computationally tractable via a field-particle hybrid transformer in which every particle cross-attends to multiscale field tokens, so compute and memory are linear in N. Astrolabe incorporates physical flow matching by taking the prior distribution for the flow to be approximate COmoving Lagrangian Acceleration (COLA)-based coordinates rather than unphysical noise, with momentum and mass conservation enforced at all points along flow time. In the Lambda-CDM cases we test, Astrolabe produces realistic fields with accurate power spectra, halo mass functions, velocity profiles, and halo density profiles. We also observe out-of-distribution generalization, with accurate emulation of dark-acoustic oscillation initial conditions. #### DDELenS: Tomographic Weak Lensing Simulations with Dynamical Dark Energy
 - **Authors:** Shu-Fan Chen, J. Colin Hill, Zoltán Haiman
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27088

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.27088

 - **Abstract**
 We present a suite of 128 cosmological simulations in $w_0w_a$CDM cosmologies with massive neutrinos, designed to enable simulation-based inference of dynamical dark energy parameters from weak gravitational lensing and other cosmological observables. Each simulation follows $1024^3$ dark matter particles in a $512\,h^{-1}$Mpc periodic box, with cosmological parameters sampled using a Sobol sequence over the ranges $\Omega_m \in [0.28, 0.35]$, $\sigma_8 \in [0.74, 0.90]$, $w_0 \in [-1.0, -0.5]$, and $w_a \in [-1.5, 0.0]$, motivated by recent results from the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument survey. The nonlinear evolution is computed with $\texttt{PKDGRAV3}$ using the N-body gauge to consistently incorporate relativistic corrections from massive neutrinos, photons, and dark energy. For each cosmology, we generate 10,000 ray-traced convergence maps at six source redshifts $z_s \in \{0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0, 2.5, 1100\}$ using $\texttt{LensTools}$, and additionally provide halo catalogues and projected halo overdensity fields for galaxy--lensing cross-correlation analyses. We generate 100,000 realizations at the fiducial cosmology for the construction of the covariance matrix. This simulation suite provides a foundation for field-level inference and non-Gaussian statistics with next-generation weak lensing surveys such as the Rubin Observatory LSST, Euclid, and the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope, as well as CMB lensing experiments including the Simons Observatory.
#### Numerical shock broadening and supernova neutrino conversion: implications for DUNE
 - **Authors:** Rafael C. R. de Lima, Pedro Martins Bailer
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27158

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.27158

 - **Abstract**
 Hydrodynamical simulations spread the supernova shock front over a few grid zones, a width set by the numerical scheme rather than by the physics of the shock. Where the front crosses the H resonance, that width can make the computed MSW evolution artificially adiabatic. We quantify the effect on six three-dimensional blue-supergiant explosion models from the Garching archive by propagating individual rays twice: through the stored profile, and with the front replaced by a narrow reconstruction, 2.2 km wide, that approximates a sudden crossing while keeping the densities on either side. The stored fronts are 1.6 to 1.7 grid zones wide. Across twelve configurations, each chosen inside the energy band in which that model's front meets the resonance, the reconstruction raises the ray-averaged crossing probability $P_H$ by 0.44 to 0.76 in absolute terms, median 0.56; narrowing the reconstruction thirty-fold changes each difference by at most 0.055. Along all 12878 rays of one configuration, the sudden-limit formula evaluated on each ray's own densities agrees closely with the propagated reconstructed result. The change is largest when the density jump spans the resonance, over a band of energies that can be estimated from the presupernova density once the shock radius and the compression are known, with tails outside it. In a separate calculation with a parametrised shock and a published emission model, time-dependent conversion changes the $\nu_e$ charged-current count in a 40 kt argon detector by -4.4%, with a sign set by the flavour composition of the source. Folding the reconstructed three-dimensional fronts with a consistent emission model remains to be done.
#### The GLEAM 4-Jy (G4Jy) Sample: V. Broadband spectral analysis
 - **Authors:** Sarah V. White, Siegfried A. Gawenda, Giulia Macario, Samuel P. Hansen, Ross J. Turner, Thomas M.O. Franzen, Joseph R. Callingham, Randall Wayth, Kshitij Thorat
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:

 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27171

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.27171

 - **Abstract**
 The G4Jy Sample consists of 1,863 of the brightest sources in the southern sky, as selected at 151 MHz using the GaLactic and Extragalactic All-sky MWA (GLEAM) survey. We supplement existing, low-frequency radio data by completing visual inspection of radio images from the Rapid ASKAP Continuum Survey (RACS) at 887.5, 1367.5 and 1655.5 MHz. Doing so on a source-by-source basis allows us to assess the quality of the association of the radio emission, which we indicate via a flag system. Having multiple radio flux-densities allows us to construct broadband radio spectral energy distributions (SEDs), and these extend up to 20 GHz for sources that also have data from the Australia Telescope Compact Array (ATCA). We fit polynomial functions to these SEDs, and determine the distribution of sources that are best-fit by absorption models, these accounting for 11 per cent of the sample. The derived source ages show the expected distribution in the radio-power--size (P--D) diagram, whilst derived jet powers show non-linear behaviour. The latter is believed to be the result of higher densities, associated with higher magnetic fields, restricting the expansion of the radio jets/lobes.
#### Searching for Dense Perturbers in Cold Dark Matter Simulations
 - **Authors:** Xingyu Zhang, Hai-Bo Yu, Demao Kong, Ethan O.Nadler
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO); Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA); High Energy Physics - Phenomenology (hep-ph)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27236

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.27236

 - **Abstract**
 Recently, several low-mass, dense perturbers have been inferred from astrophysical observations of gravitational lensing, stellar streams, and satellite galaxies. If interpreted as dark matter subhalos, their inner densities exceed the predictions of the standard cold dark matter (CDM) model. We systematically investigate this tension using a CDM-only zoom-in simulation of a Milky Way analog. We analyze more than 5000 subhalos with masses down to $10^6\,M_\odot$ and reconstruct their inner density profiles using a tidal truncation model. The simulated subhalos are then compared with the observed compact perturbers in terms of their density profiles, enclosed masses, concentrations, and projected cylindrical masses. None of the simulated CDM subhalos is sufficiently dense to reproduce the inferred mass distributions of the observed perturbers. We further examine the redshift evolution of subhalo concentrations and derive a fitting function to characterize this evolution. Our findings motivate further investigations into exceptionally rare formation histories that could produce such dense perturbers, as well as the possibility of physics beyond the standard CDM model.
#### The Future of Gamma-Ray Bursts as Probes of the Epoch of Reionization in the Era of JWST and the Extremely Large Telescope
 - **Authors:** H.M. Fausey, A.J. van der Horst, N.R. Tanvir, K. Wiersema
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27282

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.27282

 - **Abstract**
 The Epoch of Reionization (EoR) is a key era of cosmological history that remains poorly understood. An examination of the Ly-alpha damping wing of high-redshift GRBs can provide direct measurements of the fraction of neutral hydrogen (i.e., neutral fraction) of the IGM in the vicinity of the GRB host galaxy, in addition to the neutral column in the host along the sight-line to the GRB. The latter quantity can help infer the fraction of ionizing photons escaping the galaxy (i.e. escape fraction); the average escape fraction being a key input to EoR models. New and upcoming observatories such as the JWST and the Extremely Large Telescope (ELT) can obtain more robust estimates of the hydrogen column density and neutral fraction around high-redshift GRBs. We simulate spectra for the JWST NIRSpec and ELT HARMONI spectrographs using different GRB magnitudes and exposure times to determine their capabilities for measuring column densities and neutral fractions with the Ly-alpha damping wings of high-redshift GRBs. We find that both spectrographs will be highly effective for obtaining column density estimates. Despite the presence of telluric lines, we find that ELT HARMONI will be a powerful instrument for retrieving accurate neutral fraction estimates, but JWST NIRSpec will be vital for obtaining neutral fraction measurements for bright GRBs that fall in redshift ranges where ELT is less effective. Additionally, shorter JWST Target of Opportunity (ToO) follow-up times would greatly increase the accuracy of neutral fraction estimates obtained with NIRSpec spectra.
#### The chemical evolution of the LMC from APOGEE abundances: a hybrid two-infall OMEGA+ analysis
 - **Authors:** Erik Gasteiger, Viola Hegedűs, Szabolcs Mészáros
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27831

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.27831

 - **Abstract**
 The Large Magellanic Cloud (LMC) provides resolved stellar abundances and star-formation histories that can be used together to test models of dwarf-galaxy chemical evolution. We investigated if the non-monotonic APOGEE $[\mathrm{Mg}/\mathrm{Fe}]$-$[\mathrm{Fe}/\mathrm{H}]$ sequence can be explained by a hybrid history that combines a second gas-infall episode with a temporary increase in star-formation efficiency (SFE). We selected 4933 LMC red-giant-branch stars from APOGEE-2 DR17. We fitted an adaptively binned median Mg sequence with the two-zone open-box code OMEGA+. The formal fitting objective contained only the Mg sequence. The metallicity distribution function (MDF), one-dimensional abundance distributions, star-formation history (SFH), present-day mass partition, SFR, and supernova (SN) rate were retained as posterior diagnostics. The best-fitting model reproduces the Mg sequence with an RMS residual of 0.01 dex and also gives a close posterior match to O. It contains two delayed-tau infall episodes, with the second beginning at $t_2=10.39$ Gyr and with integrated mass ratio $M_2/M_1=0.159$, followed by a temporary Gaussian SFE enhancement centred at $t=11.91$ Gyr. The second infall is represented by a mixture of enriched and primordial gas. The model reproduces the timing of the late SFH enhancement and yields a present SFR within the adopted comparison range. A hybrid second-infall and SFE-enhancement history is a plausible scenario for reproducing the observed LMC Mg median sequence. Our fit explains the LMC evolution fairly well, but the remaining small MDF, mass, and SN-rate discrepancies suggest that a selection-function-aware treatment of the stellar distributions is required for a stricter comparison.
#### Understanding the formation of alpha-Fe bimodality in the Milky Way using constrained cosmological simulations of the Local Group
 - **Authors:** Anna Genina, Ewoud Wempe, Amina Helmi, Hanneke C. Woudenberg, Simon D. M. White, Robert J. J. Grand, Ruediger Pakmor, Guilhem Lavaux, Jens Jasche
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27985

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.27985

 - **Abstract**
 We use a novel suite of constrained cosmological simulations of the Local Group to investigate the formation of alpha--Fe bimodalities in stellar discs of Milky Way analogues. In agreement with past studies using the Auriga model, we show that in our simulations the high-alpha sequence forms in a merger-induced starburst, which further quenches star formation, allowing the new stellar populations to form at low [alpha/Fe]. Producing a bimodality that is prominent at large radii, with a significant [alpha/Fe] gap and overlapping metallicity as in the Milky Way, requires the quenching of star formation across all radii where the bimodality is observed. The quenching can be achieved either with a galaxy-wide starburst, or with a retrograde merger reducing angular momentum in the outer disc and allowing more efficient dilution by the metal-poor gas in the circumgalactic medium (CGM). We show that mergers can also tilt the disc, changing the direction of galactic outflows and enriching a larger volume of the CGM with galactic metals, leading to low [alpha/Fe] scatter in the subsequently formed low-alpha sequence. We argue that the slopes in [alpha/Fe] of the two alpha sequences are crucial in narrowing down the mechanism that leads to their formation. We additionally uncover an indirect role that mergers play in enhancing the accretion of metal-poor gas onto galaxies. The smoothly accreted gas can dominate the gas content in the CGM and aid in the formation of distinct chemical sequences, especially at the low-metallicity end.
#### The clumpy fraction of galaxies at $z=1-9$ with JADES: evidence for a mass-dependent transition in clump origin
 - **Authors:** Dávid Puskás, Sandro Tacchella, Phoebe Chapman, Floor van Donkelaar, Andrew J. Bunker, Courtney Carreira, A. Lola Danhaive, Francesco D'Eugenio, Qiao Duan, Patricia Iglesias-Navarro, Amanda Stoffers, Hannah Übler, Yongda Zhu, Mirko Curti, Emma Curtis-Lake, Kevin Hainline, Eiichi Egami, Daniel J. Eisenstein, Zhiyuan Ji, Benjamin D. Johnson, Roberto Maiolino, Marcia Rieke, Brant Robertson, Christina C. Williams
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28515

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.28515

 - **Abstract**
 We measure the fraction of clumpy galaxies ($f_{\rm clumpy}$) over the redshift range $z=1-9$ and stellar masses $\log_{10}(M_\star/{\rm M_\odot}) \geq 8$ using JWST/NIRCam imaging from JADES. We define clumps as compact off-centre surface-brightness peaks with characteristic sizes of a few hundred parsecs, and develop a dendrogram-based clump-finding method with astrodendro to identify them directly in rest-frame UV, blue, and optical images. Clump-detection completeness is quantified through injection-recovery simulations, which we use to define ($M_\star$, $z$)-dependent 50 per cent completeness-limited $f_{\rm clumpy}$, alongside a probability-weighted completeness-corrected statistic for comparison. For the full sample, the blue and optical $f_{\rm clumpy}$ reach $\approx55$ per cent at $z\approx1-2$ and decline to $\lesssim5$ per cent by $z\approx8-9$, while the UV fraction remains higher at $\approx12$ per cent. At $z\approx1-2$, $f_{\rm clumpy}$ increases strongly with stellar mass, from $\approx35$ per cent in the lowest-mass bin to $\approx85$ per cent at $\log_{10}(M_\star/{\rm M_\odot})\geq10$. Most clumpy galaxies host only one or two robustly detected off-centre clumps, while higher-multiplicity systems occur preferentially in massive, strongly star-forming galaxies at low to intermediate redshift. Comparison with major merger rates suggests that mergers could account for a substantial fraction of low-mass ($\log_{10}(M_\star/{\rm M_\odot})<8.5$), high-redshift clumpy galaxies, while the observed fractions in more massive systems ($\log_{10}(M_\star/{\rm M_\odot})>9$) require an additional in situ clump-formation channel. Our results are consistent with a transition from merger-dominated clumpy morphologies at early times and low stellar masses to an increasing contribution from in situ clump-formation channels, such as disc instabilities, in more massive systems.

#### A Vision for Little Red Dots in the Low-Redshift Universe with ALMA2040
 - **Authors:** Fengwu Sun, Xiaojing Lin, Miguel Pereira-Santaella, Dieu D. Nguyen
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Instrumentation and Methods for Astrophysics (astro-ph.IM); Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20697

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.20697

 - **Abstract**
 The discovery of Little Red Dots (LRDs) at high redshifts with JWST has revealed a previously unknown phase of black-hole growth. However, their black-hole (BH) masses, BH-to-stellar mass ratios, cold-gas reservoirs, and depletion times remain uncertain. The recent discovery of low-redshift LRDs at $z\simeq0.1 - 0.9$ creates a decisive opportunity to access their low-$J$ CO lines for measuring molecular gas reservoirs, and the nearest systems can in principle be spatially resolved. We use J1025+1402 ("The Egg") at $z=0.1007$, the best-studied local LRD, as a benchmark to explore the scientific opportunities with ALMA2040. Assuming a molecular gas mass of $10^9\,M_\odot$, the CO(2-1) detectability strongly depends on the assumed CO-to-H$_2$ conversion ($\alpha_{\rm CO}$). With a large $\alpha_{\rm CO} \simeq 315 - 725\,M_\odot\,({\rm K\,km\,s}^{-1}\,{\rm pc}^2)^{-1}$ at a low gas-phase metallicity, the CO line would remain very challenging for the current ALMA to detect, while ALMA2040 will likely deliver a robust CO detection and thus a gas-reservoir constraint for The Egg. Furthermore, with the best angular resolution at its CO(2-1) frequency (14.6 mas, or 27 pc), ALMA2040 may spatially resolve the CO emission from The Egg. Assuming an optimistic, SMC-like $\alpha_{\rm CO}$ with a 50-hour deep integration, we produce mock position-velocity diagrams that may distinguish a $10^7\,M_\odot$ black hole from the no-BH model through its steeper central velocity structure. We conclude that ALMA2040 has the potential to directly measure the gas masses, depletion times, and excitation of local LRDs, while the CO-bright subset may ultimately yield dynamical constraints on the mass of their central black holes.
#### Efficient numerical evolution of self-gravitating systems with dark-matter-baryon scattering
 - **Authors:** Marc Kamionkowski, Vera Gluscevic, Kris Sigurdson
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO); Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA); High Energy Physics - Phenomenology (hep-ph); High Energy Physics - Theory (hep-th)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20702

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.20702

 - **Abstract**
 We present a fast algorithm to numerically evolve a spherically symmetric system consisting of dark matter and gas, including dark-matter-baryon scattering. The method augments the spherical N-body code NSphere, which collapses the six-dimensional phase space of a spherical collisionless system to three dimensions, with a one-dimensional Lagrangian treatment of gas. Dark-matter-baryon scattering is implemented as a Monte-Carlo process between the dark-matter numerical particles and the gas shells. We also implement an algorithm for radiative gas cooling that is valid for weak cooling. The gas, as well as dark-matter-baryon scattering and cooling, adds only incrementally to the run time of the code, allowing systems to be evolved on a laptop computer, a tiny fraction of the computational resources required for a full 3D N-body/hydro simulation. To illustrate, we show the evolution of a $\sim 10^{13}\,M_\odot$ halo with a dark-matter-baryon scattering cross section $\sigma_{\rm tot}/m_\chi=0.1\,{\rm cm}^2\,{\rm g}^{-1}$ but no gas cooling. We then show examples with radiative gas cooling where the cooling is altered through interaction with dark-matter particles. The resulting code, NSphere-IDM, allows the implications of dark-matter interactions for galaxy evolution to be explored quickly over large volumes of the dark-matter parameter space, and other new physics (e.g., dark-matter self-interactions or anisotropy in the distribution function) is easily incorporated. The code can also provide useful calibrations and benchmarks for fully 3D N-body or hydrodynamical simulations.
#### CoRGI in MaNGA - II. NGC 5717: cold-gas accretion and disk growth in a BGG
 - **Authors:** Damir Gasymov, Ivan Yu. Katkov, Eva K. Grebel, Anna Pasquali, Evgenii V. Rubtsov, Katarina Kraljic, Igor V. Chilingarian, Kirill A. Grishin, Anna S. Saburova
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20704

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.20704

 - **Abstract**
 Large-scale stellar counter-rotation, in which two coplanar stellar components rotate in opposite directions, is usually interpreted as a signature of external gas accretion. Such systems are most often found in relatively sparse environments, where cold gas can be acquired and remain dynamically undisturbed long enough to settle into a disk. It is less clear whether this channel can operate in massive central galaxies, whose group or cluster environments can heat, strip, or perturb cold gas. Here we present NGC 5717, one of the most massive galaxies known to host a counter-rotating stellar disk and the first identified case in a brightest group galaxy. Using integral-field spectroscopy from SDSS-IV Mapping Nearby Galaxies at Apache Point Observatory (MaNGA), together with CFHT imaging, ancillary HI data, and SDSS/DESI spectroscopy of the surrounding group, we show that the galaxy contains a dynamically cold counter-rotating disk embedded in an old dynamically hot elliptical component. The disk is young ($\sim 1.3$ Gyr), metal-poor ([Fe/H] $\sim -0.7$ dex), extended ($h \sim 2.4$ kpc), and massive ($\log M_\star/M_\odot \sim 9.9$); the associated ionized gas co-rotates with it, while the available HI data indicate no significant remaining reservoir of neutral atomic gas ($\log M_{\mathrm{HI}}/M_\odot \lesssim 9$). These properties support an in situ origin of the secondary component from externally accreted gas. The chemical evolution model favors gas accretion from a cosmic filament, which is consistent with the surrounding cosmic-web geometry, and indicates highly efficient burst-like star formation with substantial metal-enriched outflows. NGC 5717 may therefore represent a rare example of disk growth in a massive central galaxy, enabled by external cold-gas accretion during an earlier phase of the group assembly history.
#### Wandering intermediate-mass black holes and off-nuclear tidal disruption events from early star clusters
 - **Authors:** Claire E. Williams, Smadar Naoz, Yihao Zhou, Tatsuya Akiba-Lewis, Mark Vogelsberger, Blakesley Burkhart, Naoki Yoshida, Avi Chen, Kyle Kremer, William Lake, Federico Marinacci, Shyam H. Menon

 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20719

 - **Abstract**
 Off-nuclear tidal disruption events (TDEs) reveal evidence for massive black holes outside galactic centers, but the origins of this wandering population remain uncertain. We investigate wandering intermediate-mass black holes (IMBHs) formed through runaway stellar collisions in dense, high-redshift star clusters and predict their TDE signatures across cosmic time. Our semi-analytic model samples clusters associated with low-mass subhalos at $7<z<20$ and uses merger trees from the IllustrisTNG TNG50 simulation to follow the assembly of 15 elliptical galaxies with stellar masses of $10^9-10^{11}~M_\odot$. We compare two models for high-redshift cluster formation: a maximal model taken from a high-resolution simulation and a conservative model calibrated from local cluster relations. Off-nuclear TDEs produced through this mechanism occur in bursts within 10-100 Myr of IMBH formation as the loss-cone surrounding the black hole is depleted. We estimate the rate of off-nuclear TDEs expected across time per galaxy, and characterize these events as typically occurring between $10-10^3$ kpc from their host galaxy centers. Event rates increase with galaxy mass; in the maximal model, the progenitors of the most massive galaxies reach peak off-nuclear TDE rates of approximately $5\times10^{-4}-4\times10^{-2}~\mathrm{yr}^{-1}$ per galaxy at $7\lesssim z\lesssim13$. This work provides a framework for constraining the early population of star clusters that form IMBHs through runaway collapse via their contribution to the rate of off-nuclear TDEs in the high redshift Universe.

 Supermassive black holes (BHs) are thought to reside in all massive galaxies. They play a fundamental role in shaping galaxy evolution by suppressing star formation and halting the stellar growth of the most massive galaxies via feedback mechanisms. However, to date, measuring even their most basic property, their mass, is still challenging. The mass of BHs carries information on the growth history and the mutual interplay with the host galaxy. Uncertain BH masses leave many key questions on galaxy evolution wide open. Here we show that a transformational facility such as ALMA2040 would open a new window to measure BH mass by directly observing the kinematics of cold gas in galaxy cores from the local Universe up to very high redshift, irrespective of BH activity. This would revolutionise our understanding of BH formation, growth, and their role within galaxy evolution.
#### Thermal Sunyaev-Zel'dovich Effect in the circumgalactic medium - III: does richness matter?
 - **Authors:** Sanskriti Das, Yi-Kuan Chiang, Smita Mathur
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA); Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21628

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.21628

 - **Abstract**
 We measure the thermal Sunyaev-Zel'dovich Effect in the circumgalactic/intragroup medium (CGM/IGrM) of 7 million $\rm M_{200c} = 10^{12-14}$M$_\odot$ $z \leqslant 1$ DESI Legacy Survey galaxies/groups using ACT+Planck observations, and investigate for the first time how the thermal pressure depends on richness. We detect the one-halo term down to 10$^{12.2}$M$_\odot$ at $5.8\sigma$. While several mass-richness bins are well described by the standard GNFW thermal-pressure profile ($\beta=5.49,r_o=0$), others favor a flatter and/or miscentered profile ($\beta=1.3-4.0,r_o>0.1 \rm R_{200c}$), with the mass dependence of this pattern varying with richness. The wide variety of thermal pressure profiles across mass and richness indicates a range of physical regimes, from dominant thermal feedback to efficient cooling with subdominant thermal feedback, and even substantial cooling. In some mass bins, the super-virial ($\approx2-7 \rm T_{200c}$) temperature floor (the lowest temperature allowed by the data for baryon-sufficient halos) points to significant heating. However, dominant kinetic-mode feedback is not essential anywhere, challenging a universal (ejective)feedback-vs-gravity picture. The slope of the thermal energy-mass relation in IGrM is consistent with self-similarity, but in the CGM it falls below self-similarity by $7.3\sigma$. This makes halos of relatively low mass and richness the dominant contributors to the universe's thermal energy budget at $z\leqslant1$. We therefore argue that calibrations of thermal energy scaling relations must move beyond the convention of using mass as the sole demarcator and instead incorporate additional intrinsic and environmental properties of galaxies.

 - **Abstract**
 Active Galactic Nuclei (AGN) play a key role in galaxy evolution by regulating star formation within their host galaxies. It is therefore crucial to understand how the central supermassive black holes (SMBH) are fueled by material from the galactic disk, and how AGN feedback impacts the surrounding gas. Most information so far comes from very nearby active galaxies (z < 0.008; D < 35 Mpc), providing the best spatial resolution and revealing randomly oriented dusty molecular tori that fuel SMBHs. A facility such as ALMA2040, offering significantly higher sensitivity and better angular resolution than current ALMA, would enable progress in two fundamental areas that remain beyond the capabilities of all existing and planned observatories: (i) probing the dynamics of the material feeding the AGN down to the accretion disk in nearby galaxies; and (ii) spatially resolving the tori and circum-nuclear regions of more energetic and cosmologically significant AGN, such as local quasars (z ~ 0.1 and Lbol > 10^45 erg/s).
#### ALMA2040: The High-Redshift Circumgalactic Medium. Imaging the fueling and feedback of galaxies in the early universe

 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21776

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.21776

 - **Abstract**
 Galaxies do not form in isolation, but rather as part of a wider interconnected web mediated at their interface by an extended gaseous atmosphere - the Circum-Galactic Medium (CGM). This dynamic and multiphase region is fed and shaped by gas inflows and mergers from the intergalactic medium and by feedback-driven outflows and radiation from the central galaxy. The CGM is imprinted by the past, present and future evolution of its central galaxy and environment. The cool atomic and molecular phases are of particular interest as they dominate the mass budget of outflows and provide a reservoir of direct star forming fuel for the galaxy to accrete. In the early universe where accretion rates, mergers, and feedback processes peak, the cycle of gas through the CGM is most vigorous. Sub-mm observations have detected cold gas in extended halos and gas flows around high redshift galaxies but are thus far limited to the most massive, extreme or gravitationally lensed sources, using just a handful of the strongest emission and absorption lines. A significant increase ($5-10\times$) in spectral line sensitivity compared with the current ALMA is needed to push physical scales, densities and properties to a new regime. We present three key scientific cases for an ALMA2040 telescope: (i) direct imaging of extended [CII]-traced CGM halos around Milky-Way-progenitor galaxies at $z=4$-8; (ii) resolved detection of cold, clumpy outflows on $\sim$100 pc scales tied to individual star-forming clumps; and (iii) multi-species/-transition emission and absorption diagnostics that jointly constrain the density, chemistry, and radiation fields permeating the cool CGM and its multiphase gas flows.
#### The gravitational constant and the cosmic microwave background
 - **Authors:** Brahim Lamine, Sébastien Beauchamp, Alain Blanchard, Jehanne Delhomelle, Raphaël Kou, James Rich
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22128

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.22128

 - **Abstract**
 The CMB constrains the gravitational constant $G$ at recombination to a few percent of its laboratory value, but the effect of $G$ on recombination itself is usually absorbed into a global fit rather than made explicit. We return to the semi-analytic description of the acoustic spectrum to ask how the acoustic scale $\ell_A$, the equality scale $\ell_{\rm eq}$ and the damping scale $\ell_{\rm damp}$ respond to a variation of $G$ during recombination, and where $G$ enters the residual ionization of the dark ages. We use a modified version of CLASS in which $G$ is varied through $\lambda_G \equiv \sqrt{G/G_{\rm N}}$, scanning $\lambda_G \in [0.25,2]$ at fixed physical densities and CMB temperature, with helium recomputed for each model. Analytic rate arguments are confronted with the numerical ionization histories, the visibility function, the Hu & White damping integral and the TT spectra. While $\ell_{\rm eq}$ and $\ell_A$ are nearly independent of $G$, non-equilibrium recombination gives $\ell_{\rm damp} \propto \lambda_G^{-0.304}$. The dependence exists only above a critical value of $G$, set by fundamental and cosmological parameters, a factor of 49 below $G_{\rm N}$. The dark-ages optical depth varies by only 16% for $G$ multiplied by 64, a negligible channel. Because $G$ enters only through the expansion rate, the imprint comes entirely from out-of-equilibrium recombination, and the competition between the atomic rates and the expansion is governed by one dimensionless combination, $\alpha_G \equiv G m_e \mu/\hbar c$, with $\mu$ the mean mass of matter per photon. Each scale is the distance to last scattering divided by a length imprinted before it, so a late-time change of gravity rescales all three alike. The ratio $\ell_{\rm damp}/\ell_A$ therefore isolates $G$ at recombination and, up to lensing and screening, compares it directly with the laboratory value.
#### ALMA2040: Mapping the Spatially Resolved Dust and Star Formation Physics Driving High-z Galaxy Assembly
 - **Authors:** Daizhong Liu, Gergö Popping, Pierre Cox, Andreas Faisst, Mamta Pandey-Pommier, Dieu Nguyen, Román Fernandez Aranda, Elisabete da Cunha, Andrea Lapi, Tanio Díaz Santos, Ryota Ikeda, Jacqueline Hodge, Isabella Lamperti, Manuel Aravena, Irene Shivaei, Adarsh Kumar, Roberto Decarli, Mahmoud Hamed, Umberto Maio, Gareth Jones, Jianhang Chen, Sylvia Adscheid

 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22228

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.22228

 - **Abstract**
 Interstellar dust reprocesses approximately half of the starlight emitted across cosmic history. However, dust temperature, which is essential to convert continuum flux into dust mass, molecular gas mass, and obscured star-formation rates, remains largely unconstrained on sub-galactic scales at high redshift. While single-band ALMA imaging has characterized continuum sizes for hundreds of z>1 galaxies, single-frequency measurements cannot separate the dust mass column from the local dust heating. To date, spatially resolved (<0.5-arcsec) multi-band dust temperatures exist in the literature for only ~10 extreme systems (quasars, hyperluminous starbursts, or strongly lensed galaxies) at z>1, with ongoing programs within a few years adding at most a few dozen submillimeter or UV-bright galaxies. This still leaves typical main-sequence disks largely unexplored across cosmic history. With an anticipated ~30 times improvement in continuum sensitivity (compared to the current ALMA), simultaneous multi-band receivers, and (sub-)kpc angular resolution, ALMA2040 will transform resolved dust thermometry into a systematic survey science. Here, we present the scientific rationale and technical requirements, highlighting a future statistically meaningful survey with > 100 representative main-sequence galaxies across cosmic epochs. Realistic visibility simulations demonstrate that ALMA2040 measures radial temperature gradients with ~0.5-4 K precision (L_IR~10^{12} L_sun in 2 h on-source at z~2). These measurements will break the degeneracy between intrinsic compactness and temperature gradients, deliver the first extinction-free, temperature-calibrated resolved star-formation relations, and uniquely trace radiative feedback and disk maturation from cosmic noon to cosmic dawn.

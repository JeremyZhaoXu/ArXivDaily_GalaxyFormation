#### A GLIMPSE of Dark Matter: JWST Constraints on Early Structure Formation
 - **Authors:** Julian B. Muñoz, John Chisholm, Seiji Fujimoto, Tiger Yu-Yang Hsiao, Kristen B. W. McQuinn, Priyamvada Natarajan, A. Saldana-Lopez, Hakim Atek, Arghyadeep Basu, Danielle Berg, Ryan Endsley, Lukas J. Furtak, Damien Korber, Rohan P. Naidu, Richard Pan, Daniel Schaerer
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:

 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37751

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.37751

 - **Abstract**
 While we have a solid understanding of how dark matter (DM) behaves during recombination ($z\sim 10^3$) and in the late universe ($z\lesssim$ a few), much less is known about intermediate times, when the first galaxies formed. These galaxies provide a powerful handle on DM through structure formation, as halos have to exist to host luminous sources. Here we use recent determinations of the UV luminosity function from the James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) GLIMPSE lensing program, along with legacy Hubble and Subaru data, to test the small-scale behavior of DM during the epoch of reionization ($z\sim 7$). Marginalizing over astrophysical uncertainties, we find the matter power spectrum to be consistent with the cold-DM prediction down to sub-Mpc scales, with 1$\sigma$ uncertainties of 0.2 dex at $k=3-9 \, \rm Mpc^{-1}$ and 0.5 dex at $k=9-27 \, \rm Mpc^{-1}$, the smallest scales probed at these early times. For thermal-relic warm DM we constrain $m_{\rm WDM} > 3.2\, \rm keV$ (95% CL), independent of lower-$z$ probes. Our results test small-scale structure formation in a new redshift window and demonstrate the promise of JWST to constrain the nature of DM.

 - **Abstract**
 Recent observations from the from Spectrum Roentgen Gamma (SRG)/eROSITA all-sky survey data have helped to illuminate the hot circumgalactic medium (CGM) around galaxies at Milky Way-masses and above. In our study, we explore the hot X-ray emitting (T \textgreater 10$^6$ K) component of the CGM in over 750 galaxies from the Romulus25 cosmological simulation (Tremmel et al. 2017) between 10.0 < log(M$_*/M_{\odot}$) < 11.5. We forward-model soft X-ray [0.5 - 2.0] keV observations of our simulated galaxies using pyXSIM and SIXTE to measure the X-ray surface brightness (SB) profiles of the hot CGM. We then compare the average X-ray surface brightness profiles of six stellar-mass selected sub-samples to compare to the observational results from Chadayammuri et al. (2022a) and Zhang et al. 2024a. Broadly, we find that our stacked SB profiles are too bright compared to observations from Chadayammuri et al. (2022a) and Zhang et al.(2024a) particularly in the inner CGM. Additionally, we calculate the X-ray luminosity (L$_{\chi}$) of the hot CGM for each galaxy and compare the average L$_{\chi}$ across stellar mass to observations from Zhang et al. (2024b) as well as measurements from the EAGLE, TNG, SIMBA simulations. Across stellar mass, the average L$_{\chi}$ of galaxies from Romulus25 are brighter than observations except at the lower, Milky Way-mass end where we are in relative agreement with both observations and most other simulations. We also consider a zero-metallicity emission model, because Romulus25 does not have metal-line cooling, finding the average L$_{\chi}$ decreases by nearly an order-of-magnitude, which better matches observations particularly in the Milky Way-mass regime.
#### Resolving the thermal component of stellar feedback with the "anchor-and-refine" scheme in galactic simulations
 - **Authors:** Chengzhe Li, Hui Li, Wei Cui, Federico Marinacci, Laura Sales, Mark Vogelsberger, Paul Torrey
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37767

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.37767

 - **Abstract**
 (abridged) Stellar feedback regulates galactic baryon cycles, but galaxy-scale simulations often cannot resolve supernova cooling radii. Momentum-based subgrid models can consequently underresolve the hot gas around young stars. We develop an "anchor-and-refine" feedback scheme that anchors a host cell near each star particle, preventing its displacement and preserving a thermal-feedback reservoir. When the cooling radius is resolved, all supernova energy is injected into the host cell as thermal energy; otherwise, the host cell is refined and terminal momentum is deposited into surrounding gas cells. Idealized tests agree with high-resolution simulations, yielding consistent superbubble properties and preserving hot interiors without artificial cooling. In an isolated Milky Way-mass galaxy, the scheme captures finer X-ray-emitting structures and a sharply defined, X-ray-bright central mid-plane. It also produces a smoother star formation history than pure hybrid feedback. Hot gas dominates outflow mass loading, with the mass-loading factor plateauing at approximately 0.2 at heights z>1 kpc. Phase decomposition indicates that hot gas sustains a steady, energy-driven wind, whereas cooler outflows decline with height because of radiative cooling and gravitational fallback. These results highlight the role of thermal feedback in driving galactic winds. By preserving hot superbubble interiors and the partition of thermal and kinetic energy, anchor-and-refine improves the treatment of hot gas, star formation, and feedback at limited resolution, supporting future applications to galaxy-scale and cosmological zoom-in simulations.

 - **Abstract**
 Characterizing the hot (T ~ 10$^{6}$ K) circumgalactic medium (CGM) around Milky Way-mass (MW-mass) galaxies is critical to a full understanding of galaxy evolution. In this second paper of the "X-rays in Romulus25" series, we unpack the specific physical processes giving rise to the presence of hot X-ray emitting gas in the halos of galaxies from the Romulus25 (R25) simulation, focusing on L$_{*}$ galaxies. We determine that a strong correlation appears between the mass of hot CGM gas, M$_{gas, T > 10^{5.5} K}$, and the X-ray emissivity, $\epsilon_{\chi}$, measured in the first paper of this series, (Sanchez et al. 2026a, submitted). There is also a secondary correlation between M$_{gas, T > 10^{5.5} K}$ and $\epsilon_{\chi}$ with the average metallicity of the hot CGM gas, $\bar{Z}_{gas, T > 10^{5.5} K}$. We use the well-studied PIONEER simulations from Sanchez et al. (2019), a set of zoom-in MW-mass galaxies run with the genetic modification technique of Roth et al. (2016) and the same physics as R25, to interpret these correlations. We determine that the presence of hot X-ray emitting gas is determined by two key processes: 1) the quantity of hot gas mass in the CGM, M$_{gas, T > 10^{5.5} K}$, is set by the combined total energy injection of $both$ stellar and AGN feedback, and 2) together the average temperature and metallicity of the hot CGM changes the soft X-ray spectrum of the gas resulting in brighter metal-rich CGM and dimmer metal-poor CGM for the same quantity of M$_{gas, T > 10^{5.5} K}$. These results indicate, for the first time, that metal-rich gas halos may bias X-ray observations of MW-mass galaxies.

#### Probing Cosmology with Diffusion Models: Memorization, Generalization, and Parameter Estimation
 - **Authors:** Jiaming Pan, Nicholas Kern

 - **Abstract**
 Diffusion models are increasingly used as surrogates for expensive cosmological simulations, but simple metrics like reconstruction fidelity do not guarantee a surrogate can be trusted for scientific inference. To explore this, we train diffusion models on HI fields from the CAMELS IllustrisTNG simulations and test how three properties change with training-set size: memorization of individual training examples, agreement with one- and two-point statistics (the pixel-value distribution and the power spectrum), and estimation of cosmological parameters from the generated fields.  - **Abstract**
 We study the gravothermal collapse of inelastic dark matter halos with velocity-dependent elastic and dissipative self-interactions. Within the gravothermal fluid framework, we derive the effective cooling rate for dark matter upscattering to an excited state followed by decay back to the ground state, with emitted states carrying off energy from the halo. #### Cosmic rays in galaxy halos I: the circumgalactic transport rate and a dynamically significant cosmic-ray reservoir
 - **Authors:** Roland M. Crocker, Mark R. Krumholz
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE); Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO); Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38061

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.38061

 - **Abstract**
 Fermi-LAT observations imply that most of the cosmic rays (CRs) produced in normal star-forming galaxies escape from the discs where they are accelerated into the circumgalactic medium (CGM), but the subsequent evolution of those escaping CRs is unclear. Here we construct a simple semi-analytic model for their transport and loss, which we constrain by confronting the model with a series of observations: limits on CR energy densities in the outer Milky Way from Fermi-LAT, the isotropic gamma-ray background (IGRB), stacked CGM X-ray emission from eROSITA, and the requirement that the accumulated pressure of CRs in galaxy CGMs not be so large as to render them unstable. We show that these constraints can be jointly satisfied in only two scenarios: one where CRs diffuse quickly through the CGM and freely escape beyond it into the IGM, and another where the CR diffusion time out of halos is of order the Hubble time. In the latter scenario CRs accelerated by star formation naturally explain both the IGRB and the heat input required to maintain the temperature of the CGM against X-ray cooling, and their relatively slow transport means that roughly half of the CR energy injected into the CGM of an $L_\star$ halo over its star-formation history remains there at $z \simeq 0$. This makes CR pressure dynamically significant, and also means that a non-negligible part of the IGRB is produced by CRs in the Milky Way's own halo. We argue that this latter scenario is the more likely one, as it avoids the need to posit a second source for the IGRB that is coincidentally very close in both power and spectral shape to the contribution from star-forming discs.

 - **Authors:** Danat Issa, Ore Gottlieb, Jonatan Jacquemin-Ide, Matthew Liska
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:

 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38673

 - **Abstract**
 Black hole (BH) spin grows as accretion supplies angular momentum and decreases as magnetically driven jets remove it, driving the BH toward an equilibrium spin, $a_{\rm \star; eq}$. Existing estimates of $a_{\rm \star; eq}$ rely on general-relativistic magnetohydrodynamic (GRMHD) simulations with fixed BH mass and spin, and cover only the extreme limits of horizon magnetic flux $\phi\rightarrow0$ and magnetically arrested disks (MADs), $\phi\approx\phi_{\rm MAD}$. We evolve the BH mass and spin in long 3D GRMHD simulations of radiatively inefficient accretion flows aligned or anti-aligned with the BH spin. We find the spin-up parameter as a function of BH spin and $\phi/\phi_{\rm MAD}$, and derive the equilibrium spin as a continuous function of flux, $a_{\rm \star; eq}(\phi/\phi_{\rm MAD})$. The equilibrium spin falls steeply with flux, from $a_{\rm \star; eq}\approx0.9$ in weakly magnetized flows through $a_{\rm \star; eq}(\phi/\phi_{\rm MAD}=0.5)\approx0.5$ to $a_{\rm \star; eq}(\phi/\phi_{\rm MAD}=1)\approx0.06$ in sustained MADs. In weakly magnetized flows, a slowly spinning BH must accrete at least its initial mass to reach equilibrium, whereas MADs spin down rapidly rotating BHs after accreting less than half of it. However, as the BH grows, the growing gravitational radius and enhanced inflow reduce $\phi/\phi_{\rm MAD}$, driving disks whose magnetic-flux supply cannot keep pace out of the MAD state and allowing the BH to transition from spin-down to spin-up. The final spin thus depends on both the magnetic flux history and the accreted mass. We discuss implications for a variety of BH-powered engines and provide a subgrid prescription for galaxy-scale simulations and population studies.
